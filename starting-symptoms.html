Separate no-crank from crank-no-start first.

The first split changes the entire diagnosis. If the engine does not turn, prove the high-current and control paths. If it cranks normally, stop blaming the starter system and move toward engine start requirements.

Test the circuit while it is working

Battery supply → positive path → starter load → ground return → control command.

Static voltage alone does not prove a battery or cable can support cranking current. A starter click alone does not prove the starter is bad. Loaded voltage and voltage-drop evidence show where electrical energy is being lost while the circuit is under demand.

Dash goes very dim or resets

That behavior is evidence of a supply problem, excessive voltage loss or unusually high demand. Capture battery and circuit behavior during the actual crank attempt before disconnecting or replacing components.

Dash stays normal but nothing happens

Follow the start command. Determine whether the request reaches the appropriate relay/control path and whether required interlocks are satisfied. Do not bypass neutral, clutch or other safety logic as a routine diagnostic shortcut.

Cranks normally but will not start?

That is a different diagnostic branch. Once adequate cranking is established, investigate the engine's requirements to start: controller authorization, fuel delivery, air, engine speed/position information and other engine-specific conditions. Preserve active fault information before clearing it.

Evidence worth capturing

  • Exactly what happens at the key/start command: silence, single click, repeated click, slow rotation or normal rotation.
  • Dash/gauge/module behavior during the crank attempt.
  • Battery and cable behavior under actual starter load using the appropriate test method.
  • Whether the starter control terminal receives the expected command when the complaint occurs.
  • Whether the problem changes with temperature, vibration, recent repair, battery service or repeated attempts.
  • Related voltage, communication or engine faults before clearing them.

Exact voltage-drop limits, battery test criteria, starter current values and control-circuit procedures vary by vehicle. Verify them with current service information.

Starting-system safety

Secure the vehicle before starting-system tests. Starting circuits carry very high current and can create severe heat, sparks and fire. Keep clear of rotating components, follow approved battery/starter procedures and do not bypass neutral, clutch or other safety interlocks as a routine shortcut.
