The cooling-system diagnostic order

Coolant quantity → external loss → airflow → circulation → pressure behavior → internal leak evidence.

This order prevents a common mistake: jumping directly from “overheating” or “pressure” to a head gasket, EGR cooler, thermostat, water pump or fan clutch without first proving which part of the cooling system is failing.

Overheats mostly under load

Record vehicle load, road speed, ambient conditions, fan behavior and whether temperature recovers when load is reduced. A complaint that appears only under sustained load provides different evidence than a truck that overheats at idle.

Overheats mostly at idle or low speed

Pay close attention to commanded/actual fan operation, airflow restriction and heat rejection. Do not condemn the fan solely because the engine is hot—prove whether the system is requesting fan operation and whether airflow is actually inadequate.

Pressure does not identify the source by itself

A cooling system is designed to operate under controlled pressure. The useful diagnostic question is whether pressure behavior is appropriate for temperature and operating conditions. Rapid abnormal pressure rise, repeated coolant displacement or gas entering the cooling system requires testing to identify the source; it does not by itself prove a specific gasket, cooler or engine component has failed.

Evidence worth recording

  • Coolant level and condition when the system is safely cold.
  • Where and when coolant is lost: parked, idling, under load, after shutdown or only at operating temperature.
  • Temperature behavior compared with engine load and fan operation.
  • Visible radiator/CAC/debris restriction and evidence of external leakage.
  • Whether coolant contamination appears in oil or oil contamination appears in coolant.
  • Related fault codes, derates and sensor plausibility before clearing them.

Exact temperature limits, pressure-test values, coolant specifications and component procedures vary by engine and vehicle. Verify them with current service information for the exact configuration.

Cooling-system safety

Never remove a pressure cap from a hot cooling system. Steam and hot coolant can cause severe burns. Stop operation for severe overheating, rapid coolant loss, steam, contaminated oil, or any condition where continued operation could damage the engine or create an unsafe roadside situation.
