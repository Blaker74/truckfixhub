The cooling-system diagnostic order

Coolant quantity → external loss → airflow → circulation → pressure behavior → internal leak evidence.

Do not jump directly from “overheating” or “pressure” to a head gasket, EGR cooler, thermostat, water pump or fan clutch. Prove which part of the cooling system is failing.

Hot mostly under load

Record vehicle load, road speed, ambient conditions, fan behavior and whether temperature recovers when load is reduced.

Hot mostly at idle / low speed

Pay close attention to commanded versus actual fan operation, airflow restriction and heat rejection. Prove whether the system is requesting fan operation and whether airflow is inadequate.

Pressure does not identify the source by itself

The system normally operates under controlled pressure. Rapid abnormal pressure rise, repeated coolant displacement or gas entering the system requires testing; it does not by itself prove a particular gasket, cooler or engine component failed.

Evidence worth recording

  • Coolant level and condition when safely cold.
  • Where and when coolant is lost.
  • Temperature behavior compared with engine load and fan operation.
  • Radiator/CAC/debris restriction and external leakage.
  • Coolant in oil or oil in coolant.
  • Related fault codes and sensor plausibility before clearing codes.

Exact temperature limits, pressure-test values, coolant specifications and component procedures vary by engine and vehicle. Verify current service information for the exact configuration.

Cooling-system safety

Never remove a pressure cap from a hot cooling system. Steam and hot coolant can cause severe burns. Stop operation for severe overheating, rapid coolant loss, steam, contaminated oil or any unsafe condition.
