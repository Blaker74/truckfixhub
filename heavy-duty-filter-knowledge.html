DETROIT

Detroit Genuine Filtration

Detroit engines found especially in Freightliner and Western Star use engine-generation-specific lube and fuel filtration. Current DD13/DD15/DD16 applications use cartridge/filter-system arrangements that must be matched to engine serial/VIN and model year.

Simple rule: “DD15 filter” is not enough. Determine engine generation/year and whether you need lube, engine-mounted fuel, chassis fuel/water separator, coolant or another service filter.

Aftermarket manufacturers publish Detroit coverage too; for example Fleetguard provides installation information for its LF17810 on 2020+ DD13/DD15/DD16 applications, and Baldwin publishes Freightliner Cascadia DD13/DD15 application listings.

PACCAR

PACCAR MX Filtration

PACCAR MX-11/MX-13 engines are common in Peterbilt and Kenworth. Filter configuration changes across emissions/model-year generations, including primary fuel/water separation, engine-mounted secondary fuel filtration and lube filtration.

Simple rule: identify MX-11 vs MX-13, model year/emissions generation and truck configuration before crossing filters.

Donaldson's current PACCAR coverage separates EPA13/EPA17 from EPA21 products, while Fleetguard and Baldwin catalogs show multiple MX fuel/water-separator and fuel-filter configurations. That is why appearance alone is unsafe for a cross-reference.

DONALDSON

Donaldson

Donaldson supplies heavy-duty engine air, lube, fuel, coolant and related filtration. Donaldson standard lube filters are designed to meet or exceed OEM performance requirements, with Donaldson Blue options for selected extended-service/severe applications.

Donaldson publishes specific PACCAR MX-11/MX-13 liquid-filter and on-road air-filter coverage, including model-year/emissions distinctions.

Parts-counter rule: use Donaldson's application/cross-reference data to confirm the exact OE number. A physically similar filter is not automatically equivalent.

FLEETGUARD

Fleetguard

Fleetguard offers lube, fuel, fuel/water separator, air, coolant and crankcase filtration across heavy-duty applications. Its lube technologies include full-flow, bypass/combo designs and NanoNet/StrataPore media on selected products.

Fleetguard's online catalog ties individual filters to equipment, engine and year. It includes PACCAR MX applications and Detroit DD13/DD15/DD16 aftermarket coverage as well as Cummins-powered Freightliner, Peterbilt, Kenworth and International trucks.

Important: Fleetguard coolant filters may contain specific supplemental coolant additives or may be blank/non-chemical depending on product. Never substitute one simply because the thread fits.

BALDWIN

Baldwin

Baldwin publishes on-highway application guides covering Freightliner Cascadia/Detroit, Kenworth, Peterbilt/PACCAR MX, Volvo VNL/D13 and other engine families. Its catalog spans lube, fuel, fuel/water separation, air, coolant, crankcase, DEF and cab filtration.

Baldwin's heavy-duty engine guide also publishes cross-reference tables between OE, Baldwin and Fleetguard products for many engine families.

Simple rule: a cross-reference is a starting point. Confirm the application, year and exact function before sale or installation.

TRUCK MAKES

Freightliner · Peterbilt · Kenworth · International · Western Star · Mack · Volvo

These truck makes can carry different engines and filtration systems across model years. Freightliner/Western Star may use Detroit or Cummins; Peterbilt/Kenworth may use PACCAR or Cummins; International has used multiple International/Cummins engine families; Mack/Volvo commonly use their own engine families but application history varies.

TruckFixHub rule: TRUCK → VIN → ENGINE → YEAR → FILTER FUNCTION → OE NUMBER → VERIFIED CROSS.

🛢️

Block 1 — Lube / Oil Filter

The lube filter removes contamination from engine oil while maintaining required flow. Designs include spin-on, cartridge, full-flow, bypass and combination filtration.

Do not choose by thread/gasket dimensions alone. Bypass-valve behavior, media, efficiency, flow and housing design are part of the application.

⛽

Block 2 — Fuel Filter

Modern common-rail fuel systems demand clean fuel. Trucks may use multiple stages: chassis-mounted primary filtration/fuel-water separation plus engine-mounted secondary/final filtration.

“Fuel filter” may mean two different filters on the same truck. Identify location and function before ordering.

💧

Block 3 — Fuel / Water Separator

A fuel/water separator removes water and particles before they reach sensitive high-pressure fuel components. DAVCO-style housings are common on many North American trucks, with element selection varying by engine and housing/application.

Do not select an element solely from the clear housing's appearance. Confirm housing, engine, year and OE/cross number.

🌬️

Block 4 — Engine Air Filter

The engine air filter protects the turbocharger and engine from airborne contamination. Restriction rises as the filter loads.

  • Inspect housing and ducting for leaks.
  • Check restriction using the vehicle/filter-system method.
  • Never run an engine with unfiltered intake air.
  • Do not replace an air filter merely because it looks dusty if the manufacturer uses restriction-based service guidance.
🫁

Block 5 — Crankcase Ventilation Filter

Closed-crankcase systems can use replaceable coalescing/breather elements. A restricted or incorrect element can contribute to crankcase-pressure complaints.

Confirm whether the engine has a serviceable element and its required interval from the engine/OEM information.

🌡️

Block 6 — Coolant Filter

Coolant filters are not all chemically equivalent. Some contain supplemental coolant additive; others are blank filters. Fleetguard, for example, catalogs DCA chemical water filters for specific coolant strategies.

Never replace a blank coolant filter with an additive-charged filter—or the reverse—without confirming the coolant program.

💦

Block 7 — DEF Filter

Many SCR/DEF dosing systems contain a serviceable DEF filter. This is separate from fuel filtration and must match the dosing-system application.

Keep dirt out of DEF components. Use the exact OEM service procedure because contamination can damage dosing hardware.

🧑‍🔧

Block 8 — Cab / HVAC Filter

Cab fresh-air and recirculation filters protect HVAC airflow and occupants. Some trucks use more than one cab element. Baldwin's on-highway listings, for example, show separate fresh-air and recirculation elements on certain Freightliner applications.

Weak cab airflow is a reason to inspect the correct cab filters—but do not confuse them with the engine air filter.

🔎

Block 9 — How to Cross a Filter Correctly

  1. Get VIN and engine serial if available.
  2. Identify engine make/model and year.
  3. Identify filter function and location.
  4. Read the existing OE/filter number.
  5. Use the current manufacturer's catalog.
  6. Confirm application notes, dimensions and special features.
  7. Confirm supersessions before ordering.

Cross the number AND verify the application.

🚫

Block 10 — Bad Ways to Identify a Filter

  • “It is a 2020 Peterbilt.”
  • “It looks like this one.”
  • Matching thread only.
  • Matching outside dimensions only.
  • Assuming every MX-13/DD15/D13 uses one filter forever.
  • Using an old cross without checking supersession.
📅

Block 11 — Service Intervals

Do not publish one universal mileage interval for a brand of filter. Service interval depends on engine/OEM maintenance schedule, oil/fuel/coolant strategy, duty cycle, idle time, environment and sometimes filter technology.

Follow the current engine/truck maintenance schedule and any approved extended-service program.

🧼

Block 12 — Clean Installation Matters

Clean around filter housings before opening them. Prevent dirt from entering clean-side fuel, oil, intake, coolant and DEF circuits. Lubricate seals only as the filter/OEM instructions specify and confirm old gaskets/O-rings are removed.

After service, prime/bleed where required and inspect for leaks.

📦

Block 13 — Before Ordering Filters

  • VIN.
  • Engine serial/model.
  • Model year/emissions generation.
  • Filter function and location.
  • Existing OE and aftermarket numbers.
  • Housing/system brand such as DAVCO where relevant.
  • Coolant chemistry if ordering coolant filtration.
  • Any supersession/application notes.
🏷️

Block 14 — Brand vs Application

Detroit and PACCAR provide OE application identity. Donaldson, Fleetguard and Baldwin provide broad aftermarket coverage and cross-reference tools. None of those facts means every aftermarket number fits every truck carrying that engine brand.

Brand tells you where to look. Application data tells you what fits.

Truck-by-Truck Starting Point

Freightliner

Identify Detroit vs Cummins/other engine first. Cascadia DD13/DD15 filtration varies by engine generation and chassis fuel-system configuration.

Western Star

Identify Detroit/Cummins engine and chassis fuel/air system before crossing. Do not assume Freightliner filters solely because both are DTNA trucks.

Peterbilt

Identify PACCAR MX vs Cummins. MX-11/MX-13 filter configurations vary by year; current catalogs show multiple primary and secondary fuel arrangements.

Kenworth

Identify PACCAR MX vs Cummins and model/year. Air-filter and fuel-filter arrangements can change across T680/T880/W990 and engine generations.

International

Identify exact International/Cummins engine family and year before crossing. The truck badge alone spans too many engine generations.

Mack / Volvo

Identify Mack/Volvo engine family, model year and chassis fuel/air configuration. Aftermarket catalogs include D-series applications but exact OE identity remains essential.

Easy memory rule

VIN → ENGINE → YEAR → FILTER JOB → OE NUMBER → VERIFIED CROSS → INSTALL → CHECK.

That sequence prevents most filter-counter mistakes.

⚠️ Filtration Safety

Hot oil/coolant, pressurized fuel systems and DEF chemicals require the correct service procedure and PPE. Never open a high-pressure common-rail fuel system as though it were an old mechanical system. Never run the engine with intake plumbing open to unfiltered air. Exact service intervals, priming procedures, torque values and approved filter specifications must come from current OEM/filter-manufacturer information for the exact application.
