SPN 1569 FMI 31 — ENGINE DERATE

Do not treat SPN 1569 FMI 31 as automatic proof that one particular sensor or aftertreatment component has failed.

KEY POINT: SPN 1569 FMI 31 is commonly seen when the engine control system has commanded a power or speed derate. In many applications it is a consequence of another active fault or an unresolved condition—not the root cause itself.

What should you check first?

  1. Pull every active and stored fault. Do not diagnose 1569 by itself. Record the complete SPN/FMI list and which faults are active.
  2. Look for the fault that triggered the derate. Depending on engine/application, investigate active aftertreatment, DEF/SCR, DPF, NOx, engine-protection, sensor, communication or other system faults using current OEM information.
  3. Check warning messages and derate stage. Record dash messages, lamps, whether speed/power is limited, and when the complaint began.
  4. Inspect before replacing parts. Check connectors, harness damage, power/grounds, fluid level/quality where applicable, leaks and obvious physical failures relevant to the companion codes.
  5. Follow the OEM troubleshooting tree for the root fault. Repair and verify the actual triggering condition rather than attempting to clear only SPN 1569 FMI 31.
  6. Verify the repair. Confirm the triggering fault stays inactive and that the derate clears according to the manufacturer procedure.

Why does SPN 1569 FMI 31 keep coming back?

If the condition responsible for the derate remains active, clearing codes alone will not fix it. The engine controller can command the derate again. This is why the other active codes, operating conditions and OEM diagnostic procedures matter more than guessing a component from 1569 alone.

Common diagnostic mistakes

  • Replacing a NOx sensor, DEF component or DPF solely because the truck is derated.
  • Diagnosing only SPN 1569 while ignoring companion codes.
  • Clearing codes repeatedly without proving why the derate was commanded.
  • Assuming every engine manufacturer handles the code and inducement sequence identically.
⚠️ Application matters. Fault definitions, inducement logic, reset requirements and repair procedures can differ by engine, calibration and emissions system. Verify the exact engine and use current manufacturer service information before repair.
