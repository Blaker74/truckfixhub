TRUCK & TRAILER

Application Is More Than Truck Make

Freightliner, Peterbilt, Kenworth, International, Western Star, Mack and Volvo can use OEM-specific headlamp assemblies while marker, work, auxiliary and trailer lamps may use common industry formats.

Parts-counter rule: OEM headlamp? Start with VIN. Universal/industry-format lamp? Start with old lamp number, function, dimensions, mount and connector.

🛑

Block 1 — Stop / Tail / Turn

One lamp can perform three jobs: bright stop, lower-intensity tail/running light and turn signal. Grote describes STT lamps as combined stop, tail and turn functions and offers both round and oval LED families.

If one function works and another does not, do not automatically condemn the entire lamp. Prove the separate feed/control and ground first.

🟠

Block 2 — Marker / Clearance

Marker and clearance lamps show vehicle presence and dimensions. Common formats include 2-inch, 2½-inch, compact dot/flush lamps and identification bars.

Color and mounting location matter. Do not swap red/amber or relocate lamps without confirming the required vehicle-lighting function.

🔦

Block 3 — Headlights / Forward Lighting

Forward lighting includes OEM-specific headlamp assemblies plus standardized sealed-beam replacement formats and auxiliary/fog lighting. Truck-Lite currently lists 37 Series direct LED headlights in 7-inch round, 5x7 and 4x6 formats, including heated and non-heated versions.

Before upgrading from halogen to LED, verify connector, voltage, physical fit, beam/application approval and vehicle electrical compatibility.

⬅️

Block 4 — Turn / Signal Lamps

Turn lamps can be dedicated, combination STT or side-turn/marker designs. A fast flash, no flash or warning on the dash can be caused by lamp failure, wiring, flasher/load-monitoring strategy or module logic depending on the truck.

LED conversion can change circuit load. Diagnose the vehicle's actual control system before adding resistors or changing flashers.

⚪

Block 5 — Backup Lamps

Backup lamps provide rearward white light and signaling when reverse is selected. Diagnose from the command path: reverse request/switch or transmission signal → controller/relay if used → wiring → connector → lamp → ground.

🏗️

Block 6 — Work Lights

Work lamps are designed to illuminate a work area rather than serve as road-going headlamps. Grote's Trilliant family and Truck-Lite work-light offerings span different beam patterns, outputs, mounts and voltages.

More lumens is not automatically better. Match flood/spot pattern, mounting height, electrical load and environment to the job.

🚨

Block 7 — Warning / Hazard Lighting

Beacons, strobes, directional warning lamps and light bars are separate from normal marker/STT lighting. Truck-Lite lists Super 44 LED warning lights; Grote offers strobes, beacons, directional warning lamps and light bars.

Verify flash pattern, synchronization capability, voltage, mounting and applicable regulations for the intended use.

🔌

Block 8 — Connector & Pigtail

A correct-looking lamp with the wrong connector is the wrong part. Identify:

  • Hardwired vs replaceable pigtail.
  • Number and arrangement of terminals.
  • Weather-sealed connector style.
  • Wire function/polarity.
  • Harness condition.

Never force a connector or assume wire colors are universal.

⚡

Block 9 — Lamp Does Not Work

  1. Confirm the lamp should be commanded ON.
  2. Check correct power at the connector under load.
  3. Check ground with voltage-drop testing.
  4. Inspect connector pins, corrosion and harness damage.
  5. Verify the lamp on the correct voltage/source if manufacturer procedure permits.

Power at an unplugged connector is not proof the circuit can carry load.

🌫️

Block 10 — Dim / Flickering Lamp

Think resistance before parts: poor ground, corroded terminal, loose pin, damaged harness, water intrusion or unstable supply. Compare voltage drop while the lamp is operating.

LED electronics can react differently from incandescent bulbs to poor connections and diagnostic/load-monitoring circuits.

💧

Block 11 — Water / Corrosion

Inspect the lamp, connector, pigtail and harness—not only the lens. Water entering a connector can create intermittent faults even when a sealed LED lamp remains functional.

Use the manufacturer-approved connector/sealing method; random silicone and twisted wires can create future diagnostic problems.

📐

Block 12 — How to Identify a Replacement

  • Old manufacturer/part number.
  • Required function.
  • Shape and dimensions.
  • Mounting style and hole pattern.
  • Connector/pigtail.
  • Voltage.
  • Lens/light color.
  • Heated/non-heated if applicable.
  • Regulatory/application requirement.

Match function first, footprint second.

🔁

Block 13 — Cross-Referencing Truck-Lite & Grote

Use current manufacturer interchange/product-finder information and verify every functional detail. An interchange can point to a candidate replacement; it does not remove the need to check mount, connector, voltage and regulatory function.

Do not create a Truck-Lite ↔ Grote cross merely because both lamps are 4-inch round or 6-inch oval.

🧩

Block 14 — LED vs Incandescent

LED generally reduces current draw and maintenance and offers long service life, but conversion can expose compatibility issues in circuits designed to monitor incandescent loads.

Check the truck/trailer electrical strategy before assuming an LED retrofit is plug-and-play.

🚛

Block 15 — Trailer Lighting

Trailer diagnosis begins at the tractor/trailer interface and works rearward: tractor output → 7-way connection → nose/junction box → main harness → branch harness → lamp connector → lamp/ground.

Grote's heavy-duty trailer portfolio includes lighting plus harness/power-delivery components, which reinforces the point: many “bad lamp” complaints are actually connection/harness faults.

📦

Block 16 — Before Ordering

  • Truck/trailer VIN when OEM-specific.
  • Old lamp number and manufacturer.
  • Function: STT, marker, turn, backup, headlight, work, warning.
  • Shape/size.
  • Mount.
  • Connector/pigtail.
  • Voltage.
  • Color.
  • Heated/non-heated.
  • Any required compliance/application markings.

Never cross lighting by appearance alone.

Quick Problem Finder

One lamp completely dead

Prove command, loaded power, ground and connector before replacing the lamp.

Tail works, brake/turn does not

Combination lamps have separate functional circuits. Trace the missing stop/turn feed or control.

LED flickers

Check terminal tension, corrosion, grounds, unstable voltage and vehicle load-monitoring strategy.

Several trailer lamps dead

Move upstream toward shared feed, 7-way, junction box, main harness or common ground.

New lamp does not fit

Recheck mount, footprint, connector and lamp family. Same diameter/shape does not guarantee interchange.

New LED causes warning/fast flash

Verify the truck's lamp-monitoring/flasher strategy and use an approved compatible solution rather than guessing with resistance.

Easy memory rule

FUNCTION → POWER → GROUND → CONNECTOR → LAMP → MOUNT → COMPLIANCE → VERIFY.

That sequence solves lighting complaints without turning every problem into a lamp replacement.

⚠️ Lighting Safety

Exterior road lighting is safety-critical. Preserve required lamp function, color, location, visibility and applicable FMVSS/SAE compliance. Disconnect/isolate electrical power as required before harness repair. Use sealed automotive connectors and proper wire repair methods. Exact OEM headlamp wiring, module diagnostics, circuit protection and retrofit requirements must come from current information for the exact truck.
