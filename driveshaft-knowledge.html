Meritor & Dana Spicer Drivelines Made Simple

A driveshaft transfers torque, but diagnosing one means separating U-joint, yoke, slip-spline, center-bearing, phasing, angle, balance and mounting problems. Identify the series first—then service that exact design.

MERITOR / CUMMINS

Meritor Drivelines

Major families: RPL Series Permalube plus full-round, Easy Service, wing-style and 155R/conventional designs. Current RPL quick-reference literature includes RPL20 interaxle, RPL25 main and RPL35 heavy-duty applications.

Big distinction: Meritor RPL Permalube U-joints/slip components are sealed designs that do not receive routine grease. Conventional greaseable Meritor designs have different lubrication procedures.

RPL identification matters: RPL20, RPL25 and RPL35 hardware and procedures are not interchangeable just because the shaft looks similar.

DANA SPICER

Dana Spicer Drivelines

Major heavy-duty families: traditional Spicer 10 Series and Spicer Life Series (SPL). SPL service literature covers multiple SPL series and both greaseable and service-free configurations.

Big distinction: a greaseable Spicer U-joint must be lubricated correctly through all bearings. A service-free joint is handled according to its specific assembly instructions and is not simply treated like a conventional greaseable joint.

Dana publishes separate driveshaft service manuals, SPL manuals, lubrication/torque references, application guidelines, installation guidelines and failure-analysis material.

Identify the Driveline First

Before ordering anything, determine:

  • Manufacturer: Meritor or Dana Spicer.
  • Driveshaft/U-joint series.
  • Full-round, half-round/strap, wing/flange or other attachment style.
  • Greaseable or sealed/service-free configuration.
  • Main driveshaft, interaxle shaft or multi-piece shaft.
  • Yoke style, spline information and measurable dimensions.

Rule: never identify a U-joint by cap diameter alone.

✚

U-Joint Inspection

  1. Secure the vehicle and restrain the driveshaft before service.
  2. Check each joint for looseness/play.
  3. Rotate/articulate it and feel for binding or roughness.
  4. Look for rust-colored dust, failed seals, heat or displaced bearing cups.
  5. Inspect retaining hardware and yoke seating.
  6. Check the mating yokes for wear or damage before installing a new joint.

A new U-joint in a damaged yoke does not repair the driveline.

🧴

Greaseable U-Joints

Spicer: Dana states inadequate lubrication is a leading cause of premature U-joint and slip-assembly failure. For applicable greaseable joints, fresh grease should reach/purge at every bearing seal according to the service procedure.

Meritor conventional designs: Meritor likewise specifies lubricating greaseable U-joints until new grease purges from all four seals.

If one bearing will not accept/purge grease, do not assume the joint is fully lubricated.

🚫

Sealed / Service-Free Is Different

Meritor RPL Permalube: factory-sealed, permanently lubricated design; routine lubrication is not required.

Spicer service-free: follow the exact SPL/series instructions. Dana specifically warns not to add grease to non-greasable U-joint kits.

Simple rule: find out what you have before reaching for the grease gun.

↔️

Slip Joint / Slip Splines

  1. Check for excessive radial/side movement.
  2. Check that the slip can move through its required travel without binding.
  3. Inspect spline wear, contamination, seal/boot condition and lubrication where applicable.
  4. If separated, preserve the original relationship/phasing marks.

A seized slip can push/pull loads into the transmission, center bearing or axle as the suspension moves.

⭕

Center / Carrier Bearing

  1. Inspect the bearing and rubber/support structure.
  2. Look for torn rubber, sag, looseness or bracket damage.
  3. Check shaft alignment through the support.
  4. Do not add grease inside a sealed center bearing unless its specific design calls for it.

Dana notes that the bearing itself on applicable center-bearing assemblies is lubricated during manufacture; its XC center bearing is maintenance-free at the bearing/outer surface.

🔁

Phasing

Phasing is the rotational relationship between the yokes in a shaft assembly. If a slip section is separated and put back together in the wrong spline relationship, the shaft can create cyclic speed variation and vibration.

Easy rule: mark components before separation and reinstall them in the original relationship unless the exact manufacturer procedure specifies otherwise.

📐

Driveline Angles

Working angles matter. Transmission/output, driveshaft sections and axle/input geometry work as a system. Suspension ride-height changes, axle movement, incorrect center-bearing position or component replacement can change driveline angles.

Do not chase an angle problem by randomly rotating parts. Measure the vehicle and use the applicable manufacturer/application guideline.

〰️

Vibration

Separate the complaint by when it happens:

  • Road-speed related: shaft balance/runout, U-joint, yoke, center bearing, tire/wheel or driveline geometry.
  • Torque/load related: joint angle, worn U-joint/spline, axle/suspension movement or mount issues.
  • After driveline service: phasing, hardware seating, missing balance weight, incorrect joint/shaft or installation damage.

Do not call every road-speed vibration a driveshaft balance problem.

🔩

Yokes & Attachment Hardware

Inspect end yokes, slip yokes, weld yokes, flange/wing connections and bearing saddles for wear, fretting, looseness, cracks or damaged seating surfaces.

Hardware style and torque are series-specific. Meritor and Dana service literature contain different procedures for their joint families. Use the exact series specification and replace one-time/new-hardware items when required.

🧭

Interaxle Driveshaft

The short shaft between tandem drive axles has the same basic inspection needs—joints, yokes, splines and attachment—but its angles and operating environment can differ from the main shaft.

Meritor's RPL quick reference specifically identifies RPL20 as an interaxle driveline application and RPL25 as a main driveline family. Always identify the actual series on the truck.

📦

Before Ordering Parts

  • Truck VIN and application.
  • Meritor or Spicer identification.
  • Series: e.g. RPL20/RPL25/RPL35 or Spicer 10/SPL family.
  • Full-round, half-round/strap, wing/flange attachment.
  • Bearing-cap diameter and joint width using the correct measurement method.
  • Yoke spline count, major diameter and style where needed.
  • Shaft position: main, interaxle, front/rear section.
  • Greaseable vs service-free.
  • Existing component/assembly numbers whenever available.

Measure to confirm—do not cross by appearance.

Quick Problem Finder

Clunk taking off

Check U-joint play, slip-spline play, yokes, axle/transmission yokes and suspension/axle movement before blaming the differential.

Squeak at low speed

A dry or failing U-joint can squeak, but inspect all rotating driveline and brake/wheel-end sources before condemning it.

Vibration after U-joint job

Recheck joint seating, snap rings/hardware, free articulation, shaft phasing and whether balance components were disturbed.

Grease comes from only one cap

On an applicable greaseable joint, do not assume the other bearings received grease. Follow the manufacturer purge procedure.

Slip yoke stuck

Inspect for contamination, corrosion, spline damage and lubrication/service requirements. A binding slip assembly can load other components.

Repeated U-joint failure

Look beyond the joint: yoke condition, working angles, ride height, spline travel, lubrication, phasing and application can kill the replacement.

Easy memory rule

IDENTIFY → JOINTS → YOKES → SPLINES → CENTER BEARING → PHASING → ANGLES → HARDWARE/LUBE → VERIFY.

This keeps a driveline complaint from turning into an unverified U-joint or driveshaft replacement.

⚠️ Driveshaft Safety

A heavy driveshaft can fall, rotate or separate with enough force to cause serious injury. Chock and support the vehicle correctly, restrain the shaft before disconnecting it, use eye protection and follow the current manufacturer service procedure. Never operate a truck with a loose/damaged U-joint, yoke, driveshaft, center bearing or retaining hardware.
