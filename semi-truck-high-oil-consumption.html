🛢️ HIGH OIL CONSUMPTION

First prove the oil is actually being consumed—and where it is going.

👇 Build the evidence

1️⃣ MEASURE IT. Use a consistent oil-level procedure and track oil added against mileage/hours. Overfilling or inconsistent checks can create a false complaint.
2️⃣ EXTERNAL LEAKS. Inspect engine, filter/housing, pan, covers, seals, turbo oil plumbing and other leak paths. Oil lost outside is not combustion consumption.
3️⃣ INTAKE / CHARGE AIR. Inspect for abnormal oil accumulation and follow the turbo/air-system procedure. Some oil film may be normal; prove excessive transfer before replacing a turbo.
4️⃣ CRANKCASE / BREATHER. Verify the ventilation system and use OEM crankcase-pressure/blow-by tests where applicable. Restrictions and excessive blow-by require different repairs.
5️⃣ EXHAUST / COMBUSTION. Blue/gray smoke, deposits and cylinder evidence can help direct internal-engine testing, but none alone proves rings, liners or valve-related oil control.
6️⃣ OPERATING CONDITIONS. Record load, idle time, duty cycle, oil specification, service history and recent repairs before comparing consumption to manufacturer limits.
7️⃣ MATCH IT WITH CYLINDER EVIDENCE. If oil use is accompanied by rough running, low compression, abnormal blow-by or a cylinder-specific result, use manufacturer-directed mechanical testing to separate valve/head concerns from liner/ring/cylinder-kit concerns.

🧠 Do not jump straight to overhaul

External leaks, turbocharger oil transfer, crankcase ventilation, oil level/grade, operating duty and internal engine condition can all produce an oil-use complaint.
Use current engine-manufacturer consumption limits and test methods for the exact engine serial/application before condemning internal components.
Recent repair clue: If consumption or smoke began after cylinder-head/top-end work, document what changed and verify the disturbed systems. Timing alone does not prove the repair caused the oil use, but it should not be ignored.

🔥 Detroit DD15 oil use + weak-cylinder path

Do not route every DD15 oil-consumption complaint into a compression diagnosis. First prove the oil loss path. If testing also shows a weak cylinder, abnormal blow-by, rough running or other mechanical evidence, the DD15 low-compression path can help organize the next checks.
