💨 EXCESSIVE DIESEL SMOKE

Smoke color is a clue—not a parts list. Record WHEN it smokes and what the engine is doing at the same time.

👇 Capture the evidence

1️⃣ COLOR: Black/dark, white/light vapor, blue/gray or uncertain. Lighting and aftertreatment can change what exhaust looks like, so combine color with other evidence.
2️⃣ WHEN: Cold start, hot idle, acceleration, heavy load, deceleration, after extended idle, during/after regeneration, or constantly.
3️⃣ OTHER SYMPTOMS: Low power, rough idle, coolant loss, oil consumption, fuel smell, overheating, derate or fault codes.
4️⃣ SAVE FAULTS + DATA: Record complete OEM/SPN/FMI information and useful commanded-versus-actual data before clearing codes.
5️⃣ RECENT REPAIR? If smoke began after injector, cylinder-head, camshaft or valvetrain work, treat that timing as evidence and verify the disturbed work before assuming an unrelated failure.

🎨 Smoke color paths

⬛ BLACK / DARK: Think air-to-fuel balance first. Check air restriction, charge-air leaks, boost/control, EGR/air management where applicable, fueling evidence and engine load. Black smoke does not automatically mean bad injectors or turbo.
⬜ WHITE / LIGHT VAPOR: Determine whether it behaves like unburned fuel, coolant/steam, or normal cold condensation. Cold combustion quality, cylinder contribution, fuel delivery, coolant loss and mechanical condition can overlap.
🔵 BLUE / GRAY: Investigate oil entering the combustion or air path, but prove the source. Turbo oil leakage, crankcase/breather conditions and cylinder/valve-related oil control require different tests.

🧠 Match smoke with the second symptom

SMOKE + LOW POWER: Follow air/boost and fuel supply together. A charge-air leak or air-management problem can change both power and smoke.
SMOKE + ROUGH IDLE: Compare cylinder contribution and fuel/mechanical evidence. One weak cylinder is a different path from a whole-engine air problem.
WHITE SMOKE + COOLANT LOSS: Do not assume a head gasket. Confirm the coolant-loss pattern and use the manufacturer pressure/internal-leak path to prove where coolant is going.
BLUE SMOKE + OIL CONSUMPTION: Track actual oil use and inspect intake/charge-air and crankcase systems before condemning internal engine parts.
SMOKE + DERATE / AFTERTREATMENT FAULTS: Diagnose the engine-side cause as well as the aftertreatment consequence. Excess engine-out soot or fluid contamination can create downstream problems.

🔥 Detroit DD15 smoke + weak-cylinder path

If a DD15 has white/abnormal smoke together with rough running, a weak-cylinder result, or a complaint that began after top-end work, separate fuel, coolant and mechanical evidence before replacing parts.
