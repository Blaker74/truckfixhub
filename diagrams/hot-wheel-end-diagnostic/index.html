One Wheel End Is Hot

Do not guess at the bearing. First determine where the heat is coming from.

1 · SEE IT

Confirm which wheel end is hotter than its comparable wheel end after similar operation. Note odor, smoke, lubricant leakage, brake pull, noise and recent service.

↓

2 · STOP FOR DANGER

Smoke, extreme heat, obvious wheel looseness, damaged hub components or signs of imminent wheel-end failure require the vehicle to remain out of service until safely inspected.

↓

3 · NAME THE HEAT SOURCE

Brake heat?Inspect for dragging brake, incomplete release, chamber/slack/caliper issues and friction evidence.
Hub / bearing heat?Check wheel-end play, roughness, noise, lubricant condition and evidence of bearing distress.
Lubrication problem?Check correct lubricant presence, level/condition and signs of loss or contamination.
Seal / service issue?Look for active leakage and verify whether recent wheel-end work could have affected adjustment, lubrication or sealing.
↓

4 · TEST BEFORE DISASSEMBLY

Compare side-to-side evidence. Check brake release, wheel rotation, looseness/play, roughness and visible lubricant condition using safe approved inspection methods. Do not diagnose a bearing from temperature alone.

↓

5 · PROVE THE CAUSE

Repair only the condition supported by evidence. If wheel-end adjustment, bearing condition, seal installation or lubrication is involved, identify the exact axle/hub configuration and use the current manufacturer procedure and specifications.

↓

6 · VERIFY

After repair, verify safe wheel-end operation, correct brake release, no active leakage or abnormal play/noise, and normal comparative temperature under an appropriate road test.

TruckFixHub rule: Heat is evidence—not a part number. Separate brake drag from bearing, lubrication and seal causes before replacing anything.
