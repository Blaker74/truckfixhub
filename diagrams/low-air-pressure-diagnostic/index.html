Low Air Pressure

Do not start by guessing at a valve. Decide whether the truck cannot make enough air, cannot keep the air, or cannot move it where it belongs.

1 · SEE IT — WHAT IS THE AIR SYSTEM DOING?

Record dash pressure behavior, warning condition, build time behavior, whether pressure falls with the engine off, and whether the complaint changes when brakes or accessories are used.

↓

2 · NAME THE FAILURE PATTERN

WON'T BUILDPressure stays low or rises very slowly.
BUILDS, THEN FALLSThe system makes air but cannot retain it.
ONE CIRCUIT / FUNCTIONMain supply exists but a branch does not work correctly.
WARNING DOESN'T MATCHGauge/sensor/warning information may disagree with actual system pressure.
↓

3 · TEST — CAN THE SYSTEM GENERATE AIR?

If pressure will not build normally, inspect the air-generation path: compressor drive/function, compressor inlet, discharge path, dryer-related restriction/control, governor/control behavior and major open leakage. Do not condemn the compressor until its inlet, discharge and control conditions are understood.

↓

DOES PRESSURE BUILD BUT THEN DROP?

If yes, move away from generation and toward leakage. With the vehicle safely secured, listen and inspect systematically. Separate a continuous leak from a leak that appears only during brake application, parking-brake release, suspension/accessory operation or another command.

↓

4 · TEST — FOLLOW THE AIR PATH

Work from source → dryer → reservoirs → protection/control → affected circuit → end device. Compare what pressure is available before and after the suspected restriction or control point. A downstream symptom does not automatically mean the downstream component failed.

↓

5 · PROVE IT

After repair, verify normal pressure build and retention, correct warning behavior, correct brake/accessory operation, and that the original complaint does not return. Confirm exact performance limits using current information for the vehicle and components.

⚠️ SAFETY: Air brakes are safety-critical and contain stored energy. Chock and secure the vehicle, use proper support, and follow current manufacturer procedures. Never rely on this general path for exact pressure limits, leakage limits, adjustment values or service procedures.
