Check it in this order

1️⃣ LOOK
2️⃣ LEAK TEST
3️⃣ CHECK DATA
4️⃣ PROVE TURBO

Look first: filter and intake ducting, clamps, boots, charge-air piping and cooler. A leak may only show itself under load.

Then test: use the approved charge-air leak test and exact limits for the vehicle. Compare relevant sensor and commanded-versus-actual data when available.

🎛️ Turbo control clue?

Ask three simple questions: Did the truck command movement? Did the command reach the actuator? Did the turbo mechanism respond? A control problem can look like a bad turbo even when the turbo itself is not the root cause.

Low boost is a result, not a turbo diagnosis. Intake restriction, charge-air leakage, control problems, sensor inputs, engine demand and exhaust restriction can all affect boost.

🛑 SAFETY: Turbochargers and exhaust components can be extremely hot. Keep clear of rotating machinery. Never use improvised pressure-test methods or exceed approved system pressure. Verify procedures and limits for the exact engine and chassis.
