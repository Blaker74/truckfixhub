Truck Pulls or Wanders

Do not start with alignment. Prove the mechanical condition first.

1 · Is the truck safe to drive?

Stop for severe steering looseness, damaged steering or suspension parts, unsafe tires, wheel-end looseness, excessive heat, or anything that could affect control of the vehicle.

↓
2 · Tires & wheels

Compare tire pressure, size, condition and wear across the axle. Look for irregular wear, damaged tires, mismatched tires, bent/damaged wheels, missing hardware or obvious runout.

↓
3 · Wheel ends

Check for hub/bearing looseness, abnormal heat, brake drag and other wheel-end conditions that can create a pull or unstable steering feel.

↓
4 · Steering hardware

Inspect steering linkage, joints, steering gear mounting and related hardware for looseness, damage or movement. Correct mechanical faults before alignment.

↓
5 · Suspension & ride height

Inspect springs, bushings, torque rods, shocks, mounts and air-suspension components. Verify ride height using the correct vehicle procedure before judging alignment.

↓
6 · Does it pull only while braking?

If yes, move to brake diagnosis. A brake-side problem can mimic a steering or alignment pull.

↓
7 · Alignment comes after the hardware passes

Only after tires, wheel ends, steering and suspension are proven serviceable should alignment measurements and adjustments become the next step.

TruckFixHub rule: Tires & wheels → wheel ends → steering hardware → suspension & ride height → alignment.
