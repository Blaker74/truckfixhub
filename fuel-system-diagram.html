👇 Follow the complaint

🔑 CRANKS BUT WON’T START: Confirm usable fuel, then prove the tank-to-engine supply path. Check restriction, filters, air intrusion and low-side performance before moving into high-pressure/injection diagnosis.
🐌 LOW POWER UNDER LOAD: A supply problem may hide at idle and appear when demand rises. Save faults, inspect filters/plumbing, and test supply and commanded-versus-actual pressure under the condition that creates the complaint when the OEM procedure allows it.
🌫️ ROUGH / SMOKE: Record when the symptom occurs. Fuel is one possibility, but air supply and mechanical engine condition matter too. Use directed cylinder/injector tests rather than smoke color alone.
⚠️ FUEL PRESSURE CODE: Desired pressure not being achieved begins a diagnostic path; it does not automatically identify a failed pump, sensor, regulator or injector. Save the complete code and test the system in order.
🫧 LOSES PRIME / HARD START: Some low-pressure sections can draw air without producing an obvious external fuel leak. Use the manufacturer-approved air-intrusion test instead of relying only on visual inspection.

🧠 Before replacing expensive parts

1️⃣ Is the fuel usable and available? Verify quantity/condition and consider contamination or pickup problems.
2️⃣ Can fuel reach the engine? Check lines, filters, restrictions, leakage and air intrusion.
3️⃣ Can the supply keep up? Test the low-pressure side under the conditions that create the complaint.
4️⃣ What is commanded versus actual? Use the exact engine’s data and diagnostic procedure before condemning pressure-generation or injection components.
