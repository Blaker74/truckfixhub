👇 Follow the complaint

🔑 CRANKS BUT WON’T START: Confirm usable fuel, then prove tank-to-engine supply, restriction, filters, air intrusion and required pressure before moving deeper.
🫧 LOSES PRIME: Focus on drain-back and suction-side air entry. A connection can pull air without leaving an obvious external fuel leak.
📉 LOW PRESSURE: First identify whether the evidence concerns low-side supply or high-pressure injection. Low pressure is a result; it does not automatically condemn a pump.
🛑 STALLS: Save evidence before repeated key cycles. Decide whether fuel faded, electrical/control power disappeared, or a protection strategy commanded shutdown.
⏱️ DIES AT IDLE: Compare hot/cold behavior and determine whether pressure, command, electrical inputs or combustion stability disappears as RPM falls.
🛢️ FUEL IN OIL: Treat rising oil level/fuel dilution seriously. Investigate injection/combustion, engine-specific fuel-oil interfaces and relevant regeneration history.
🐌 LOW POWER: A supply restriction can hide at idle and appear only when demand rises. Test under the condition that creates the complaint.
🌫️ ROUGH / SMOKE: Fuel is only one branch. Air supply and mechanical condition also matter. Use directed cylinder/injector testing rather than smoke color alone.

🧠 The expensive-parts firewall

1️⃣ Is the correct fuel available?
2️⃣ Can it leave the tank and pass the filters without excessive restriction or air?
3️⃣ Can low-side supply maintain required delivery when demand changes?
4️⃣ What is commanded versus actual on the exact engine?
5️⃣ Which approved test proves the pump, control, leakage path, injector or cylinder is actually at fault?

🛑 DIESEL DIES AT IDLE

If it runs with throttle but will not stay running at idle, define what changes when engine speed and fuel demand drop.

1️⃣ HOT OR COLD? Note coolant temperature, restart behavior, idle time before stall, accessory loads and whether raising RPM prevents the stall.
2️⃣ SAVE FAULTS / DATA: Look for engine speed, commanded/actual fuel pressure, voltage and other application-specific inputs before guessing at injectors.
3️⃣ FUEL SUPPLY: Inspect filters, air intrusion and supply performance. Some faults behave differently at idle than under load because control and pressure conditions change.
4️⃣ ELECTRICAL / INPUTS: Check module power and grounds plus critical speed/position or control inputs if data drops out as the engine dies.
5️⃣ CONTROL VS MECHANICAL: Determine whether fueling is being commanded away, pressure cannot be maintained, or the engine cannot sustain stable combustion because of a mechanical/cylinder condition.
6️⃣ VERIFY: After repair, test hot and cold idle, electrical/accessory loads and the original operating condition.
HOT / COLD → DATA → FUEL SUPPLY → COMMAND / PRESSURE → ELECTRICAL INPUTS → MECHANICAL IF PROVEN → VERIFY.
⚠️ Keep clear of belts, fans and moving components while diagnosing a running engine. Electronically controlled fans can engage unexpectedly.

🛢️ DIESEL FUEL IN ENGINE OIL

Fuel dilution can reduce lubricant viscosity and threaten bearings and other engine components. Find the entry path before continued operation.

1️⃣ CONFIRM THE CONDITION: Rising oil level, abnormal odor or suspected thin oil are clues. Use the engine manufacturer’s approved inspection/testing approach when fuel dilution is suspected.
2️⃣ REVIEW OPERATING HISTORY: Note excessive idling, repeated interrupted regeneration, cold operation, misfire/poor combustion, recent fuel-system work and when the oil level began rising.
3️⃣ INJECTION PATH: Depending on engine design, injector leakage or poor combustion can contribute to fuel entering the crankcase. Use directed injector/cylinder tests rather than replacing a complete set from suspicion.
4️⃣ FUEL / OIL INTERFACES: Some engines contain pumps, housings, seals or other interfaces where a failure can allow fuel and oil to cross. Follow the exact engine’s service information to identify possible paths.
5️⃣ AFTERTREATMENT STRATEGY: On engines that use in-cylinder fueling strategies associated with regeneration, review regeneration history and related faults. Do not assume regeneration is the cause without evidence.
6️⃣ REPAIR + OIL SERVICE + VERIFY: Correct the proven source and follow manufacturer requirements for contaminated oil/filter service. Monitor oil level and condition afterward.
CONFIRM DILUTION → OPERATING HISTORY → INJECTION / COMBUSTION → FUEL-OIL INTERFACES → REGEN STRATEGY → PROVE SOURCE → SERVICE → VERIFY.
⚠️ Significant fuel dilution can damage an engine. If oil level is rising or lubrication is compromised, avoid continued operation until the cause and required service are determined.

🫧 DIESEL LOSES PRIME

Hard starting after sitting often means fuel drained back or air entered the low-pressure side. No visible fuel leak does not rule out air intrusion.

1️⃣ DEFINE THE PATTERN: Overnight only? Nose uphill/downhill? After filter service? Hot soak? Long parking time? Record what changes the start.
2️⃣ START AT THE TANK: Verify fuel level, pickup integrity and plumbing. A pickup or line problem can expose itself only at certain fuel levels or vehicle angles.
3️⃣ FILTERS / SEALS: Inspect filter installation, seals, housings, drains and fittings. A suction-side leak may pull air without dripping fuel externally.
4️⃣ LINES / CONNECTIONS: Inspect hoses, quick connections, fittings and rubbed sections from tank to supply pump. Follow the OEM air-intrusion test when available.
5️⃣ HOLDING / CHECK FUNCTIONS: If the system uses check valves or other devices to retain fuel, test them by the exact engine procedure instead of replacing them from suspicion.
6️⃣ VERIFY: After repair, recreate the original soak time and parking condition. A quick restart immediately after repair does not prove an overnight drain-back complaint is fixed.
WHEN DOES PRIME DISAPPEAR? → TANK / PICKUP → FILTER / SEALS → LINES → RETENTION → PROVE → SOAK → VERIFY.
⚠️ Diesel fuel systems vary widely. High-pressure sections can cause injection injury. Follow the manufacturer procedure before opening or testing pressurized fuel components.

📉 LOW FUEL PRESSURE

Low pressure is a system result. It does not automatically mean the pump is bad.

1️⃣ IDENTIFY WHICH PRESSURE: Low-pressure supply and high-pressure injection systems are different diagnostic paths. Know what the fault/data actually represents.
2️⃣ VERIFY THE COMPLAINT: Compare commanded and actual data where applicable and note whether the problem appears at idle, crank, acceleration or heavy load.
3️⃣ PROVE SUPPLY: Check fuel condition, pickup, lines, filters, restriction and air intrusion before condemning expensive pressure-generation components.
4️⃣ TEST DELIVERY: A supply system can look adequate at idle and fail when demand rises. Use the OEM pressure/flow/restriction procedure under the relevant condition.
5️⃣ CONTROL / SENSOR PLAUSIBILITY: A control valve, regulator, sensor, wiring issue or leakage path can affect pressure. Test the exact system instead of treating one code description as a parts list.
6️⃣ VERIFY: Repeat the load or operating condition that originally caused pressure to fall and confirm actual response now follows the required command/specification.
WHICH PRESSURE? → COMMAND VS ACTUAL → SUPPLY → RESTRICTION / AIR → DELIVERY → CONTROL / LEAKAGE → PROVE → VERIFY.
⚠️ Never loosen high-pressure diesel lines to “see if fuel comes out.” High-pressure fuel can penetrate skin. Use approved test procedures and equipment.

🛑 ENGINE STALLS WHILE DRIVING

A stall can come from fuel loss, electrical/control loss, a protective shutdown or mechanical failure. Preserve the evidence before cycling the key repeatedly.

1️⃣ CAPTURE THE EVENT: Was it instant like the key turned off, or did power fade first? Did gauges/modules die too? Did warning lamps appear? Save codes and freeze-frame/event data.
2️⃣ FUEL SIDE: If it fades, surges or loses power first, prove tank pickup, venting where applicable, filters, restriction, air intrusion and supply performance.
3️⃣ ELECTRICAL / CONTROL: If shutdown is instant, inspect module power/grounds, ignition feeds, connectors, engine speed/position inputs and network-related evidence using wiring diagrams.
4️⃣ SHUTDOWN STRATEGY: Determine whether the engine controller intentionally commanded shutdown because of a protection condition. A shutdown result is not the root cause.
5️⃣ INTERMITTENT CLUES: Heat, vibration, bumps, rain, fuel level and load can expose different failures. Recreate safely while monitoring the data most likely to disappear first.
6️⃣ VERIFY: Repair only the proven cause, then reproduce the original road/load/temperature condition safely.
HOW DID IT DIE? → SAVE DATA → FUEL / ELECTRICAL / PROTECTION → FIND WHAT DISAPPEARED FIRST → PROVE → REPAIR → VERIFY.
⚠️ An engine that stalls in traffic can create an immediate road hazard. Move the vehicle to a safe location before diagnosis and follow manufacturer safety procedures.
