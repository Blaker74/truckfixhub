Find the true source before replacing a seal.
🔥 Hub running hot?
Separate bearing heat from brake heat.
↔️ Wheel has movement?
Treat unexpected looseness as a safety concern.
🔊 Growl or rumble?
Prove whether the sound is really from the wheel end.

👇 PICK THE COMPLAINT

💧 Oil or grease is showing around the wheel

  1. Secure the vehicle and identify exactly where the lubricant begins.
  2. Check lubricant level and condition using the correct wheel-end procedure.
  3. Inspect the hub / axle venting arrangement where applicable.
  4. Inspect seal area, spindle / seal surfaces and bearing condition rather than replacing the seal alone.
  5. If lubricant reached the brake components, inspect and service the brake system according to manufacturer requirements before returning the vehicle to service.

🔥 One hub is much hotter than the others

  1. Stop safely if heat is severe, increasing rapidly, or accompanied by smoke, odor or looseness.
  2. Compare the affected wheel end with similar wheel ends under comparable operating conditions.
  3. Check for brake drag first because brake heat can look like a bearing problem.
  4. Inspect lubricant level / condition and wheel-bearing adjustment or end play using the manufacturer procedure.
  5. Inspect bearings, races and related components if lubrication, adjustment or condition indicates a wheel-end problem.

↔️ The wheel / hub has noticeable movement

  1. Do not keep operating the vehicle until the looseness is understood.
  2. Properly support the vehicle and verify where the movement occurs.
  3. Measure bearing end play / adjustment using the approved procedure rather than judging by feel alone.
  4. Inspect bearings, races, spindle, hub and retention hardware as required.
  5. After repair or adjustment, verify the complete wheel-end assembly meets manufacturer specifications.

Leaks, heat, looseness and noise are clues

A wheel-end complaint can come from more than one component. A leaking seal may be related to seal surfaces, lubrication, venting or bearing condition. Heat can come from bearings or brake drag. Noise can come from bearings, tires or other rotating parts. Inspect the complete wheel end and use the correct manufacturer bearing-adjustment procedure instead of guessing from one symptom.
