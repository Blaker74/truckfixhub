👇 Diagnose in the right order

🔥 OVERHEATING: Let the system cool safely. Verify coolant quantity and leakage, inspect cooling-stack airflow, determine whether the fan operates when required, then evaluate circulation and deeper causes using the correct OEM procedure.
💧 COOLANT LOSS: Find where the coolant is going. Inspect for external evidence first. If no external leak is found, move to manufacturer-approved pressure and internal-leak testing rather than assuming a head gasket.
🌬️ FAN WON’T ENGAGE: Separate the fan mechanism from the control system. Verify the exact fan design, commands, electrical/air supply and applicable temperature or pressure inputs before replacing the fan clutch or control component.
🌀 FAN STAYS ON: Determine whether the fan is actually being commanded on because of temperature, A/C, pressure, fault strategy or another request. Then test the control path and fan mechanism.
🫧 PUSHES COOLANT / BUILDS PRESSURE: Pressure and bubbles are clues, not automatic proof of combustion leakage. Check fill level, cap/reservoir system, overheating, flow and airflow first; prove internal leakage with the specified test before major disassembly.

🧠 Read the operating condition

At idle only: airflow/fan behavior can become especially important because road speed is not supplying airflow.
Highway speed: inspect cooling-stack restriction, coolant circulation, operating load and other conditions that can overwhelm heat rejection.
Only on a hard pull: record load, boost, engine performance, fan behavior and temperature progression. Increased heat production may expose a marginal cooling system or another engine problem.
After shutdown: temperature and pressure behavior can differ from a running complaint. Record exactly when coolant loss or pressure occurs instead of combining different symptoms.
