📉 AIR PRESSURE DROPS FAST?

Quick answer

Build the system to normal pressure, secure the vehicle and determine when the pressure loss occurs: brakes released, service brake applied, parking brakes released, trailer connected or disconnected, or suspension/accessories active. That condition tells you which branch to isolate. A fast pressure drop is not automatically a bad compressor—the compressor may simply be trying to replace air the system is losing.

Isolation order

  1. Record primary and secondary gauge behavior.
  2. Compare engine running versus engine off using approved leak-test procedures.
  3. Listen and use approved leak-detection solution at fittings, valves, chambers and lines.
  4. Compare service brake released versus applied.
  5. Compare tractor-only versus trailer connected.
  6. Check suspension and accessory circuits if loss changes with those systems.
  7. Trace the leaking branch before replacing the component where the air is heard.

Pattern clues

Drops only with service brake applied: focus on service delivery circuits, chambers, hoses and related valves.
Changes when trailer is disconnected: isolate tractor supply/control from trailer-side leakage.
Air heard at a valve exhaust: the valve may be exhausting air supplied backward from another failed component. Trace the source.
Truck also builds air slowly: diagnose leakage and supply/compressor performance together.
🧠 Parts-cannon rule: replace the failed source, not automatically the valve where you hear the air escaping.
⚠️ Air brakes are safety-critical and contain stored energy. Chock/support the vehicle and follow approved brake-system service procedures.
