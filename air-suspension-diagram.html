🎈 AIR SUSPENSION

Follow the air from the supply to the air bags.

1️⃣ AIR SUPPLY
The vehicle air system provides the suspension with compressed air
↓
2️⃣ HEIGHT CONTROL / DUMP CONTROL
Controls air entering or leaving the suspension to manage ride height
↓
3️⃣ AIR LINES + FITTINGS
Carry air between valves and air springs
↓
4️⃣ AIR SPRINGS / BAGS
Support the chassis while allowing suspension movement
↓
5️⃣ AXLE LOCATION COMPONENTS
Torque rods, bushings, shocks and mounts locate and control axle movement

Think local first.
⬇️ Whole suspension low?

👇 PICK THE COMPLAINT

⬇️ One side sits low

  1. Properly support the vehicle before working around the suspension.
  2. Compare the affected side with the opposite side and inspect the air spring for damage or leakage.
  3. Check local lines, fittings and plumbing serving that side.
  4. Inspect mounts, linkage and mechanical suspension parts for damage or binding.
  5. If local components are sound, follow the plumbing upstream toward the height-control system.

⬇️ The whole suspension stays low

  1. Confirm the vehicle air system has adequate pressure.
  2. Check whether a dump control is commanded or stuck in the dump position.
  3. Inspect the height-control valve, linkage and shared supply plumbing.
  4. Verify air reaches the suspension control before condemning multiple air springs.
  5. Use the manufacturer ride-height procedure after the air/control problem is corrected.

💨 It leaks down while parked

  1. Note whether one corner, one axle or the entire suspension drops.
  2. Safely support the vehicle and use an approved leak-detection method.
  3. Check air springs, fittings and lines first in the area that drops.
  4. Check height-control and dump valves if the leak is shared or no external line/bag leak is found.
  5. Repair the proven leak and recheck after the truck sits under the same conditions.

Separate the air problem from the mechanical problem

Air pressure supports the vehicle, but torque rods, bushings, shocks, mounts and other suspension parts control axle location and movement. A truck that sits low may have an air-supply or leak problem; a truck that shifts, clunks or tracks poorly may also have worn mechanical suspension components. Inspect both sides of the system before replacing parts.
