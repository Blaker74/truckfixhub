🎈 SEMI TRUCK AIR SUSPENSION TROUBLESHOOTING

Follow the air from the supply to the air bags.

👇 PICK THE COMPLAINT

⬇️ One side sits low

  1. Properly support the vehicle before working around the suspension.
  2. Compare the affected side with the opposite side and inspect the air spring for damage or leakage.
  3. Check local lines, fittings and plumbing serving that side.
  4. Inspect mounts, linkage and mechanical suspension parts for damage or binding.
  5. If local components are sound, follow the plumbing upstream toward the height-control system.

⬇️ The whole suspension stays low

  1. Confirm the vehicle air system has adequate pressure.
  2. Check whether a dump control is commanded or stuck in the dump position.
  3. Inspect the height-control valve, linkage and shared supply plumbing.
  4. Verify air reaches the suspension control before condemning multiple air springs.
  5. Use the manufacturer ride-height procedure after the air/control problem is corrected.

💨 It leaks down while parked

  1. Note whether one corner, one axle or the entire suspension drops.
  2. Safely support the vehicle and use an approved leak-detection method.
  3. Check air springs, fittings and lines first in the area that drops.
  4. Check height-control and dump valves if the leak is shared or no external line/bag leak is found.
  5. Repair the proven leak and recheck after the truck sits under the same conditions.

📚 Air suspension diagnosis starts with the pattern

Where the truck drops, when it drops and whether it can recover are valuable clues. One corner, one axle and the whole suspension do not point to the same part of the system. Use the pattern to decide whether to begin local or at a shared supply/control.

⬇️ One corner vs. the whole suspension

A single low corner makes local plumbing, the air spring, linkage, mounts and nearby mechanical damage more important. When an entire suspension group stays low, shared air supply, dump control or height-control plumbing moves higher on the list. Trace the air path rather than replacing multiple bags at once.

💨 Leak-down time is evidence

Record how long the truck takes to settle and exactly which area moves first. A fast drop may be easier to locate than a small intermittent leak, but both should be tested under comparable conditions. After repair, repeat the same parked test to prove the leak-down rate changed.

↕️ Do not adjust ride height to hide a fault

Before changing a height-control adjustment, inspect the linkage, valve mounting, air supply, air springs and axle-locating components. Adjustment cannot repair a leaking bag, bent linkage, damaged bushing or incorrect mechanical position. Use the manufacturer’s specified measurement points and procedure for final ride height.

🎈 Air bags support—the suspension locates

Air springs carry load and allow movement, but torque rods, bushings, mounts and related components control axle position. A truck can hold air correctly and still have tracking, clunking or geometry problems because the mechanical side of the suspension is worn or damaged.

🛑 Dump function can imitate a failure

If equipped with a dump system, confirm its commanded state and control operation before assuming the suspension has lost supply. A control that remains in dump, a plumbing problem or a valve issue can keep the suspension down even when the vehicle air system itself has pressure.

📐 Ride height can affect other systems

Incorrect chassis-to-axle position can change driveline and suspension geometry and may contribute to secondary ride, tracking or vibration complaints. Correct the air or mechanical cause first, then verify ride height using the exact application procedure.

TruckFixHub diagnostic chain: support vehicle → identify what drops → verify air supply → inspect local/shared plumbing and controls → inspect mechanical axle location → correct proven cause → set/verify ride height → repeat leak-down check.
