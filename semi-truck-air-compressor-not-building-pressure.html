🧰 AIR COMPRESSOR NOT BUILDING PRESSURE?

Quick answer

Do not replace the compressor until you prove it cannot supply air. A major system leak, governor/unloader control problem, restricted compressor intake, discharge-line problem or air-dryer/supply-path fault can all produce little or no tank-pressure increase. Follow the compressor and chassis manufacturer's test procedure to separate compressor output from downstream loss.

Diagnostic order

  1. Check for a massive audible air leak before testing the compressor.
  2. Confirm the compressor is mechanically driven as designed.
  3. Inspect compressor intake supply for restriction/damage.
  4. Verify governor/unloader control state using manufacturer procedures.
  5. Inspect discharge plumbing for damage, leakage, blockage or severe restriction.
  6. Inspect dryer/supply path and related check-valve/control components according to the system schematic.
  7. Perform the OEM compressor output/build test before condemning the compressor.

Pattern clues

Pressure never rises from near zero: look for major loss, supply-path blockage/control issue or lack of compressor output.
Pressure rises but extremely slowly: use the dedicated slow-build path and compare leakage with compressor performance.
Air continuously exhausts at dryer: diagnose purge/control and compressor loaded/unloaded state.
Problem started after compressor/dryer/governor repair: verify line routing and component installation against the schematic.
🧠 Parts-cannon rule: no tank pressure does not automatically equal a failed compressor. Prove compressor output separately from downstream loss or restriction.
⚠️ Never disconnect a pressurized air line. Secure the vehicle and exhaust the affected circuit before service.
