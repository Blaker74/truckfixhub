⚙️ REAR AXLE & DIFFERENTIAL

Follow the torque from the driveshaft to the drive tires.

1️⃣ DRIVESHAFT
Delivers rotating torque from the transmission to the drive axle
↓
2️⃣ INPUT / PINION
Transfers torque into the axle gearset
↓
3️⃣ RING GEAR + DIFFERENTIAL
Changes torque direction and allows drive wheels to rotate at different speeds while turning
↓
4️⃣ AXLE SHAFTS
Carry torque from the differential outward toward the wheel ends
↓
5️⃣ HUBS → DRIVE TIRES
Transfer axle torque to the wheels and road

👇 PICK THE COMPLAINT

🎵 It whines or howls at road speed

  1. Write down whether the noise occurs on acceleration, coast, steady throttle or while turning.
  2. Verify axle lubricant level and condition using the correct procedure.
  3. Separate tire and wheel-end noise from axle noise before opening the differential.
  4. Inspect driveline / pinion-area condition and note whether torque direction changes the sound.
  5. If the noise is isolated to the axle, continue with the axle manufacturer’s bearing and gearset diagnostic procedure.

🔨 It clunks when taking off or changing from pull to coast

  1. Secure the vehicle and inspect U-joints, yokes and driveline connections first.
  2. Check for excessive rotational movement while separating transmission, driveshaft and axle sources.
  3. Inspect pinion/input and axle-related mounting or connection concerns.
  4. Check axle shafts / differential only after upstream driveline play is understood.
  5. Repair the proven source of excessive play rather than assuming the ring and pinion are bad.

🚫 Engine and transmission work, but the truck will not drive

  1. Do not work around a rotating driveline; use safe manufacturer-approved testing.
  2. Determine whether torque reaches the driveshaft and axle input.
  3. If the axle input receives torque, inspect the axle shafts and differential path using the proper procedure.
  4. On tandem-drive systems, include inter-axle / power-divider components and controls as equipped.
  5. Find the exact point where torque stops before disassembling major components.

When the noise changes is one of your best clues

Axle and differential noises often change as torque direction changes. A complaint that appears under acceleration, changes on coast, follows road speed or changes while turning gives useful direction—but it does not prove one gear or bearing has failed. Compare the symptom with lubricant condition, driveline inspection and the axle manufacturer’s diagnostic procedure.
