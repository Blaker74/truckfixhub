TRUCKFIXHUB AFTERTREATMENT GUIDE

Semi Truck Regen Light On?

A regen or DPF warning can mean the aftertreatment system needs regeneration, but the warning by itself does not identify the root cause. The correct response depends on the truck, engine, warning level and active fault codes.

Start with what the dash is telling you

  • Record the exact warning message or lamp.
  • Capture all active SPN/FMI fault codes before clearing anything.
  • Note whether the truck is requesting a regeneration or already in a derate.
  • Record whether a parked regen has been attempted and what happened.
  • Note recent low-power, DEF, NOx, exhaust-temperature or DPF-related complaints.

Why might the regen light come on?

Depending on the engine and aftertreatment system, technicians may investigate DPF soot loading, differential-pressure information, exhaust-temperature inputs, regeneration history, operating conditions, sensors, wiring and other engine or aftertreatment faults that can affect regeneration.

Regen requested but it will not start?

A parked regeneration may be inhibited when required operating conditions are not met or when an active fault prevents the regeneration strategy from proceeding. Do not repeatedly force regen attempts without first checking the fault information and the manufacturer's requirements.

DPF differential-pressure fault?

Differential-pressure information is one input used to evaluate DPF conditions. If a related fault is present, diagnosis may include the sensor, tubes or plumbing, wiring and connectors, DPF condition and OEM-directed tests rather than replacing a component from the code description alone.

DEF or derate warning at the same time?

Capture the entire warning sequence. A regen complaint can exist alongside SCR, DEF, NOx or other aftertreatment faults, and the final derate may be a consequence rather than the original failure.

Before replacing parts

Use the complete fault-code set, engine data and regeneration history to choose the diagnostic path. A regen warning does not automatically mean the DPF itself needs replacement.

Important regeneration safety

Regeneration can create very high exhaust temperatures. Follow the truck and engine manufacturer's regeneration procedure, location requirements and warnings. Keep the exhaust area clear of people and combustible materials. Do not bypass emissions controls or safety interlocks.
