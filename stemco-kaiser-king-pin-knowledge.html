King Pins — Made Simple

The king pin connects the steering knuckle to the steer axle and lets the wheel end pivot for steering. Diagnose the looseness, identify the exact kit, inspect the axle and spindle, install the correct bushing system, grease it thoroughly, then prove the repair.

What is a king pin?

The king pin is the pivot between the steer axle and steering knuckle. Bushings support the pin inside the spindle/knuckle. Wear creates unwanted movement that can affect steering feel and tire wear.

What usually wears?

The pin, upper/lower bushings, thrust bearing, seals and related hardware are service items, but the axle eye and spindle bores must also be inspected. A new kit cannot correct a damaged or out-of-round foundation.

STEMCO Kaiser kit families

QwikKit® — spiral steel, no ream

QwikKit uses grooved king pins and no-ream spiral steel bushings. STEMCO says its groove/bushing system directs grease to high-wear areas and holds substantially more grease than standard sets. The bushings are designed to help move debris outward. This is the fast-install family many technicians associate with Kaiser.

PlusKit® — pre-sized bi-metal bronze, no ream

PlusKit is the bronze-bushing no-ream option. STEMCO describes the pre-sized bi-metal bronze bushings as providing a precision fit without conventional reaming.

ECONO KIT™ — traditional ream

ECONO KIT is STEMCO Kaiser's conventional ream-style option. A reamer is required. Do not use QwikKit/PlusKit no-ream installation logic on an ECONO KIT.

Do not order a king pin by appearance

Identify the axle/application and verify the exact STEMCO Kaiser kit or OEM cross. STEMCO reports hundreds of kit options and numerous QwikKit applications. King pins that look similar can differ in diameter, length, lock arrangement, bearing, seals, bushings and application hardware.

Signs that deserve inspection

  • Steer-tire wear such as one-sided, feathered or diagonal patterns
  • Clunking, popping or grinding around the steering knuckle
  • Visible or measurable spindle movement
  • Wander or steering looseness
  • Grease contamination or inability to lubricate correctly

Prove movement first

STEMCO's QwikKit training uses a magnetic-base dial indicator on the axle with the indicator at the spindle eye while the tire is pried up and down. Its current training instruction calls 0.010 inch or more movement a replacement indication. Treat this as STEMCO's QwikKit training criterion and verify the exact axle/OEM limit when required.

The most important inspection may happen after the old pin is out

Clean the axle eye and spindle bores. Check them for damage and out-of-round condition. STEMCO training calls for gauging the axle eye and spindle eyes; some kits include a No-Go gauge. If a king pin fits the two spindle bushings individually but will not pass through both together, STEMCO warns the spindle may be spread and may require replacement.

QwikKit installation — simple building blocks

This is an organized summary of STEMCO's training path. Exact kit instructions take priority because king-pin applications vary.

Secure the vehicle and measure the old assembly

Use PPE, chock and support the vehicle correctly. Before teardown, measure king-pin/spindle movement with a dial indicator so the complaint has evidence.

Remove the wheel end and steering hardware

Follow established vehicle procedures to remove the wheel, brake/hub components, steering arms/cross bar as required, king-pin end plates and lock pins. STEMCO specifically cautions against using heat to remove lock pins or the king pin.

Remove the king pin, spindle, bushings and seals

Support the work safely and remove the pin using suitable press/jack methods. Keep the old shims as a reference. For QwikKit bushings, use the correct STEMCO Kaiser bushing driver rather than a punch or chisel that could damage the spindle eye.

Clean and inspect the axle eye and spindle

Clean the bores and remove light burrs/contamination without enlarging the bores. Gauge the axle eye and spindle bores for condition and out-of-round. Do not hide a damaged axle or spindle with a new bushing.

Install the lip seals correctly

On applicable QwikKit installations, install the new lip seals carefully with the lip oriented toward the axle eye. Lip seals are easily damaged, so use the proper driver for the kit.

Install the QwikKit spiral bushings

Use the STEMCO Kaiser driver/tool specified for the kit. The spiral bushing is started and installed according to the kit instruction; do not substitute a reaming procedure for the no-ream QwikKit design.

Test-fit the king pin before final assembly

Clean the bushings, insert the pin through each bushing individually, then through both together. The pin should pass through the aligned bores. If it fits separately but not together, stop and investigate spindle alignment/spread.

Prepare the thrust bearing and shim stack

Pre-pack the bearing with grease and orient it exactly as the kit instructions show. STEMCO's QwikKit training uses the removed shim stack as a starting reference and fine-tunes the stack for proper bearing fit. Do not treat one shim stack as universal.

Install the pin and lock hardware

Orient the king pin's TOP marking and lock flat exactly as the application instructions require. Install the spindle, shims, bearing and pin without damaging the bearing. Align and install the lock pin(s). Torque values vary across STEMCO instructions/applications, so use the exact instruction sheet for the kit rather than one universal number.

Install end plates/plugs and grease fittings

Fit the correct seals/O-rings, plates or plugs and grease fittings supplied for the application. Follow the kit-specific fastening procedure.

Grease it thoroughly while moving the spindle

STEMCO directs technicians to pump grease while moving the spindle side to side until grease appears at the intended exit areas. QwikKit carries a large grease volume; insufficient initial grease can make steering feel stiff until lubricant reaches the grooves.

Reassemble and prove the repair

Reinstall steering, brake, hub and wheel components using their exact procedures. Recheck movement with the dial indicator and confirm the spindle turns smoothly with minimal unwanted movement. Verify steering operation before returning the vehicle to service.

⚠️ King-pin safety boundaries

Steering and wheel-end work is safety-critical. A steer axle must be supported correctly and heavy components controlled. Do not use heat as a shortcut on the king-pin/axle assembly where STEMCO prohibits it. Exact movement limits, lock-pin torque, wheel-end setup, brake procedures and wheel torque must match the exact axle, kit and vehicle instructions.

QwikKit

Think: spiral steel bushing + grooved pin + no ream + high grease capacity.

PlusKit

Think: pre-sized bi-metal bronze bushing + no ream.

ECONO KIT

Think: conventional bushing installation + reamer required.

King Pin Tools

STEMCO Kaiser offers application-specific bushing drivers and removal tooling. Match the tool to the kit/application instead of improvising against the spindle bore.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Calling every loose steer wheel a king-pin failure without measuring it.
  • Replacing the kit without checking axle-eye and spindle-bore condition.
  • Ordering by pin appearance instead of axle/application.
  • Using a punch/chisel on QwikKit bushings and damaging the spindle bore.
  • Using heat to force removal where STEMCO prohibits it.
  • Reaming a no-ream QwikKit or PlusKit.
  • Forcing a pin through misaligned spindle bores.
  • Using a universal shim stack or torque value across different kits.
  • Under-greasing the new QwikKit.
  • Finishing the job without rechecking movement.
