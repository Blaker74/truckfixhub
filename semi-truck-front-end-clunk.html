🔨 FRONT-END CLUNK

A clunk is easiest to find when you first identify what movement makes it happen.

1️⃣ RECREATE THE EVENT: Bump, brake application, steering input, acceleration, driveway twist or only one wheel hitting a bump?
2️⃣ VISUAL EVIDENCE: Look for shiny witness marks, shifted parts, loose/missing hardware, damaged bushings, cracked components and abnormal contact.
3️⃣ STEERING LINKAGE: Inspect steering shaft/linkage, drag link, tie rod, steering arms and related joints using approved free-play procedures.
4️⃣ SUSPENSION: Inspect springs, hangers, shackles/bushings, U-bolts/retention, shocks and axle-locating components as equipped.
5️⃣ KINGPIN / WHEEL END: Separate kingpin/knuckle movement from wheel-bearing movement using the correct unloaded inspection method.
6️⃣ VERIFY: Repair the proven source, torque/adjust by specification and recreate the original event.
WHAT MAKES IT CLUNK? → WITNESS MARKS → STEERING → SUSPENSION → KINGPIN / WHEEL END → PROVE → VERIFY.
⚠️ Never crawl under an unsupported vehicle. Steering and suspension fasteners are safety-critical; use current OEM torque and inspection specifications.
