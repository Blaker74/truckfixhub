🥛 COOLANT IN ENGINE OIL

Do not jump straight to a head gasket. Coolant and oil can meet through several engine components. Prove the leak path.

1️⃣ CONFIRM CONTAMINATION: Verify coolant is actually entering the oil using level history, appearance and appropriate fluid analysis/testing.
2️⃣ STOP DAMAGE: Coolant-contaminated oil can damage bearings and lubricated surfaces. Do not continue operating an engine with confirmed significant contamination.
3️⃣ PRESSURE TEST: Follow the exact OEM cooling-system pressure-test procedure and inspect for internal evidence with the engine secured as required.
4️⃣ OIL COOLER PATH: Where engine design allows oil/coolant interaction through a cooler or housing, test that assembly using the manufacturer procedure before deeper disassembly.
5️⃣ HEAD / GASKET / LINER PATH: If cooler-related paths are eliminated, move through the OEM internal-leak tree for cylinder head, gasket, liner/sealing areas and other engine-specific passages.
6️⃣ VERIFY: After repair, clean/service contaminated fluids as specified, pressure test again and monitor both coolant and oil levels.
CONFIRM → STOP OPERATION IF NEEDED → PRESSURE TEST → TEST ACCESSIBLE OIL/COOLANT INTERFACES → INTERNAL ENGINE TESTING → PROVE → REPAIR → VERIFY.
⚠️ Coolant in lubricating oil can cause rapid engine damage. Use current engine manufacturer procedures for pressure testing, fluid cleanup and internal leak diagnosis.
