🔩 SUSPECTED KINGPIN PLAY

Movement at a steer wheel does not automatically mean kingpins. First identify exactly which joint is moving.

1️⃣ SUPPORT CORRECTLY: Follow axle/vehicle manufacturer procedures for safely unloading and checking the steer axle components.
2️⃣ WATCH THE JOINT: Observe movement at the knuckle/kingpin area while the approved inspection force is applied. Do not diagnose only by how the tire feels in your hands.
3️⃣ SEPARATE WHEEL BEARING: Hub/bearing movement can imitate kingpin looseness. Use the manufacturer’s method to isolate the source.
4️⃣ CHECK LINKAGE: Tie-rod ends and other steering joints can add movement and may coexist with kingpin wear.
5️⃣ MEASURE AGAINST SPEC: Use the exact axle manufacturer’s inspection limits and measurement procedure. Do not use a generic universal play limit.
6️⃣ AFTER REPAIR: Verify steering movement, lubrication/service requirements and alignment as applicable.
SUPPORT SAFELY → WATCH ACTUAL JOINT → SEPARATE BEARING → CHECK LINKAGE → OEM LIMIT → REPAIR → VERIFY.
⚠️ Kingpin and steer-axle service is safety-critical. Use the axle manufacturer’s current specifications, tools and procedures.
