Fifth Wheels Made Simple

HOLLAND, JOST and Fontaine work differently inside, but the diagnostic approach is simple: identify what you have, inspect the coupling, find where the movement or failure is, then use the exact service procedure for that model.

SAF-HOLLAND

HOLLAND

Common names: FW35, FW31/FW33, FW17 and FWAL families.

Think of HOLLAND like this: identify the exact wheel first. FW35-style service information covers the lock, release handle, lubrication, adjustment, brackets and slider.

Important: the FW35 conventional wheel needs regular mechanism lubrication and cleaning. HOLLAND also has low/no-lube designs, so do not assume every HOLLAND wheel gets greased the same way.

JOST INTERNATIONAL

JOST

Common names: JSK37U, JSK37UW low-lube, JSK36N, JSK3CN and JSK37Y terminal-tractor families.

Think of JOST like this: its 37U family uses a three-point locking design. After coupling, physically look at the fifth wheel—do not rely only on the tug test or the sound of the lock.

Visual check: trailer seated with no gap, kingpin inside the lock and release handle in the correct locked position for that model.

Official JOST manuals →
FONTAINE

Fontaine

Common service families: No-Slack 6000/7000 and 5092 families plus current lightweight/heavy-duty applications.

Think of Fontaine like this: identify the No-Slack/top-plate family before choosing parts or adjusting the lock. Fontaine publishes separate coupling, maintenance, adjustment, rebuild and upper-coupler information.

Before coupling: inspect mounting, top-plate movement, lubrication/configuration and make sure the lock is actually open.

Will Not Lock / Couple

Start here:

  1. Check trailer height. A trailer too high can high-hook or miss the lock.
  2. Make sure the fifth wheel is open before backing under the trailer.
  3. Check kingpin and upper-coupler condition.
  4. Look for dried grease, dirt, ice, bent parts or a lock that cannot travel fully.
  5. After coupling, get out and visually verify the kingpin is actually captured.

Do not: keep slamming into the trailer hoping it locks.

🪝

Will Not Release

Start here:

  1. Remove bind between tractor and trailer according to the manufacturer coupling/uncoupling procedure.
  2. Check that the release handle/linkage can move through its full travel.
  3. Inspect for contamination, corrosion, damage or lack of lubrication where applicable.
  4. On air-release systems, separate an air/control problem from a mechanical lock problem.
  5. Use the exact model procedure before adjusting or disassembling the lock.

Do not: grind, torch or force safety-critical lock parts.

↔️

Too Much Slack / Clunk

Do not assume the fifth wheel needs adjustment.

  1. Inspect/gauge the trailer kingpin.
  2. Determine whether movement is in the lock around the kingpin.
  3. Check top-plate/bracket pins and bushings.
  4. Check slider mechanism and mounting.
  5. Only then use the model-specific lock inspection/adjustment procedure.

The loose feeling can come from the trailer, lock, brackets or slider—not just the adjustment mechanism.

🛤️

Slider Will Not Move

Break it into two questions: Are the pins/plungers releasing? Is the fifth wheel physically free to slide?

  1. Check air supply/control on air-operated sliders.
  2. Watch whether release cylinder/linkage moves.
  3. Verify plungers actually retract from the rack.
  4. Inspect rack/track for damage, dirt, ice, rust or binding.
  5. Check for bent or missing slider parts.

The top-plate lock and slider lock are two different systems.

🧴

Grease / Lubrication

First identify the design. A conventional lubricated fifth wheel and a low/no-lube fifth wheel are not serviced the same way.

For a conventional HOLLAND FW35, official literature calls for regular locking-mechanism lubrication and periodic cleaning/adjustment. Trailer-contact surfaces and specified mechanism points receive the prescribed lubricant.

JOST and Fontaine also publish model-specific maintenance procedures. Never cover low-lube pads/inserts with grease just because another fifth wheel uses grease.

🔧

Rebuild Kit / Parts

Get these before ordering:

  • Manufacturer: HOLLAND, JOST or Fontaine.
  • Exact model/family.
  • Serial or production identification when available.
  • Top plate only or complete fifth wheel.
  • Stationary or sliding.
  • Mount/bracket style.
  • Manual, air or other release style.
  • Standard-lube or low/no-lube configuration.

Rule: never choose a rebuild kit by appearance alone.

Quick Problem Finder

Trailer sits above plate

Stop. Recheck trailer height. An incorrect approach height can cause an improper/high coupling.

Handle looks locked

Still visually inspect underneath. JOST specifically warns that an improper coupling can pass a pull test.

Hard steering with trailer

Inspect fifth-wheel/trailer contact condition and correct lubrication or low-lube components before blaming steering parts.

Lock keeps getting tight

Check contamination, lubrication, kingpin condition and actual lock wear. Do not repeatedly readjust without finding the cause.

Air slider clicks but stays put

Watch the plungers. Air-cylinder movement does not prove both locks have fully disengaged from the rack.

Replacing the top plate

Verify compatibility, mounting/bracket application and installed height. Replacement top plates are not universal.

Easy memory rule

IDENTIFY → HEIGHT → KINGPIN → LOCK → RELEASE → BRACKET → SLIDER → LUBE/ADJUST → VERIFY.

If you follow that order, you are far less likely to adjust or replace the wrong part.

⚠️ Fifth Wheel Safety

A fifth wheel is the connection holding the tractor and trailer together. If it will not positively couple, lock, release or slide as designed, take it out of service until the problem is corrected. Always follow the current manufacturer procedure for the exact model when inspecting, adjusting, rebuilding or installing the fifth wheel.
