↔️ SEMI TRUCK PULLS TO ONE SIDE?

First determine whether it pulls all the time or only when the brakes are applied.

Quick answer

If a semi truck pulls left or right, separate a constant steering pull from a brake-application pull. A constant pull can involve tire pressure or construction, alignment, steering/suspension wear, ride height, axle geometry or road crown. A pull that appears mainly during braking moves brake balance, adjustment and component condition much higher on the list. Inspect the truck for unsafe tire, wheel, steering, suspension or brake conditions before road testing.

Start here — in this order

1️⃣ Decide when the truck pulls

Does it drift during steady cruising, only under braking, only under acceleration, or after hitting bumps? Do not mix those symptoms into one diagnosis.

2️⃣ Check steer tires first

Verify inflation and inspect tread wear, damage, mismatched tire conditions and other obvious differences side-to-side. Tire-related pull can imitate an alignment problem.

3️⃣ Inspect steering and suspension for looseness or damage

Check the applicable linkage, joints, bushings, springs, torque rods and mounting points using the manufacturer's inspection criteria. Correct unsafe looseness before alignment.

4️⃣ If it pulls while braking, inspect brake balance

Compare the left and right brake systems for mechanical condition, adjustment where applicable, contamination, dragging or weak braking and air-system concerns. Follow the brake manufacturer's procedures.

5️⃣ Verify ride height and axle/alignment condition

Ride-height or axle-position problems can influence alignment and handling. Measure rather than judging alignment from steering-wheel position alone.

6️⃣ Consider road crown and load before condemning parts

Road slope, uneven loading and trailer influence can affect tracking. Reproduce the complaint safely under comparable conditions before deciding the truck itself has a persistent pull.

What the symptom can tell you

🛑 Pulls only when braking

Brake imbalance becomes a primary path. Inspect both sides rather than replacing the component on the side the truck pulls toward without testing.

🛞 Pulls constantly on a level road

Check tires, alignment, steering/suspension condition, ride height and axle geometry. Confirm the road is not creating the apparent drift.

↔️ Pull changed after tire replacement

Recheck inflation, tire condition/matching and wheel installation before changing alignment settings or steering components.

🔧 Pull started after suspension/alignment work

Reinspect the work, measurements and fasteners first. Verify ride height and axle relationship according to the chassis/suspension procedure.

🔥 One wheel end is hotter

Investigate brake drag and wheel-end condition promptly. Temperature difference is a clue, not a complete diagnosis.

🚛 Pull changes with trailer attached

Trailer alignment, brakes, tires, suspension or load distribution can influence the tractor. Separate tractor-only behavior from combination-vehicle behavior.

Information to collect before buying parts

  • Truck year, make and model
  • Direction of pull and when it occurs
  • Steady cruise versus braking versus acceleration
  • Steer-tire pressures, sizes and wear patterns
  • Steering/suspension looseness or damage
  • Brake condition and side-to-side differences
  • Ride height and recent alignment measurements
  • Whether complaint changes with trailer/load
  • Recent tire, brake, steering or suspension work
🧠 Parts-cannon rule: a truck pulling right does not automatically mean a right-side part is bad. First separate tire, brake, alignment and chassis causes.
⚠️ Steering, brakes, tires and suspension are safety-critical. Do not road-test a truck with unsafe looseness, damaged tires/wheels, severe brake imbalance or other conditions that could cause loss of control.
