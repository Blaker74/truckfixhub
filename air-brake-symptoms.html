Locate the boundary

Air supply → storage → control/delivery → chamber actuation → foundation brake.

A truck that cannot build air is not diagnosed like a truck with normal supply pressure and one dragging wheel. Find where normal operation stops.

Several brakes affected

Investigate what those wheel ends share before treating each wheel as a separate failure. Common supply, delivery, control or parking-brake problems can affect several brakes.

One wheel affected

Move toward that branch: delivery, hose, chamber and foundation-brake hardware. Prove whether pressure is trapped or the brake is mechanically unable to release.

Evidence worth capturing

  • Which reservoir or gauge is losing pressure and under what condition.
  • Whether the complaint changes with service or parking brake commands.
  • Whether the compressor is producing air and whether the system retains it.
  • Where an audible leak occurs and whether it changes with a brake command.
  • Which wheel ends are hot, dragging, weak or failing to release.
  • Dryer purge behavior and abnormal moisture or contamination.
  • ABS warnings without assuming an ABS code explains a mechanical complaint.

Exact pressure settings, leakage limits, port functions, adjustment criteria and procedures vary by manufacturer, model and configuration. Verify current service information.

Brake-system safety

Brake and compressed-air faults can make a vehicle unsafe to move. Chock wheels and support the vehicle correctly. Stored air and spring-brake forces can cause serious injury. Never disassemble spring-brake chambers or bypass safety systems outside approved manufacturer procedures.
