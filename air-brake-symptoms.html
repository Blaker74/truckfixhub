Separate the air problem from the brake problem.

Air generation, storage, delivery, control and foundation brakes are connected—but they are not the same failure. Identify which section cannot perform its job before replacing components.

The diagnostic boundary matters

Air supply → storage → distribution/control → brake chamber actuation → foundation brake.

A truck that will not build air is not diagnosed the same way as a truck with adequate air pressure and one dragging wheel. Start by locating the boundary where normal operation stops.

Several brakes affected

When multiple wheel ends share the complaint, investigate what they share before treating every wheel as a separate failure. Common delivery, control, parking-brake or supply problems can affect more than one brake.

One wheel affected

A single hot, dragging or weak wheel end moves the investigation toward that branch and its foundation-brake hardware, chamber, hose, valve delivery and mechanical condition. Prove whether pressure is being trapped or the brake is mechanically unable to release.

Evidence worth capturing

  • Which reservoir or gauge is losing pressure and under what condition.
  • Whether the complaint occurs with service brakes applied, parking brakes released, trailer connected, or engine shut down.
  • Whether the compressor is producing air and whether system pressure is actually being retained.
  • Where an audible leak occurs and whether it changes with a brake command.
  • Which wheel ends are hot, dragging, weak or failing to release.
  • Air-dryer purge behavior and evidence of abnormal moisture or contamination.
  • ABS warnings or related faults without assuming the ABS code explains a mechanical brake complaint.

Exact pressure specifications, leakage limits, adjustment criteria and component procedures vary by vehicle and brake system. Verify them with current service information.

Brake-system safety

Brake and compressed-air faults can make a vehicle unsafe to move. Chock wheels, support the vehicle correctly and never rely on air suspension or a jack alone. Stored air and spring-brake forces can cause serious injury. Do not disassemble spring-brake chambers or bypass safety systems outside approved service procedures. Do not operate a vehicle with an unresolved unsafe brake condition.
