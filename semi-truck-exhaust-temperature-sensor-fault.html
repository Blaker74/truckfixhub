🌡️ EXHAUST TEMPERATURE SENSOR FAULT

Aftertreatment controls depend on believable temperature information. Diagnose sensor position, circuit and plausibility before replacing parts.

1️⃣ IDENTIFY THE SENSOR: Determine the exact EGT sensor position named by the fault. Multiple sensors can be used across DOC/DPF/SCR assemblies.
2️⃣ COLD-SOAK PLAUSIBILITY: Where the OEM procedure uses it, compare temperature readings after adequate cold soak. A large unexplained disagreement can help identify a suspect circuit or reading.
3️⃣ HARNESS / CONNECTOR: Inspect routing near hot exhaust, abrasion, melted insulation, terminal damage, corrosion and connector security.
4️⃣ ELECTRICAL TEST: Follow the exact manufacturer circuit/sensor test. Do not substitute generic resistance values or assumptions across different sensor designs.
5️⃣ SYSTEM EFFECT: Implausible EGT information can prevent or interrupt regeneration and can trigger related aftertreatment faults. Correct the measurement fault before chasing secondary symptoms.
6️⃣ VERIFY: Confirm the reading tracks plausibly through warm-up or the OEM verification procedure and that related faults stay cleared.
EXACT SENSOR → COLD PLAUSIBILITY → HARNESS → OEM CIRCUIT TEST → SYSTEM EFFECT → VERIFY.
⚠️ Exhaust sensors and housings can remain dangerously hot long after shutdown. Allow proper cooling before inspection or removal.
