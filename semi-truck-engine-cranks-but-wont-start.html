DIESEL NO-START · HEAVY-DUTY TRUCK TROUBLESHOOTING

Semi truck cranks but won't start

If the starter turns the engine but the diesel never starts, don't begin by throwing a fuel pump, crank sensor or ECM at it. First separate cranking speed, electronic control, engine-speed signal, fuel delivery, air and mechanical condition.

Quick answer: where should you start?

  • Cranks slowly: start with batteries, cables, connections, starter load and mechanical resistance.
  • Cranks normally but never fires: check ECM communication, active faults, engine-speed data and the engine-specific fuel-pressure path.
  • Mainly fails cold: compare cranking performance and temperature-related fuel/sensor conditions.
  • Starts and then dies: treat it as a different symptom; document how long it runs, faults and fuel/control clues.

The exact specifications vary by engine family, so use the OEM procedure before condemning a component.

Safety first

Secure the vehicle before testing. Keep clear of belts, fans and other moving components; some engine fans can engage automatically. Follow OEM procedures for fuel-system work because modern common-rail systems can operate at hazardous pressure. Do not use starting fluid unless the engine/vehicle manufacturer specifically permits it and the approved procedure is followed.

First question: is it really cranking normally?

A true “cranks but won't start” complaint means the engine is actually rotating at a reasonable cranking speed. Slow or labored cranking can point toward batteries, cables, connections, starter load or mechanical resistance. If it cranks slowly, use the semi-truck slow-crank and starter troubleshooting guide first.

What to record before replacing anything

  • Did the engine quit while driving, or fail to restart after shutdown?
  • Does it attempt to fire, or never catch at all?
  • Is cranking speed normal and consistent?
  • Are engine warning, security or immobilizer messages present?
  • Does the scan tool communicate with the engine controller?
  • Are active SPN/FMI or manufacturer-specific faults present?
  • Does the ECM report plausible engine speed while cranking?
  • Was there recent fuel-system, electrical, ECM, sensor or engine work?
  • Are fuel level and fuel condition known rather than assumed?

Diesel no-start diagnostic path

1. Verify electrical basics. Confirm adequate battery voltage, cable integrity and cranking speed under load. A starter can rotate while system voltage is still too poor for reliable control-module operation.

2. Verify ECM power and communication. Check whether the engine controller powers up and communicates. Loss of ECM power/ground, network communication or an enable condition can resemble a fuel problem.

3. Check engine-speed information while cranking. An implausible or missing crank/cam speed signal can prevent commanded fueling on some systems. Interpret sensor data and faults according to the exact engine's OEM procedure.

4. Evaluate fuel delivery correctly. Depending on the engine, diagnosis may include supply restriction, air intrusion, priming, supply-pump operation, filters, commanded versus actual rail pressure and injector/control conditions. Required pressures and tests vary substantially by engine family.

5. Consider air and mechanical condition. If electronic and fuel requirements are being met, diagnosis may move toward intake restriction, valve timing, compression or other mechanical causes using approved procedures.

Common mistake: parts swapping

A crank sensor, fuel pump, injector, wiring problem, ECM power issue and mechanical problem can all participate in a no-start complaint. That does not make any one of them the automatic answer. Test the condition that would condemn the component before buying the part.
