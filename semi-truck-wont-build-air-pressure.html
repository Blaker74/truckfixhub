SEMI TRUCK WON'T BUILD AIR PRESSURE

Use the symptom to narrow the system before replacing the compressor, governor, dryer or valves.

⚠️ Air brakes are safety-critical. Chock the vehicle, follow manufacturer procedures, and do not operate a truck that cannot build and maintain safe air pressure.

What the symptom tells you

A truck that will not build air pressure may have inadequate compressor output, a large system leak, an unloading/governor problem, an air-dryer or purge-valve fault, a restriction between the compressor and reservoirs, or a check-valve problem. Do not assume a slow or failed pressure build automatically means the compressor is bad.

Diagnostic order

  1. Confirm the complaint. Watch both reservoir gauges from low pressure and note whether neither circuit rises, one rises, or pressure climbs slowly and stalls.
  2. Listen for a major leak. A failed hose, fitting, chamber, valve, dryer purge valve or reservoir connection can dump compressor output faster than the system can recover.
  3. Check compressor drive and output. Verify the compressor is being driven and evaluate output using the correct engine/chassis procedure.
  4. Check governor and unloader operation. A compressor held in the unloaded state may produce little or no useful reservoir pressure.
  5. Inspect the air dryer. Continuous leakage at the purge area or an internal dryer fault can prevent normal pressure buildup.
  6. Check discharge plumbing and restrictions. Inspect the compressor discharge line, fittings, dryer inlet/outlet and related plumbing for damage, carbon restriction, freezing or kinks.
  7. Check reservoir/check-valve behavior. If one circuit behaves differently from the other, isolate the affected circuit and verify protection/check valves according to the chassis service information.
  8. Verify the repair. Drain pressure as specified, restart the truck and confirm normal build rate, governor cut-out/cut-in behavior and pressure retention.

Common causes

  • Large external air leak
  • Air dryer purge valve leaking continuously
  • Governor or compressor unloader malfunction
  • Weak/damaged compressor or compressor drive problem
  • Restricted or damaged compressor discharge line
  • Frozen moisture or contamination in cold weather
  • Faulty reservoir/check/protection valve
  • Air-system plumbing failure

Don't fire the parts cannon

Pressure gauges show the result, not the failed component. Determine where compressor output is going and whether the compressor is loaded before condemning expensive parts.
