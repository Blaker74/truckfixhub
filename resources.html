Start with the manufacturer.

General guides help frame the question. Current manufacturer information supplies the application-specific answer.

Gather your identifiers first

Have the full VIN available privately, plus the engine model and serial number, transmission or axle tag if relevant, and the exact fault information. A truck model year does not establish every component installed today. Use the identification checklist before choosing a catalog entry.

Official starting points

  • Cummins QuickServe OnlineEngine information tied to an engine serial number; access requirements vary.
  • DetroitDetroit engine, transmission, and axle resources. Follow current service and support links for the application.
  • BendixAir brake and related component information. Match the exact product before using service literature.
  • HendricksonSuspension identification, product information, and technical literature.
  • EatonProduct support and technical resources for the applicable drivetrain component.
  • DanaAxle and driveline product and support resources.

Choose the right document

A sales brochure explains a product; it is not an installation procedure. A cross-reference suggests a possible interchange; it does not prove fitment. A service bulletin may apply only within a production range. Check document revision, component identity, and applicability before treating a result as an instruction.

When a shop needs to take over

If testing requires equipment or training you do not have, gather the symptom history and identification details for a qualified provider. Ask what inspection or measurement supports the proposed repair, which parts and labor are included, and how the original complaint will be checked afterward.

How to evaluate a repair provider

Independent links for reference; no manufacturer affiliation or endorsement is implied. Some resources require registration or payment to the provider.
