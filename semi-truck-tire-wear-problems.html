TRUCKFIXHUB TIRE & ALIGNMENT GUIDE

Semi Truck Tires Wearing Unevenly?

Uneven steer, drive or trailer tire wear can be a clue to inflation, alignment, suspension, wheel, axle or operating conditions. The wear pattern matters, but it should be combined with a mechanical inspection rather than used as a one-step diagnosis.

Damaged tires require immediate attention

If a tire has exposed cords, a bulge, separation, rapid pressure loss, severe damage or another condition that makes continued operation unsafe, stop in a safe location and follow the tire and vehicle manufacturer's service procedures. Do not rely on a wear-pattern guide to determine whether a damaged tire is safe to run.

What wear pattern are you seeing?

  • One shoulder wearing faster than the other
  • Both shoulders wearing
  • Center of tread wearing faster
  • Cupping, scalloping or irregular patches
  • Feathered tread edges
  • Diagonal or repeating wear pattern
  • One tire wearing much faster than its mate
  • Rapid wear that returned after alignment or tire replacement

Common areas technicians investigate

  • Cold inflation pressure and pressure maintenance
  • Tire size, load range, application and matched duals where required
  • Steer-axle and vehicle alignment
  • Drive and trailer axle alignment or tracking
  • Steering linkage and suspension wear
  • Ride height and suspension condition where applicable
  • Wheel/tire balance and runout when vibration is present
  • Shock absorbers or damping-related concerns when supported by inspection
  • Wheel bearings, hubs and wheel-end condition when symptoms indicate
  • Load distribution and operating conditions

Steer tire wear plus truck pulling

If uneven steer-tire wear appears together with a truck that consistently pulls left or right, record which tire is wearing and the direction of the pull. Tire condition, inflation, alignment and steering/suspension inspection should be considered together.

Tire wear plus steering-wheel vibration

Irregular tire wear can accompany a vibration complaint, but vibration can also involve wheel, hub, front-end or driveline conditions. Record the road-speed range where the shake is strongest and whether it changes during braking.

Tire wear plus wheel-end noise, heat or looseness

If abnormal tire wear appears with grinding or growling noise, lubricant leakage, unusual wheel-end heat, looseness or abnormal wheel movement, the complaint needs more than an alignment check. Bearing, hub, brake and wheel-end conditions may need qualified inspection.

Wear returned after an alignment

An alignment reading alone cannot correct worn, loose or damaged mechanical components. If abnormal wear returns quickly, technicians may need to verify tire condition and inflation, steering/suspension integrity, wheel-end condition, axle tracking, ride height and other application-specific factors before repeating alignment work.

What to record for the tire shop

  • Axle and wheel position
  • Tire brand/model, size and approximate mileage
  • Cold inflation pressure history if known
  • Exact wear pattern and which edge is affected
  • Whether the truck pulls or vibrates
  • Loaded versus empty behavior
  • Recent tire, alignment, suspension or wheel-end work

Wear patterns are clues, not final diagnoses

TruckFixHub provides educational troubleshooting information. Tire wear can have overlapping causes, and steering, suspension, tires and wheel ends are safety-critical. Use qualified heavy-duty inspection and the applicable tire, axle and vehicle manufacturer procedures.
