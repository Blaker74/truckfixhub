Class 8 Truck Emissions Problems: DPF Regen, DEF Warnings and Engine Derate

A regen request, a DEF warning and reduced engine power describe different things. Start with the exact message, then match it to your truck’s manual and diagnostic results.

TruckFixHub editorial overview · Sources reviewed October 11, 2026

Follow your vehicle’s warning instructions. If the truck displays a STOP warning, follow its stopping and shutdown directions. This overview does not authorize continued driving or a parked regen. Use the applicable manual for your engine, model year and equipment.

What DPF, DEF and SCR mean

Manufacturer explanation

Two different jobs in the exhaust system

The diesel particulate filter (DPF) captures soot. Selective catalytic reduction (SCR) uses diesel exhaust fluid (DEF) to reduce nitrogen oxides (NOx). The Western Star 57X manual describes exhaust passing through the diesel oxidation catalyst (DOC) and DPF before DEF is introduced for SCR.

That distinction matters when describing a problem: “emissions trouble” does not identify which part of the system needs attention.

DPF regeneration: soot removal is not ash cleaning

Cummins technical guidance

Why a regen does not make a filter maintenance-free

Cummins explains that regeneration uses heat to oxidize soot collected in the DPF. Remaining ash is removed during scheduled cleaning, according to the engine manufacturer’s recommendations. Regeneration frequency and cleaning needs vary with the system, application and duty cycle.

For a truck that keeps requesting regens, record how often they occur and whether they finish. Ask the technician to compare that history with the applicable diagnostic procedure and maintenance schedule. This overview cannot establish whether a particular filter is restricted or due for cleaning.

DEF contamination: fluid quality can affect hardware

Cummins technical guidance

A full DEF tank does not establish that the fluid is suitable

Cummins documents three concerns: particles, chemical contamination and incorrect urea concentration. Particles can damage DEF pumps and dosers; chemicals can damage the SCR catalyst. Improper storage or handling can change concentration and reduce system performance.

Cummins says chemical contamination cannot be removed and contaminated fluid must be replaced. Its guidance also calls for DEF-compatible handling materials. This is a documented failure mechanism, not proof that contamination caused your warning.

Record where and when the DEF was added and whether the warning followed a refill. Have the fluid and system evaluated using the manufacturer’s procedures rather than assuming a pump or sensor needs replacement.

Engine derate: what the 57X manual documents

Model-specific driver guidance

Low DEF and contamination have different warning paths

The Western Star 57X manual says low DEF produces a warning and directs the driver to refill. An empty tank can lead to progressively reduced power. If ignored, the documented sequence can apply a 5 mph speed limit.

The same manual says detected DEF contamination or aftertreatment tampering requires an authorized service facility to check for SCR damage and deactivate warnings and engine limits. Simply topping off the tank is not the documented correction for that condition.

These details belong to the cited 57X manual. Do not assume identical warning timing, reset conditions or speed limits across all Class 8 trucks. Match the displayed message and full fault codes to your vehicle’s documentation.

Before calling the shop or ordering parts

  • Identify the truck: make, model, year, engine model, engine serial number and mileage.
  • Capture the warning: a photo of the display and complete fault codes, including SPN/FMI when available.
  • Describe the sequence: what happened first, driving conditions, DEF level and any recent refill.
  • Bring the history: regen attempts, completion results, DPF cleaning records and recent repairs.
  • Ask for evidence: the diagnostic finding, supporting test results and the verified result after repair.

This checklist is TruckFixHub’s editorial recommendation. A warning identifies a condition to investigate; it does not, by itself, establish a parts order.

Scope of this overview
