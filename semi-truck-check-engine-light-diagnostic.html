CHECK ENGINE LIGHT

Don't start with a parts catalog. Start with evidence.

What the light actually tells you

A check-engine or engine warning light means the truck's electronic control system detected a condition that deserves attention. The light alone does not identify the failed part. Your first job is to capture the fault information and the operating conditions surrounding it.

🔎 Start here

1️⃣ RECORD THE FAULT — Save the complete SPN/FMI or manufacturer-specific code before clearing anything. Note whether it is active or inactive and how many times it occurred.
2️⃣ RECORD THE CONDITIONS — Was the engine cold or hot? Under load? During regen? At idle? Did power drop? Did another warning light appear?
3️⃣ INSPECT BEFORE REPLACING — Check battery voltage, grounds, connectors, harness routing, fluid levels, obvious leaks, loose plumbing and anything recently repaired or disturbed.

🧠 SPN and FMI in plain English

SPN identifies the parameter or area the electronic system is reporting. FMI describes the type of condition detected, such as voltage, current, data or value being outside an expected range. Together they narrow the diagnostic path—but they still do not automatically prove that the named sensor or component has failed.

🚫 Avoid the parts cannon

A sensor-related fault can be caused by the sensor, but it can also be caused by damaged wiring, poor terminal tension, bad power or ground, contamination, plumbing problems, another system fault or an operating condition that pushed the reading outside its expected range. Test the circuit and system before condemning the component.

After the repair

Return the truck to the conditions that originally produced the complaint when it is safe to do so. Confirm the fault does not return, verify related readings are normal and document what actually corrected the problem.
