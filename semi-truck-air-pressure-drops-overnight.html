Semi Truck Loses Air Pressure Overnight?

A parked truck that is low on air by morning gives useful clues about leakage, but the amount and source must be diagnosed rather than guessed.

Do not ignore air loss

Air-brake system leakage can affect safe operation. Required leakage, warning and pressure checks should be performed according to applicable procedures before the vehicle is returned to service.

Useful clues to record

  • Pressure when parked and pressure at restart
  • Primary versus secondary gauge change
  • Whether a trailer was connected
  • Whether parking brakes were applied
  • Audible leaks after shutdown
  • Recent valve, chamber, dryer or line work

Why parked observations help

Comparing circuits and configurations can help a qualified technician narrow the search. Leakage may occur at fittings, lines, valves, chambers, reservoirs or other pneumatic components depending on the vehicle. A quiet overnight loss does not identify the failed component by itself.

Trailer connected versus disconnected

If the pattern changes with a trailer connected, that is useful diagnostic information. Do not assume the trailer is the cause until tractor and trailer circuits are tested correctly.
