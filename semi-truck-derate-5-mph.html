AFTERTREATMENT · DERATE TROUBLESHOOTING

Semi truck stuck in a 5 MPH derate?

A severe derate is usually the result of an unresolved condition, not a diagnosis by itself. Start with the warning sequence and complete fault-code set before replacing DEF, NOx, DPF or SCR components.

Quick answer: what should you check first?

  1. Record every active and inactive fault code before clearing anything.
  2. Write down the warning sequence. DEF, check-engine, stop-engine, regen and inducement messages can help establish what happened first.
  3. Confirm the engine and aftertreatment system. Procedures differ by manufacturer and calibration.
  4. Check the evidence behind the codes using OEM diagnostic procedures rather than assuming the component named in a fault description is bad.

The 5 MPH restriction tells you the truck has reached a severe inducement state. It does not tell you which part failed.

Did a DEF or emissions warning come on first?

If a DEF or emissions warning appeared before the derate, record when it started, what the dash displayed, DEF level and every active SPN/FMI code. Depending on the engine, vehicle and calibration, the underlying issue may involve DEF level or quality, dosing or supply, sensors, wiring, SCR performance or another aftertreatment condition.

Information worth collecting before buying parts

  • All active and inactive SPN/FMI fault codes.
  • Engine make, model and calibration information.
  • DEF level and any DEF-quality, pressure, temperature or dosing faults.
  • DPF soot/load information and recent regeneration history.
  • SCR efficiency and inlet/outlet NOx related faults.
  • Exhaust temperature or sensor faults.
  • The dash-warning sequence that occurred before the road-speed restriction.
  • Recent aftertreatment, wiring, sensor or exhaust-system repairs.

Will clearing the codes remove the derate?

Do not treat clearing codes as a repair. Depending on the system, the underlying condition may remain active, the fault may immediately return, or specific OEM verification and recovery steps may be required after the actual problem is corrected. Diagnose and repair the cause first.

Will a forced regen fix a 5 MPH derate?

Not necessarily. A regeneration addresses certain DPF loading conditions; it does not repair every DEF, SCR, NOx sensor, wiring, dosing or engine fault that can contribute to an inducement. Follow the fault-specific OEM procedure before attempting a regeneration.

Don't diagnose from the derate symptom alone

Replacing a NOx sensor, changing DEF, forcing a regeneration or replacing another aftertreatment component simply because the truck is derated can waste money and leave the original fault untouched. Confirm the failed condition before condemning the part.

Safety and emissions notice

Do not bypass emissions controls or clear faults simply to defeat a derate. Repair the underlying fault using OEM diagnostic procedures. If the vehicle cannot be operated safely or legally, arrange qualified service or towing.

Keep the repair organized

Save the truck, fault codes, complaint and repair outcome together so the next problem starts with history instead of guesswork.
