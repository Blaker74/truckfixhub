TRUCKFIXHUB TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE

Semi Truck in a 5 MPH Derate?

Severe emissions inducement can follow an unresolved aftertreatment problem. The warning sequence and complete fault-code set are often more useful than the final road-speed restriction by itself.

Did the DEF warning come on first?

If a DEF or emissions warning appeared before the derate, record when it started, what the dash displayed, DEF level, and every active SPN/FMI code. Depending on the engine, vehicle and calibration, the underlying issue may involve DEF level or quality, dosing or supply, sensors, wiring, SCR performance or another aftertreatment condition.

Collect this before replacing parts

  • All active and inactive SPN/FMI fault codes.
  • Engine make, model and calibration information.
  • DEF level and any DEF-quality or dosing faults.
  • DPF soot load and recent regeneration history.
  • SCR efficiency and inlet/outlet NOx related faults.
  • Any warning sequence that occurred before the speed restriction.

Common diagnostic areas

Depending on the OEM and fault set, diagnosis may involve DEF quality and delivery, DEF dosing, SCR efficiency, NOx sensing, DPF loading, exhaust temperature or other engine/aftertreatment faults. The active SPN/FMI set determines the correct diagnostic path.

Do not diagnose from the 5 MPH symptom alone

The final speed restriction does not identify the failed component. Read the complete code set and follow the engine manufacturer's diagnostic sequence. Clearing faults, replacing a NOx sensor, changing DEF or forcing a regeneration without evidence may not correct the condition.

Important

Do not bypass emissions controls or clear faults simply to defeat a derate. Repair the underlying fault using OEM diagnostic procedures. If the vehicle cannot be operated safely or legally, arrange qualified service or towing.
