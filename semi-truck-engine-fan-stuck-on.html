TRUCKFIXHUB COOLING SYSTEM GUIDE

Semi Truck Engine Fan Stays On?

A cooling fan that stays engaged can be responding to a legitimate temperature or pressure demand, a control-system command, or a fault in the fan clutch, wiring, air supply or related sensors. Confirm why the fan is being commanded before replacing parts.

Common symptoms

  • Fan roars continuously after the engine warms up
  • Fan engages soon after startup and does not release
  • Fan cycles much more often than normal
  • Fuel economy drops after the fan begins running constantly
  • Fan remains engaged even when coolant temperature appears normal
  • Fan behavior changes when the A/C is turned on or off
  • Check-engine or temperature-related fault codes are present

Why an engine fan may stay engaged

  • High engine coolant temperature or a cooling-system problem
  • High A/C refrigerant pressure or an A/C pressure input requesting fan operation
  • Fan clutch control solenoid, valve or air-supply problem on applicable systems
  • Electrical wiring, connector, relay or power/ground fault
  • Coolant-temperature or other sensor input that is incorrect or implausible
  • ECM command caused by an active fault or operating condition
  • Fan clutch mechanical problem

The exact strategy varies by engine, chassis and fan-clutch design, so the commanded state and fault information matter.

First question: is the ECM commanding the fan on?

A diagnostic scan can help separate a commanded fan condition from a mechanical or control problem. If the ECM is requesting fan operation, investigate the reason for that command before assuming the clutch itself has failed. Record coolant temperature, relevant A/C pressure information and active or inactive fault codes when available.

Fan stays on but engine is not overheating

If displayed coolant temperature remains normal, technicians may investigate other fan-request inputs and the fan control circuit. A normal dash gauge by itself does not prove every temperature, pressure or control input is correct.

Fan clutch and control system

Heavy-duty trucks use different fan-clutch and control arrangements. Depending on the application, inspection may include air supply, control solenoids, electrical commands, connectors, clutch condition and manufacturer-specific tests. Avoid replacing the fan clutch solely because the fan is engaged.

Problem started after wiring or cooling-system work

Record what was recently serviced. Disturbed connectors, damaged wiring, sensor issues or changes around the cooling and A/C systems can provide useful diagnostic clues.

Keep clear of the fan

An engine cooling fan can engage unexpectedly. Keep hands, clothing, tools and loose objects away from the fan and belts whenever the engine can run. Do not attempt hands-on fan diagnosis with the engine operating unless following the proper manufacturer procedure with appropriate training and safeguards.
