⚙️ ENGINE PARTS — SEE IT & NAME IT

Recognizable shapes for common heavy-duty diesel engine components.

Exact appearance and location vary by engine family. Use the truck/engine serial information and current OEM service information before ordering or replacing a component.

Turbocharger

Look for: Snail-shaped compressor/turbine housings with large intake and exhaust connections.

Job: Uses exhaust energy to compress intake air.

SEE TURBO SYSTEM →

Fuel Injector

Look for: Long precision component entering the cylinder head with fuel/electrical connection at the top.

Job: Delivers precisely controlled fuel.

SEE FUEL SYSTEM →

Alternator

Look for: Vented round aluminum housing driven by a belt, with a heavy output cable.

Job: Supplies electrical power and maintains battery charge.

SEE CHARGING →

Starter Motor

Look for: Large cylindrical motor near the flywheel housing with a smaller solenoid attached.

Job: Turns the engine during cranking.

SEE STARTING →

Water Pump

Look for: Cast pump housing at the front/side of engine, often driven by gear or belt with coolant passages attached.

Job: Circulates coolant through engine and cooling system.

SEE COOLING SYSTEM →

Thermostat

Look for: Small round temperature valve hidden inside a coolant outlet/thermostat housing.

Job: Regulates coolant flow based on temperature.

SEE COOLING SYSTEM →

Charge Air Cooler

Look for: Radiator-like heat exchanger with large boost tubes, normally in front of the radiator.

Job: Cools compressed turbo air before it enters the engine.

SEE CHARGE AIR →

EGR Valve

Look for: Heavy metal valve body in EGR plumbing, often with an actuator mounted on it.

Job: Controls recirculated exhaust flow.

SEE EMISSIONS →

EGR Cooler

Look for: Metal heat-exchanger body connected to EGR exhaust piping and engine coolant lines.

Job: Removes heat from recirculated exhaust gas.

SEE EGR SYSTEM →

Fuel Pump

Look for: Engine-mounted pump body with fuel inlet/outlet connections; exact design varies greatly by engine.

Job: Supplies fuel at the pressure/flow required by the engine fuel system.

SEE FUEL SYSTEM →
