If one step moves differently from the opposite side, you have found a useful clue.

Drum brake path

On an S-cam drum-brake application, chamber movement travels through the pushrod and slack-adjuster/cam mechanism so the shoes contact the drum. Compare movement, travel, return, friction condition and hardware with the opposite side. Exact design and inspection limits depend on the installed brake.

Disc brake path

On an air-disc application, the chamber actuates the caliper mechanism so the pads clamp the rotor. Diagnose the installed system as a disc brake rather than applying drum-brake assumptions to it.

🔧 Problem started after brake work

Inspect what was disturbed first: correct component application, installation, hardware, routing, adjustment where applicable, free movement and side-to-side differences. A new part does not prove the installation is correct.

Do not confuse air-side and wheel-side faults

If correct air reaches the actuator but the wheel-end response is wrong, move your attention toward the mechanical/foundation-brake path. If the actuator never receives the expected air command, move back toward hoses, valves and controls.

Air side or wheel side? Make that split first. Then follow the movement one step at a time.

🛑 SAFETY: Foundation brakes and spring-brake actuators are safety-critical. Chock and secure the vehicle before inspection. Never disassemble a spring brake chamber. Keep clear of moving pushrods, slack adjusters and brake mechanisms during application. Verify exact stroke, wear, adjustment and service limits for the installed components and current requirements.
