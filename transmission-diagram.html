👇 Follow the complaint

🚫 WON’T GO INTO GEAR: Identify transmission design, save faults, verify clutch/input release and required control conditions before internal diagnosis.
💥 HARD SHIFT: Record the exact transition, load and temperature. Separate clutch/input behavior, control strategy and driveline lash from internal transmission problems.
↕️ MISSED SHIFT: Requested versus actual gear, speed information, clutch/torque interruption and actuator operation can reveal where the shift sequence failed.
🔒 STUCK IN GEAR: Secure the vehicle first. Determine whether input torque is trapping the gear, command/actuation is wrong, or mechanical binding is proven.
💧 FLUID LEAK: Clean and trace the highest fresh source. Check correct level procedure, venting and the reason a seal/interface failed.
🔊 NOISE: Decide whether it follows engine RPM, road speed, one gear, acceleration or coast before opening the case.

💧 TRANSMISSION FLUID LEAK

Fluid travels. The wettest area is not always the original leak source.

1️⃣ IDENTIFY THE FLUID: Confirm the leaking fluid and the correct transmission type before diagnosis.
2️⃣ CLEAN + TRACE: Clean the area safely and locate the highest/earliest point where fresh fluid appears.
3️⃣ CHECK LEVEL CORRECTLY: Fluid-level procedures vary by transmission and temperature. Use the exact manufacturer method.
4️⃣ INSPECT EXTERNAL PATHS: Check cooler lines/fittings where equipped, covers, plugs, sensors/switches, PTO interfaces, housings and other service points applicable to the unit.
5️⃣ SEALS + VENTING: A seal leak may be influenced by shaft condition, movement or venting. Do not replace a seal without checking why it failed.
6️⃣ VERIFY: After repair and correct fluid service, operate to the original temperature/load condition and reinspect.
IDENTIFY FLUID → CLEAN → TRACE SOURCE → LEVEL / VENT → INTERFACE / SEAL CAUSE → REPAIR → VERIFY.
⚠️ Low transmission fluid can cause damage. Do not continue operation when the leak or fluid level makes the vehicle unsafe.

↕️ TRANSMISSION MISSES A SHIFT

A missed shift is valuable evidence. Record exactly which shift fails and what the controller was trying to do.

1️⃣ WHICH SHIFT? Record starting gear, requested gear, load, throttle, road speed, temperature and whether the failure repeats.
2️⃣ SAVE CODES: Preserve active/inactive transmission, engine and network faults plus event data before key cycles or clearing.
3️⃣ SPEED INFORMATION: Electronically controlled shifting depends on believable input/output/engine speed information. Follow OEM plausibility and circuit tests.
4️⃣ CLUTCH / TORQUE INTERRUPTION: On automated manual systems, verify clutch/actuator operation and required torque-management inputs rather than assuming an internal gear problem.
5️⃣ ACTUATION: Test the exact shift actuator, air supply or electrical control path used by the transmission design.
6️⃣ INTERNAL ONLY WHEN PROVEN: If commands, speeds, clutch and actuation are correct but engagement fails, continue with manufacturer internal diagnostics.
EXACT SHIFT → CODES / DATA → SPEED INPUTS → CLUTCH / TORQUE CONTROL → ACTUATION → INTERNAL IF PROVEN → VERIFY.
⚠️ Do not road-test an unsafe shifting condition in traffic. Follow OEM stationary and road-test procedures.

🔊 TRANSMISSION NOISE

Before opening the transmission, determine whether the noise follows engine speed, a particular gear, road speed, load or coast.

1️⃣ DESCRIBE IT: Whine, growl, rattle, grind or knock? Record when it starts and stops.
2️⃣ ENGINE RPM VS ROAD SPEED: This first split helps separate engine/clutch/input-related noise from output/driveline/wheel-speed sources.
3️⃣ GEAR DEPENDENCE: If the noise changes dramatically in one gear or ratio, document that evidence before disassembly.
4️⃣ LOAD VS COAST: A noise that changes between pull, float and coast can help isolate torque-sensitive components in the transmission or driveline.
5️⃣ CHECK OUTSIDE THE CASE: Inspect clutch/input system, mounts, driveshaft, U-joints, center bearing, axle/differential and wheel ends as appropriate.
6️⃣ LUBRICANT / INTERNAL: Verify correct lubricant level/condition by OEM procedure. Proceed to internal diagnosis only after external sources are separated.
NOISE TYPE → RPM OR ROAD SPEED → GEAR → LOAD / COAST → EXTERNAL DRIVELINE → INTERNAL IF PROVEN.
⚠️ Never work near a rotating driveshaft or unsupported vehicle. Use approved diagnostic and lifting procedures.

💥 TRANSMISSION SHIFTS HARD

Harsh shifting can come from input/clutch behavior, control strategy, transmission operation or driveline lash.

1️⃣ IDENTIFY THE TRANSMISSION: Manual, automated manual and automatic systems use different controls and diagnostic procedures.
2️⃣ DEFINE THE SHIFT: Which gear or transition? Upshift or downshift? Hot or cold? Loaded or empty? Every shift or one specific event?
3️⃣ SAVE FAULTS + DATA: On electronically controlled units, record codes and requested/actual gear plus relevant clutch, speed and actuator data before clearing.
4️⃣ INPUT / CLUTCH: On clutch-equipped systems, verify clutch release, wear/adjustment or actuator operation using the exact manufacturer procedure.
5️⃣ CONTROL / FLUID / AIR: Depending on design, verify power, grounds, network inputs, air supply, fluid level/condition and control functions before internal teardown.
6️⃣ DRIVELINE: Excessive driveline lash or mount problems can make an otherwise normal torque transition feel harsh.
7️⃣ VERIFY: Repeat the exact gear, load and temperature condition after the proven repair.
IDENTIFY UNIT → EXACT SHIFT → DATA → INPUT / CLUTCH → CONTROL → INTERNAL ONLY IF PROVEN → DRIVELINE → VERIFY.
⚠️ Transmission calibration, clutch learning and fluid procedures are application-specific. Follow current OEM service information.

🔒 TRANSMISSION STUCK IN GEAR

First determine whether the transmission is mechanically trapped, still loaded by the clutch/input, or commanded/actuated incorrectly.

1️⃣ SECURE THE VEHICLE: Treat unexpected movement as a serious hazard. Chock/support and follow OEM recovery procedures.
2️⃣ IDENTIFY DESIGN: Manual, automated manual and automatic systems require different release and diagnostic paths.
3️⃣ INPUT LOAD: On clutch-equipped systems, determine whether the clutch is fully releasing. Driveline torque can prevent a gear from disengaging.
4️⃣ REQUESTED VS ACTUAL: On controlled transmissions, record the requested gear, actual gear, fault codes and actuator status before disturbing the system.
5️⃣ CONTROL / ACTUATION: Verify power, grounds, network, air supply and shift actuator operation as equipped.
6️⃣ MECHANICAL: If input release and command/actuation are proven, follow OEM mechanical inspection for binding or internal damage.
SECURE → IDENTIFY → REMOVE INPUT LOAD → REQUEST VS ACTUAL → CONTROL / ACTUATOR → MECHANICAL IF PROVEN.
⚠️ A truck stuck in gear can move unexpectedly. Do not work beneath or around an unsecured vehicle.
