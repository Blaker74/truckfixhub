👇 PICK THE COMPLAINT

🚫 It will not go into gear / engage

  1. Identify the transmission type: manual, automated manual or automatic.
  2. Check active transmission and engine faults before replacing anything.
  3. Verify the clutch releases correctly or the converter/input conditions are correct for that transmission.
  4. Check required electrical, air and control inputs and confirm the requested gear is actually being commanded.
  5. If input and controls are proven, continue with the manufacturer’s internal transmission diagnostic path.

↕️ It shifts rough, late, or skips a gear

  1. Write down the exact shift or gear where the complaint happens.
  2. Note whether it changes with load, temperature, road speed or throttle.
  3. Read active and inactive faults and review relevant sensor / control information.
  4. Verify fluid or lubricant level and condition where applicable using the correct procedure.
  5. Separate a control/input problem from an internal mechanical problem before major disassembly.

🔊 It makes noise while driving

  1. Determine whether the noise follows engine RPM or road speed.
  2. Check whether it appears in one gear, every gear, acceleration, coast or neutral.
  3. Inspect clutch/input, transmission mounting and lubricant condition as applicable.
  4. If the noise follows road speed beyond the transmission, continue into drivetrain diagnosis.
  5. Do not condemn the transmission until nearby driveline, axle and wheel-end sources are separated.

Not every “transmission problem” starts inside the transmission

Heavy-duty trucks may use manual, automated manual or automatic transmissions, so the exact components and tests vary. A complaint can begin before the transmission at the clutch or converter, in shift controls and power/air supply, inside the unit, or after it in the driveline. Identify the section first, then use the correct manufacturer procedure for that transmission.
