⚙️ TRANSMISSION

First decide: is the problem getting power IN, changing gears, or sending power OUT?

1️⃣ ENGINE / CLUTCH OR TORQUE CONVERTER
Transfers engine torque into the transmission
↓
2️⃣ CONTROLS / SHIFT COMMAND
Driver input plus electronic, pneumatic or mechanical controls request and manage shifts as equipped
↓
3️⃣ TRANSMISSION
Internal components provide the selected gear ratio
↓
4️⃣ OUTPUT
Transfers transmission torque to the driveline
↓
5️⃣ DRIVESHAFT → DRIVE AXLE
Carries torque toward the drive wheels
🚫 Won’t go into gear?
Separate an input/release problem from a control problem. Check clutch or converter conditions, air/electrical supply, shift request and active faults before assuming internal failure.
↕️ Shifts badly?
Record the exact shift, gear, load, temperature and operating condition. Check controls, sensors, fluid/lubricant where applicable and fault information using the manufacturer procedure.
🔊 Noise?
Ask when it occurs: engine RPM, a specific gear, road speed, acceleration or coast. Noise timing helps separate transmission from clutch, driveline, axle and wheel-end sources.
💧 Fluid / oil leak?
Clean the area and locate the highest true wet point. Verify the correct fluid/lubricant level and vent condition before assuming a seal or gasket is the only cause.

Not every “transmission problem” starts inside the transmission

Heavy-duty trucks may use manual, automated manual or automatic transmissions, so the exact components and tests vary. A complaint can begin before the transmission at the clutch or converter, in shift controls and power/air supply, inside the unit, or after it in the driveline. Identify the section first, then use the correct manufacturer procedure for that transmission.
