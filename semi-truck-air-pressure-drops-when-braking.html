TRUCKFIXHUB AIR-BRAKE GUIDE

Air Pressure Drops When You Press the Brakes?

Some air use during a brake application is expected. A large, rapid or continuing pressure drop can indicate an air-system or brake-system problem that needs inspection before the truck is operated.

Do not ignore excessive air loss

If system pressure falls toward the low-air warning range, the warning activates, or the truck cannot maintain adequate pressure, stop and have the air-brake system inspected. Do not use an internet guide to declare an unsafe brake system roadworthy.

Useful clues

  • Pressure drops once and stabilizes: distinguish expected brake application air use from an abnormal loss.
  • Pressure keeps falling while holding the pedal: listen for a continuing leak in components or plumbing pressurized during the brake application.
  • One gauge drops differently: record which circuit is affected.
  • Leak sound near a wheel: inspect the chamber, hoses, fittings and related plumbing using proper brake-service procedures.
  • Problem changes with trailer connected: include tractor/trailer control and supply circuits in the diagnosis.

Hear air leaking when the brakes are applied?

The timing of the leak matters. A leak that begins or becomes much louder only during service-brake application points the diagnosis toward components that are being pressurized at that moment.

Air leaking near a brake chamber?

Do not assume every wheel-area air leak is the chamber itself. Positively locate the leak and inspect the chamber, hose, fittings and nearby brake plumbing.

Information worth recording

  • Primary and secondary pressure before applying brakes
  • How far each gauge drops
  • Whether pressure stabilizes or continues falling
  • Whether you can hear an air leak and where
  • Whether the trailer is connected
  • Whether the low-air warning activates
