Finding the right heavy-duty truck repair shop

Not every shop works on every truck, engine, trailer, emissions system or breakdown type. Before authorizing a tow or service call, use this guide to confirm the shop can actually perform the work.

Important: TruckFixHub does not currently maintain a verified, live directory of shop locations, hours or availability. This page provides a selection checklist rather than unverified business listings.

Before you call

  1. Record your exact location, direction of travel and a safe access point. If stopped on a highway, follow roadside safety procedures and contact emergency services when needed.
  2. Gather the VIN, truck year and model, engine make and model, and any relevant fault codes.
  3. Describe the actual symptom: no crank, crank-no-start, air pressure loss, derate, overheating, brake concern or trailer problem. Note when it began.
  4. Explain whether the vehicle is drivable, towing is required, or a mobile service visit might be appropriate. Do not drive a vehicle with an unresolved safety-critical defect.

Six questions to ask the shop

  1. Can you service this specific truck and engine? Ask about the manufacturer, model, engine family and system involved.
  2. Do you have the required diagnostic equipment? Some faults require OEM software, test tools or calibration access.
  3. Can you handle the repair in-house? Confirm capabilities for electrical, emissions, air brakes, wheel ends, transmission or trailer work as applicable.
  4. When can you inspect it? Distinguish the earliest inspection time from the estimated repair completion time.
  5. How are diagnosis and authorization billed? Ask about callout, towing, diagnostic, storage and after-hours fees before committing.
  6. How will you communicate findings? Request the confirmed fault, proposed repair, parts availability, estimate and approval process in writing.

Red flags

Be cautious if a provider promises a repair without examining the vehicle, cannot explain the proposed diagnostic process, refuses to discuss fees, or recommends replacing expensive components solely because a fault code mentions them. A code identifies a system or condition; it does not always identify a failed part.

Where to search

Search for the relevant vehicle or engine manufacturer's authorized service locator, or use a current map service to find local heavy-duty diesel repair providers. Confirm the business's phone number, capabilities, operating hours and availability directly before traveling or dispatching a tow. TruckFixHub does not endorse or verify individual businesses listed by third-party services.

Safety: Do not crawl under an unsupported truck, release stored air pressure, or work on hot or energized systems without proper training and OEM procedures. Roadside safety and safe towing come before cost or convenience.

Keep a useful record

Save the shop name, contact, date, fault description, authorization, diagnosis, replaced parts and final invoice. These records can help with repeat faults, warranty claims and future parts identification.
