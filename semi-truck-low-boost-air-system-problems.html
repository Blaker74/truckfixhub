The turbo is one part of a complete airflow system. Low measured boost can come from air not getting in, compressed air escaping, turbo/control problems, engine demand problems or exhaust not getting out.

🌬️ Air cannot get in

Inspect the intake path for restriction, damaged or collapsed ducting, incorrect filter installation and anything disturbed during recent service. Use the exact engine or vehicle restriction test when required. Do not diagnose a filter by appearance alone.

💨 Compressed air escapes

Charge-air leaks can reduce delivered air after the turbo has already done work. Look for loose clamps, damaged boots, rubbed piping, oily witness marks and charge-air-cooler damage. Use an approved pressure or leak test for the exact system rather than guessing from sound alone.

🌀 Turbo itself

Move toward the turbo only after checking the air path around it. Inspect for application-appropriate mechanical condition and compare commanded or expected boost behavior with actual behavior when the system provides that data. A turbo cannot create normal boost if the engine is not receiving fuel/load demand or if air is escaping downstream.

🎛️ Turbo control / actuator

Variable-geometry and electronically controlled systems add a command path. Ask: did the controller request movement, did the command reach the actuator, and did the mechanism respond? Codes and commanded-versus-actual data can help separate a control problem from an airflow problem.

🚫 Exhaust cannot get out

Excessive exhaust restriction can hurt engine breathing and power. Aftertreatment loading, exhaust damage or another restriction may overlap with boost complaints. Follow related pressure, temperature, regeneration and fault-code evidence before condemning the turbo.
