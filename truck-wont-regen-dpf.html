TRUCKFIXHUB TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE

Truck Won't Regen?

A parked regeneration that will not start—or will not complete—does not automatically mean the DPF needs replacement.

What to check first

  • Record every active SPN/FMI before clearing anything.
  • Confirm the engine is at the required operating temperature.
  • Check fuel level and OEM-specific parked-regen enable conditions.
  • Confirm parking brake, transmission/neutral and other safety interlocks are satisfied.
  • Look for active engine or aftertreatment faults that can inhibit regeneration.
  • Inspect DPF differential-pressure sensing tubes and wiring where appropriate.
  • Review soot load and regeneration history with compatible diagnostic software.

Why repeated regens matter

If a truck repeatedly asks for regeneration, diagnose why soot is accumulating or why regeneration is ineffective. Possible areas include engine-out soot production, exhaust temperature, pressure sensing, dosing, aftertreatment condition and operating duty cycle.

Do not force a regen blindly

High soot load, active faults or damaged aftertreatment components can require OEM-directed testing or service before a regeneration is appropriate. Follow the engine manufacturer's procedures.
