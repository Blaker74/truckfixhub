## Engine knowledge
### Current / modern engine families indexed
- DD5 — 5.1 L, four-cylinder medium-duty platform
- DD8 — 7.7 L medium-duty platform
- DD13 — 12.8 L heavy-duty platform
- DD15 — 14.8 L heavy-duty platform
- DD16 — 15.6 L heavy-duty platform

### DD13
Detroit identifies the DD13 as a 12.8 L six-cylinder engine used across on-highway and vocational applications. Current-generation information includes swirl-piston combustion, turbocharger and thermal-management developments, low-load PTO capability, DPF service-interval improvements, downspeeding options, cooling-system optimization and oil-system enhancements.

### DD15
Detroit identifies the DD15 as a 14.8 L six-cylinder heavy-duty engine. Current-generation information highlights downspeeding, swirl-piston combustion, simple-geometry turbocharging, thermal-management / Thermocoasting strategies, aftertreatment improvements and extended maintenance capability.

### DD16
Detroit positions the 15.6 L DD16 for the heaviest hauling and severe-duty applications. Current public specifications list output up to approximately 600 horsepower and 2050 lb-ft depending on generation/application.

### Gen 6 heavy-duty engines
Detroit has announced Gen 6 DD13, DD15 and DD16 engines for the 2027-emissions era. Detroit states DD13 and DD15 production begins in January 2027, with DD16 upgrades following in 2028. Manufacturer material describes changes including Miller-cycle intake timing, simplified direct-pressure fuel injectors, variable-displacement oil pump strategy, wastegated asymmetric turbocharging and updated 1-Box aftertreatment with pre-SCR technology.

Because this is future/current product information, TruckFixHub must re-check Detroit before presenting exact Gen 6 specifications or production timing.

## Legacy Detroit engine knowledge
Demand Detroit maintains manufacturer reference pages for legacy engines including:
- Series 50
- Series 60
- MBE 900
- MBE 4000

### Series 60
Detroit identifies the Series 60 as its first fully electronically controlled heavy-duty diesel engine and states that more than 1.2 million were sold beginning in 1987. Index Series 60 knowledge under DDEC electronic controls, legacy heavy-duty diagnostics, overhaul, fuel, turbocharger, cooling, lubrication and emissions-generation differences.

### Series 50
Detroit describes the Series 50 as an 8.5 L four-cylinder relative of the Series 60 using DDEC electronic engine controls. Applications included trucks, motor homes, buses, coaches, construction/industrial equipment and military vehicles, with CNG/LNG variants also produced.

## DT12 transmission knowledge
Demand Detroit divides current DT12 automated manual transmissions into on-highway and vocational families.

### On-highway
Indexed models include DT12-HE, DT12-H and DT12-HL depending on current offering/application. Core knowledge areas include:
- 12-speed automated manual operation
- Computer-controlled clutch and shifting
- Economy / performance / manual operating strategies where equipped
- Adaptive Thermal Management
- Intelligent Powertrain Management (IPM)
- Detroit engine integration
- Driveline protection and downspeeding

### Vocational
Indexed vocational models include DT12-V, DT12-VL and DT12-VX. Knowledge areas include vocational gearing, GCW/application differences, computer-controlled clutch operation and integration with Detroit engines and vehicle systems.

Exact ratings and compatibility must be verified against the current Detroit page for the specific transmission model.

## Integrated Detroit Powertrain
Detroit describes the Integrated Detroit Powertrain as coordinated engine, DT12 transmission and axle technology. Important indexed concepts include:
- Engine/transmission electronic communication
- Intelligent Powertrain Management (IPM)
- Downspeeding
- Driveline protection
- Axle Lubrication Management (ALM)
- Integrated gearing/axle ratios
- 1-Box aftertreatment

This is valuable TruckFixHub context because a complaint that appears to belong to one component can involve commands, protection strategies or data shared across the integrated powertrain.

## Diagnostic principles to carry into TruckFixHub
1. Identify the exact Detroit engine/transmission/axle and generation before applying specifications.
2. Record the complete fault information; a fault code is diagnostic direction, not automatic authorization to replace a part.
3. Consider integrated powertrain communication when multiple systems are involved.
4. Separate current DD-platform information from Series 60 / Series 50 / MBE legacy information.
5. Separate EPA/emissions generations because components, controls and aftertreatment strategies change.
6. Use Detroit service literature / DiagnosticLink procedures for exact troubleshooting and repair steps.
7. Verify current maintenance intervals, fluids, torque values, warranty terms and part numbers before use.
