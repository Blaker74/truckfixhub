🛞 SEMI TRUCK AIR SUSPENSION WON'T AIR UP?

First decide whether air is missing from the suspension or being intentionally dumped.

Quick answer

If a semi truck's air suspension will not inflate, first make sure the chassis air system has adequate pressure. Then check whether the suspension dump system is commanded to dump, inspect the height-control valve and linkage, trace air supply and delivery lines, and check the air springs for major leakage. Do not replace the height-control valve until you know it has air supply and is being mechanically or electronically commanded correctly.

Start here — in this order

1️⃣ Verify chassis air pressure

The suspension cannot inflate without an adequate air supply. If primary/secondary pressure is also slow or low, diagnose the main air system first.

2️⃣ Check the suspension dump command

Verify the dash dump switch and applicable dump solenoid/control state. A suspension that is intentionally held in dump can look like a failed height-control valve.

3️⃣ Inspect the height-control valve linkage

Look for disconnected, bent, damaged or incorrectly positioned linkage. Follow the suspension manufacturer's procedure before adjusting ride height.

4️⃣ Prove air reaches the height-control system

Trace the supply path using the correct schematic. A blocked, pinched, leaking or disconnected supply line can leave a good valve with nothing to deliver.

5️⃣ Check delivery to the air springs

If the valve is receiving supply and should be filling, inspect delivery plumbing and the air springs themselves for leaks or restrictions.

6️⃣ Verify ride height after repair

Ride height is a manufacturer specification, not a visual guess. Measure and adjust it according to the specific suspension procedure.

What the symptom can tell you

⬇️ Entire rear suspension stays down

Look for a common supply, dump-control or height-control problem before assuming multiple air springs failed at once.

↔️ One side or one bag stays low

Inspect that branch of the delivery plumbing, air spring and related suspension hardware. The system design determines whether bags share a common circuit.

🔊 Air leaks while trying to inflate

Locate the physical leak. A large leak at an air spring, fitting or line can consume the air being delivered and prevent normal ride height.

🔘 Problem started after using dump switch

Check whether the dump valve or solenoid returned to its normal state and whether its electrical/pneumatic control is functioning correctly.

🚚 Tractor airs up but trailer suspension does not

Treat the trailer suspension as its own system. Verify trailer air supply, dump controls, height-control valve, plumbing and bags.

🌙 Truck is down after sitting overnight

A suspension leak may allow the vehicle to settle while the brake reservoirs retain enough pressure. Find the leak instead of assuming the height valve is defective.

Information to collect before buying parts

  • Truck/trailer year, make and suspension type
  • Chassis air pressure
  • Whether all bags or only one area stays down
  • Dump-switch and dump-valve behavior
  • Height-control valve linkage condition
  • Air supply present at the suspension circuit
  • Location of any audible/measured leaks
  • Whether it fails constantly or only after sitting
  • Recent suspension, valve or air-line repairs
🧠 Parts-cannon rule: suspension won't air up does not automatically equal bad height-control valve. Prove supply air, dump state, linkage, delivery and air-spring integrity first.
⚠️ Air suspension can raise, lower or move unexpectedly. Never place yourself beneath an air-suspended vehicle unless it is correctly supported according to the manufacturer's safety procedure.
