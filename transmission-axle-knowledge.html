Transmissions & Rear Ends Made Simple

Eaton Fuller RTLO/FRO, Eaton Endurant, Detroit DT12, Allison 3000/4000 and Detroit rear axles. First identify the unit. Then decide whether the complaint is control, clutch/converter, internal transmission, driveline or axle.

EATON FULLER

RTLO · FRO

RTLO: heavy-duty Roadranger family commonly seen in 13- and 18-speed configurations. Eaton service literature covers the main section plus range/auxiliary systems.

FRO: Fuller heavy-duty 10-speed family. It uses a main gearbox plus an air-operated range system. The Roadranger shift valve, range cylinder, air lines and mechanical transmission all matter when diagnosing a range complaint.

Simple idea: a manual Fuller can have a mechanical gear problem, clutch/release problem, shift-control problem or range-air problem. Do not tear into the gearbox until you know which one.

EATON CUMMINS

Endurant

Endurant HD is a 12-speed automated manual transmission. Current Eaton Cummins literature lists 16 pints (7.57 L) of Eaton PS-386 for the standard HD specification. Endurant XD is an 18-speed automated family; cooler/PTO configuration can affect capacity and service requirements.

Simple idea: Endurant is still a geared transmission and clutch, but computers and actuators do the shifting. Power, air supply where applicable, network communication, clutch/actuator health and calibration can create symptoms that feel mechanical.

Official Endurant manuals →
DETROIT

DT12

DT12 is Detroit's automated manual transmission family. Current on-highway and vocational models use 12 forward speeds; current Detroit product pages list four reverse speeds for those model families. On-highway models include DT12-HE/HL and vocational models include DT12-V/VL/VX.

Simple idea: DT12 has no driver-operated clutch pedal. Electronics control clutch engagement and gear selection. A bad shift is not automatically an internal transmission failure.

Detroit integrates DT12 communication with Detroit engines and powertrain controls, so voltage, datalink, clutch protection, sensors, software/configuration and mechanical condition must be separated during diagnosis.

ALLISON

3000 · 4000 Series

Allison 3000 and 4000 Series are fully automatic transmissions using a torque converter rather than the dry-clutch architecture of an AMT. Allison publishes model/application information plus specific fluid and filter service instructions.

Simple idea: Allison diagnosis starts with fluid condition/level using the correct procedure, electronic controls and codes, selector response, input/output behavior and converter/clutch operation—not with a manual-transmission clutch diagnosis.

3000 Series covers many medium/heavy vocations; 4000 Series serves heavier applications. Exact ratios, fluid quantities, filters and controls depend on model and configuration.

Official Allison service sheets →
DETROIT AXLES

Detroit Rear Ends

Detroit offers single and tandem rear axles. Current tandem families include Models 4S/4T in 34,000–44,000 lb ratings and Model 4V in 44,000–46,000 lb ratings. Model 4V includes an internal oil pump for low-speed vocational lubrication.

Simple idea: the rear end contains more than a ring and pinion. Carrier/differential, input/output yokes, interaxle components on tandems, wheel ends, lubricant, breathers and axle housing all can create complaints.

Current Detroit maintenance literature specifies filling applicable axles to the bottom of the correct fill hole and warns that a smaller nearby temperature-sensor port is not the fill/level hole.

🪪

Decode the Transmission Name First

Do not order from “10-speed,” “13-speed” or “automatic” alone.

  • Record complete transmission model and serial.
  • RTLO and FRO suffixes/ratings matter.
  • Endurant HD vs XD matters.
  • DT12 model/generation matters.
  • Allison 3000 vs 4000 and exact model/control generation matter.

The tag is worth more than a visual guess.

↕️

Won't Go Into Gear

Manual Eaton: check clutch release/drag and shift linkage before assuming internal gear damage.

AMT — DT12/Endurant: capture faults first; check voltage/network foundation, clutch/actuator status, air supply where the design requires it, gear-position feedback and calibration.

Allison: capture codes, verify selector/controls and approved fluid-level procedure, then follow electronic/hydraulic diagnosis.

💥

Harsh / Bad Shift

Ask exactly when it shifts badly. One gear? Range shift? Hot only? Under load? From a stop?

  • FRO/RTLO: clutch, driver/shift technique, range system, linkage and internal synchronizing/range components depending model.
  • DT12/Endurant: clutch/actuation, calibration, sensors, controls/network and mechanical transmission.
  • Allison: controls, fluid/hydraulic behavior, converter/clutch operation and internal condition.
🌬️

Eaton Range Will Not Shift

On applicable Fuller range transmissions, separate the air-control side from the mechanical range section.

  1. Confirm adequate vehicle air.
  2. Inspect Roadranger valve and air lines.
  3. Check range-cylinder response.
  4. Verify linkage/shift condition.
  5. Only then move toward internal range-section diagnosis.

Eaton warns to exhaust vehicle air tanks before servicing pneumatic shift components.

🦶

Clutch: Manual vs AMT vs Allison

RTLO/FRO: conventional clutch/release system is part of driver shifting.

DT12/Endurant: an automated clutch transfers torque, but the computer/actuator controls it.

Allison: torque converter architecture—do not diagnose it like a dry-clutch AMT.

This single distinction prevents a lot of wrong diagnostic paths.

⚡

AMT Electrical / Network Foundation

Before condemning a DT12 or Endurant transmission, check whether low voltage, poor grounds, datalink faults or missing engine/vehicle information are present. Modern AMTs depend on coordinated controllers and sensor feedback.

Rule: save complete fault information before clearing anything.

🛢️

Transmission Fluid

Never use one fluid rule for all of these transmissions.

Current Endurant HD information specifies Eaton PS-386 and approximately 16 pints/7.57 L for its listed standard configuration. DT12 has generation-specific Detroit fluid service information. Allison publishes approved-fluid/filter procedures by series. Older Eaton Fuller models likewise require the lubricant specified for the exact transmission/application.

Service fill is not permission to guess. Verify the exact model and current manual.

⚙️

Rear End Noise

Classify the noise:

  • Pull/drive: occurs under positive torque.
  • Coast: appears when throttle is released.
  • Both: may follow road speed regardless of torque.
  • Turning: consider differential/wheel-end behavior as well.

Also inspect driveline U-joints/yokes, wheel ends and tires. A noise near the rear axle is not proof the differential is bad.

↔️

Rear End Clunk / Backlash

Find where the movement actually occurs. Check driveshaft joints and splines, input yoke, interaxle shaft on tandems, differential/carrier, axle shafts and suspension movement.

Do not condemn ring-and-pinion backlash from a cab clunk alone.

🧲

Metal on the Axle Plug

Magnetic drain plugs collect wear material. Detroit maintenance literature calls for cleaning the magnet and inspecting for large quantities of metal particles during service.

A light coating and chunks/large particles are not the same evidence. If abnormal debris is found, investigate before simply refilling and returning the truck to service.

🛞

Tandem Power Divider / Interaxle

On tandem drives, the forward carrier also participates in distributing torque to the rear axle. A complaint can involve the main carrier, interaxle driveline, power-divider/differential components, controls or axle shafts.

Identify whether the symptom changes with differential/power-divider lock operation before deciding which carrier is at fault.

📦

Before Ordering Parts

  • Truck VIN and application.
  • Complete transmission or axle model.
  • Serial/build number.
  • Ratio where applicable.
  • Manual, AMT or automatic.
  • Single or tandem rear axle.
  • Input/output/yoke configuration.
  • PTO/cooler/retarder or special configuration where applicable.
  • Actual failed component confirmed by diagnosis.

Never identify a transmission or carrier part by appearance alone.

Quick Problem Finder

FRO stuck in one range

Check vehicle air, Roadranger valve/lines and range-cylinder response before opening the transmission.

DT12 won't select gear

Save faults. Check electrical/network foundation and clutch/actuator/gear-position evidence before calling it an internal gearbox.

Endurant won't move

Separate clutch/actuator/control faults from internal transmission and downstream driveline problems.

Allison won't engage

Start with codes, selector/control status and correct fluid-level procedure; then follow model-specific electronic/hydraulic troubleshooting.

Rear axle whines

Record whether noise is on drive, coast or both and eliminate tires, wheel ends and driveline before condemning the gearset.

Rear end leaking

Find the source—pinion/input seal, axle/wheel end, housing, carrier joint, vent/breather or plug—before replacing a seal.

Easy memory rule

IDENTIFY → CONTROL → COUPLE → SHIFT → TRANSMIT → DIFFERENTIAL → WHEEL ENDS → VERIFY.

That order keeps a shift or rear-end complaint from turning into an expensive unverified transmission or carrier replacement.

⚠️ Transmission & Axle Safety

Heavy transmissions, drivelines and carriers can move or fall without warning. Chock/support the vehicle correctly, restrain components before removal, exhaust pneumatic systems when the service procedure requires it and follow the current manufacturer procedure for lifting, adjustment, fastener torque, fluid service and electronic calibration.
