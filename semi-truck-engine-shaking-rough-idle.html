If one cylinder or the whole engine does not get the right fuel, air, command, combustion or mechanical action, the engine can run unevenly.

❄️ Rough only when cold

Record outside temperature, cranking speed, smoke color and how long the roughness lasts. Cold-start combustion, fuel delivery, battery/cranking condition and sensor inputs can overlap. Let the evidence choose the next test.

🅿️ Rough while sitting still

If the vibration is present while stationary and changes with engine RPM, stay on the engine/accessory/mount path first. Save active codes and look for smoke, fuel clues, unusual sounds and recent work before moving toward cylinder-specific testing.

⛰️ Rough under load

If it becomes rough while pulling and also loses power, follow the low-power map. Fuel supply, air/boost, aftertreatment and engine-control problems can overlap.

📉 Open low-power picture →

🔧 Started after repair?

Start where hands were last working. Recheck disturbed connectors, grounds, intake plumbing, fuel connections, filters and anything opened or moved. A new symptom immediately after service is a strong clue.

🛣️ Only shakes while moving?

If the engine is smooth while parked but vibration appears with road speed, the source may be tires, wheels, wheel ends, steering, suspension or driveline instead of engine combustion.

🛞 Road-speed vibration guide →

Before blaming an injector

Capture codes and cylinder-related data available for the exact engine. Prove shared fuel supply first. Check relevant electrical connections and air-management clues. Use the manufacturer's cylinder contribution, cutout, injector or mechanical tests when the evidence points there.

One shared fuel or air problem can make several cylinders look bad.

Rough running is a symptom, not an injector diagnosis. Prove whether the problem is fuel, air, electrical/control, combustion, mechanical—or not the engine at all.

🛑 SAFETY: Stop safely for a red stop-engine warning, severe knocking, uncontrolled engine speed, heavy smoke with unsafe operation, rapid oil or coolant loss, or another manufacturer-directed shutdown condition. Verify exact tests and limits for the exact engine.
