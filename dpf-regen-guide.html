DPF & Regen Troubleshooting Guide

For heavy-duty diesel trucks with frequent, failed or incomplete regeneration concerns.

Why a truck may keep asking for a regen

A repeated regen request is a symptom, not a diagnosis. Soot production, sensor information, exhaust temperature, engine operation, aftertreatment condition and duty cycle can all affect regeneration behavior.

Information worth recording

  • Every active SPN/FMI and warning message
  • Whether a parked regen will start and whether it completes
  • How soon the warning returns afterward
  • Any loss of power or derate
  • Recent engine or aftertreatment repairs
  • Driving pattern, idle time and operating conditions

Before replacing the DPF

Proper diagnosis may require checking fault history, pressure and temperature information, engine-related causes of excessive soot, sensor plausibility, exhaust leaks and the physical condition of the aftertreatment system. A DPF-related complaint does not automatically mean the filter itself has failed.

Safety

Regeneration produces very high exhaust temperatures. Follow the vehicle manufacturer's regeneration instructions, observe fire-clearance requirements and do not perform a parked regen where hot exhaust could ignite nearby material.
