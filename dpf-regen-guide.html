DPF & Regen Troubleshooting

For heavy-duty diesel trucks with frequent, failed or incomplete regeneration concerns. A regen request tells you what the system wants—not which part failed.

Why a truck may keep asking for a regen

A repeated regen request is a symptom, not a diagnosis. Soot production, sensor information, exhaust temperature, engine operation, aftertreatment condition and duty cycle can all affect regeneration behavior.

Information worth recording first

  • Every active and inactive SPN/FMI and exact warning message
  • Whether a parked regen will start and whether it completes
  • How soon the warning returns afterward
  • Any loss of power, inducement or derate
  • Recent engine, exhaust, sensor or aftertreatment repairs
  • Driving pattern, idle time and operating conditions
  • DPF cleaning or maintenance history if known

Before replacing the DPF

Proper diagnosis may require checking fault history, pressure and temperature information, engine-related causes of excessive soot, sensor plausibility, exhaust leaks and the physical condition of the aftertreatment system. A DPF-related complaint does not automatically mean the filter itself has failed.

If the regen will not start or complete

Record exactly what happens. Some faults or operating conditions can inhibit regeneration, and an aborted regen may be the result of another unresolved problem. Use the engine and vehicle manufacturer's diagnostic procedure rather than repeatedly forcing attempts or clearing evidence.

Truck won't regen troubleshooting →

Regeneration creates very high exhaust temperatures

Only perform a parked or stationary regeneration when the applicable manufacturer procedure permits it and all required safety conditions are satisfied. Keep people and combustible material clear of the exhaust and aftertreatment area, follow site restrictions, and never improvise a forced-regeneration procedure.

Don't use a regen to hide the underlying problem

Clearing codes or repeatedly forcing regeneration can erase useful diagnostic evidence while leaving the cause unresolved. Capture the complete fault set and symptom sequence first.
