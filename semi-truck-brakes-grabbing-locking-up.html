“It locks up” is the symptom. The affected position is the first big clue.

🛞 One wheel grabs

Compare that wheel with the opposite side. Inspect the local brake and wheel-end condition, including foundation-brake movement, friction condition or contamination, adjustment/travel where applicable, and related hardware. Do not assume the air valve is the cause of a one-wheel complaint.

↔️ One axle grabs

When multiple brakes on the same axle react together, compare shared air/control behavior with the individual foundation brakes. Preserve whether both sides react at the same instant and whether the axle releases normally.

🚚 Several tractor brakes grab

Shared application/control behavior becomes more important when several tractor brakes react together. Record pedal input, pressure behavior, whether the event is repeatable, and whether the trailer changes the complaint.

🚛 Trailer brakes grab

Separate tractor from trailer. Determine whether the complaint follows this trailer, happens with multiple trailers, or changes with trailer connection. That comparison can prevent unnecessary tractor or trailer parts replacement.

🌧️ Wet, cold or first-stop behavior

If weather or sitting time changes the symptom, preserve that pattern. Moisture, contamination and friction-surface condition may matter, but the exact brake condition still needs inspection and proof.

Find the affected wheel, axle or vehicle section before choosing a test. Pattern first. Part name later.

🛑 SAFETY: Brake grabbing or wheel lockup can cause loss of control, tire damage and uneven stopping. Do not continue operating a vehicle with severe, unpredictable or unresolved brake lockup. Secure the vehicle before inspection and follow the correct brake-system procedures and specifications for the installed components.
