TRUCKFIXHUB FAULT-CODE GUIDE

SPN 3251 — DPF Differential Pressure

What the fault can tell you — and why it does not automatically mean the DPF itself is bad.

What to look at first

  • Record the exact FMI and all companion faults.
  • Inspect differential-pressure sensor wiring and connectors.
  • Inspect pressure tubes/lines for soot blockage, damage, moisture or restriction.
  • Review soot load and regeneration history.
  • Check for engine conditions that can create excessive soot.

Why the FMI matters

SPN identifies the monitored parameter while FMI describes the failure mode. The diagnostic path for an electrical signal fault can be very different from a valid high-pressure reading, so always use the complete SPN/FMI combination and the correct OEM procedure.

Repeated regen problems?

A truck that repeatedly requests regeneration or cannot complete one may have a sensor/tube issue, excessive soot production, temperature problem or a filter that requires service. Do not assume a parked regen fixes the root cause.

Safety & diagnosis

Exact definitions and diagnostic steps vary by engine manufacturer, model year and calibration. Use OEM service information and qualified heavy-duty diagnostics. Do not replace a DPF or sensor solely from a generic fault description.
