Heavy-Duty Suspensions Made Simple

Hendrickson, NEWAY, Reyco Granning, Freightliner AirLiner and Peterbilt Low Air Leaf. Identify the suspension first, then separate air, ride-height, bushing, beam, spring, torque-rod and mounting problems.

HENDRICKSON

Hendrickson

Common truck families: PRIMAAX EX air suspension, HAULMAAX EX rubber suspension, ULTIMAAX severe-duty rubber suspension and other Hendrickson truck systems.

Simple difference: PRIMAAX uses air springs and ride-height control. HAULMAAX/ULTIMAAX use engineered rubber/load-spring systems and walking/equalizing-beam principles for severe vocational work.

PRIMAAX inspection areas: air springs/lines, fasteners, pivot/D-pin bushings, clamp group, frame hangers, height-control valve, torque rods, QUIK-ALIGN connections, shocks, tire wear and U-beam assembly.

SAF-HOLLAND NEWAY

NEWAY

Common family: ADZ heavy-duty drive-axle air suspension. ADZ is used in on/off-road and vocational applications and is available in multiple capacities/configurations.

Think of NEWAY like this: air springs carry/control ride height while beams, bushings, crossmembers and mounting hardware locate the axles and handle suspension loads.

Service focus: correct ride height, equal air-spring inflation, leaks, pivot/axle connections, fasteners, bushings and alignment. SAF-HOLLAND publishes separate ADZ maintenance and repair/rebuild manuals.

Official NEWAY ADZ literature →
REYCO GRANNING

Reyco

Truck families include: 79KB and 102/102cc steel-spring drive-axle systems, 102AR/102AR-HD air suspensions and RD-Series drive-axle air suspension.

First question: is it a steel-spring Reyco or an air-suspension Reyco? The diagnostic path changes immediately.

Steel-spring complaints route toward springs, hangers, equalizing/mounting components, bushings and axle location. Air systems add air springs, valves, plumbing and ride-height control to the inspection.

FREIGHTLINER

AirLiner

AirLiner is Freightliner's rear air suspension family. Maintenance literature calls for inspection of air springs/pistons, air-line clearance, axle/beam connections, front pivot connections, shocks and stabilizer components where equipped.

Easy diagnostic idea: if the truck leans or ride height is wrong, do not immediately blame the air bag. Check air supply/leaks, height-control system, pivots/mounting and mechanical damage.

Freightliner literature also stresses adequate clearance around inflated air springs so nearby components do not rub and damage the bag.

PETERBILT / PACCAR

Low Air Leaf

Peterbilt currently lists Low Air Leaf and Low-Low Air Leaf among rear-suspension choices on heavy-duty models. The system combines leaf-spring structure with air springs and ride-height control.

Think of Low Air Leaf like this: the leaf and mounting hardware locate/support the axle while the air system controls ride characteristics and height. A lean, rough ride or driveline-angle complaint can come from either the mechanical or air-control side.

Important: Peterbilt ride heights differ by single/tandem configuration and Low Air Leaf vs Low-Low Air Leaf. Use the exact vehicle/manual rather than a universal number.

Truck Sitting Too High / Too Low

  1. Confirm the suspension family and correct measuring point.
  2. Park on level ground and establish the correct operating condition.
  3. Check air pressure and air-spring inflation.
  4. Inspect height-control valve/linkage and air plumbing.
  5. Look for damaged/bound mechanical components.
  6. Measure ride height against the exact OEM specification.

Do not adjust the valve until you know the correct ride height for that suspension.

↘️

Truck Leaning to One Side

  1. Check tire size/pressure and vehicle loading first.
  2. Compare air-spring inflation and inspect for leaks.
  3. Inspect height-control plumbing/linkage.
  4. Look for broken springs, damaged beams, bushings or mounting brackets.
  5. Check axle/frame relationship and ride height.

A lean is not automatically a bad air bag.

💨

Air Bag Keeps Going Flat

  1. Determine whether one bag or the entire suspension loses air.
  2. Soap-test bag, fittings and lines.
  3. Inspect bag for rubbing/contact damage.
  4. Check supply and height-control valve circuit.
  5. Verify the suspension is not mechanically forcing the bag out of position.

Replace the cause of the damage, not only the damaged bag.

🛞

Dog Tracking / Tire Wear

  1. Check tires and wheel ends.
  2. Verify ride height first on air suspensions.
  3. Inspect pivot bushings, torque rods, beams/springs and axle connections.
  4. Look for evidence of shifted or loose components.
  5. Measure axle alignment using the manufacturer's procedure.

Incorrect ride height or worn locating components can change alignment and driveline geometry.

🔨

Clunk / Rear Axle Movement

  1. Inspect torque rods and their bushings.
  2. Check pivot/bar-pin/bushing areas for movement.
  3. Inspect spring/beam mounts and frame brackets.
  4. Check clamp/U-bolt groups using the correct service procedure.
  5. Inspect shocks and stabilizer components where equipped.

Find where the movement is occurring before ordering a suspension assembly.

🪨

Rides Rough

  1. Confirm correct ride height.
  2. Check whether air springs inflate normally.
  3. Inspect shocks for damage/leakage.
  4. Look for seized/bound pivots or damaged springs/beams.
  5. Consider load and application: vocational rubber suspensions behave differently from highway air ride.

“Rough ride” by itself does not identify a failed component.

🔧

Before Ordering Parts

  • Truck make, model, year and VIN.
  • Suspension manufacturer.
  • Exact suspension family/model.
  • Single, tandem or tridem axle.
  • Suspension/axle capacity.
  • Ride height/configuration where applicable.
  • Part/casting/tag numbers from the component.
  • Which side/axle/location.
  • Actual failed component confirmed by inspection.

Never identify a suspension part by appearance alone.

⚠️

Ride Height Is More Important Than It Looks

Ride height can affect suspension geometry, axle alignment, driveline angles, air-spring position and ride quality. Peterbilt publishes different factory ride heights for Air Leaf, Air Trac, Low Air Leaf, Low-Low Air Leaf and Flex Air; NEWAY and Hendrickson likewise specify ride height by application.

Rule: identify the exact suspension before measuring or adjusting.

Quick Problem Finder

Air bag rubbed through

Check clearance and why the bag contacted another component before installing the replacement.

New height valve, still wrong

Verify linkage orientation, air plumbing, correct measuring point and exact ride-height specification.

Repeated torque-rod bushing failure

Look beyond the bushing: ride height, axle alignment, mounting damage and application can load the rod abnormally.

Inside-edge tire wear

Do not start at the air bag. Inspect tires/wheel ends, suspension bushings and axle alignment with ride height correct.

Rear suspension shifts on throttle

Inspect axle-locating components, torque rods, pivots/bushings, clamp groups and mounts for movement.

Dump truck rides differently empty

Severe-duty rubber suspensions such as HAULMAAX/ULTIMAAX use progressive spring behavior; compare complaint with the design and load condition before calling it a failure.

Easy memory rule

IDENTIFY → AIR/SPRING → RIDE HEIGHT → BUSHINGS/RODS → MOUNTS → ALIGNMENT → VERIFY.

That sequence separates an air-control problem from a worn mechanical suspension problem before parts are replaced.

⚠️ Suspension Safety

Suspensions support and locate the vehicle axles. Never work beneath a vehicle supported only by air suspension or a jack. Chock and support the vehicle correctly, exhaust/contain suspension air when the manufacturer procedure requires it, and follow current OEM instructions for fastener torque, ride-height adjustment, bushing replacement, welding and alignment.
