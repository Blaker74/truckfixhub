Stabilize the chassis before blaming alignment.

Tires, wheel ends, steering linkage, suspension geometry and ride height all influence how a truck tracks. Inspect the mechanical foundation first, then measure alignment.

The alignment machine is not the first diagnostic step

Tire condition → wheel-end integrity → steering linkage → suspension condition → ride height → alignment measurement.

Alignment cannot repair a loose joint, damaged wheel end, incorrect tire pressure or failed suspension component. Correct mechanical faults first so the measurements describe a stable chassis.

Pull all the time

Record whether the pull is present on level road at steady cruise and whether it changes with road crown, tire position, load or suspension height. A consistent cruise pull is different evidence from a pull that appears only while braking.

Pull only while braking

Do not make an alignment-only diagnosis. Compare braking behavior side to side and investigate brake drag, unequal application, wheel-end condition and other braking causes that can steer the truck during deceleration.

Use the symptom pattern

  • Road-speed shake: compare tire/wheel, wheel-end and rotating-component evidence before assuming steering linkage.
  • Bump-induced shimmy: inspect looseness, damping and steering/suspension joints that allow movement after a road input.
  • Wander: evaluate free play, tire condition/pressure, steering linkage, suspension movement and geometry.
  • Uneven tire wear: document the actual wear pattern and position; wear is evidence of what the tire has experienced, not an automatic alignment diagnosis.
  • Hard steering: separate mechanical binding from steering-assist supply/control problems before replacing the steering gear.

Evidence worth capturing

Record axle position, tire position and wear pattern, tire pressures, load condition, ride height, when the symptom occurs, recent tire/alignment/suspension work, and any visible looseness or damage. Exact free-play limits, ride-height dimensions, alignment specifications and torque values vary by vehicle; verify them with current service information.

Steering and suspension safety

Steering, wheel-end and suspension failures can cause loss of vehicle control. Do not operate a vehicle with severe looseness, damaged steering linkage, a suspected wheel-bearing failure, unsecured suspension components or another condition that makes directional control unsafe. Chock and support the vehicle correctly before inspection.
