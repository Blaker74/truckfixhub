Build the foundation before measuring alignment

TIRES → WHEEL ENDS → STEERING LINKAGE → SUSPENSION → RIDE HEIGHT → ALIGNMENT

An alignment cannot repair a loose joint, damaged wheel end, tire problem or failed suspension component. Fix mechanical problems first so the alignment machine is measuring a stable truck.

↩️ Pulls while cruising

Notice whether it happens on a level road and whether road crown, tire position, load or suspension height changes it. That evidence helps separate chassis and tire causes.

🛑 Pulls only while braking

Do not make an alignment-only diagnosis. A brake or wheel-end problem can steer the truck during deceleration, so compare braking behavior side to side.

Let the symptom tell you where to look

  • Road-speed shake: inspect tire/wheel, wheel-end and rotating-component evidence.
  • Bump-induced shimmy: look for looseness, damping problems and joints that allow movement after the bump.
  • Wander: check tire condition, free play, linkage, suspension movement and geometry.
  • Uneven tire wear: record the actual pattern and position. Tire wear is evidence, not automatic proof of bad alignment.
  • Hard steering: separate mechanical binding from steering-assist supply/control problems before replacing a steering gear.

Evidence worth saving

Record axle and tire position, wear pattern, tire pressures, load condition, ride height, when the symptom occurs, recent tire/alignment/suspension work, and visible looseness or damage.

Exact free-play limits, ride-height dimensions, alignment specifications and torque values vary by vehicle. Verify them with current service information.

⚠️ Steering and suspension safety

Steering, wheel-end and suspension failures can cause loss of vehicle control. Do not operate a vehicle with severe looseness, damaged steering linkage, a suspected wheel-bearing failure, unsecured suspension components or another condition that makes directional control unsafe. Chock and support the vehicle correctly before inspection.
