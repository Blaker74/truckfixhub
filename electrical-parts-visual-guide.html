⚡ ELECTRICAL PARTS — SEE IT & NAME IT

Recognize the pieces that carry, switch, generate and control electrical power.

Electrical diagnosis starts with voltage, voltage drop, grounds, circuit protection and the exact wiring diagram—not guessing at modules. Disconnect or test circuits only by the correct manufacturer procedure.

Battery

Look for: Rectangular battery case with positive and negative terminals, commonly grouped in a battery box.

Job: Stores electrical energy and supplies high current for starting and vehicle systems.

STARTING / CHARGING →

Starter Motor / Solenoid

Look for: Heavy cylindrical motor near the flywheel housing with a smaller solenoid and large battery cables.

Job: Cranks the engine when the starting circuit commands it.

DIAGNOSE STARTING →

Alternator

Look for: Belt-driven vented metal housing with pulley and electrical output connections.

Job: Generates electrical power and maintains battery charge while the engine runs.

DIAGNOSE CHARGING →

Fuse / Circuit Protection

Look for: Replaceable blade, cartridge or high-current fuse in a fuse/power distribution panel.

Job: Opens a circuit when current exceeds its designed limit.

FUSE KEEPS BLOWING →

Relay

Look for: Small cube/rectangular plug-in device with several terminals in a power distribution center.

Job: Uses a low-current control circuit to switch another electrical circuit.

SEE ELECTRICAL DIAGNOSTICS →

Ground Strap / Ground Cable

Look for: Heavy cable or braided strap connecting battery, engine, frame, cab or electrical components to ground.

Job: Provides the return path required for current flow.

DIAGNOSE GROUNDS →

Electrical Connector

Look for: Mating plastic connector bodies with terminals, seals and locking tabs.

Job: Joins wiring while maintaining reliable electrical contact and environmental sealing.

CONNECTOR CORROSION →

Electronic Control Module

Look for: Sealed electronic box with one or more large multi-pin connectors; location varies by system.

Job: Processes inputs, communicates on vehicle networks and commands outputs.

SEE CONTROL / WIRING →

Electronic Sensor

Look for: Small threaded, inserted or mounted device with an electrical connector; shape depends on what it measures.

Job: Converts a physical condition such as pressure, temperature, position or speed into information a module can use.

Wiring Harness

Look for: Bundled wires in loom/tape routed between modules, sensors, switches and power distribution.

Job: Carries power, ground, control and communication signals around the truck.
