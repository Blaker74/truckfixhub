Fan Clutches Made Simple

Freightliner · Peterbilt · Kenworth · International · Western Star · Mack · Volvo. The truck name does not tell you how the fan clutch works. Identify the fan-drive manufacturer and design first. Horton and Kysor/BorgWarner systems can use different air logic, service parts and procedures.

HORTON

Horton Fan Drives

Horton publishes application guides specifically for Freightliner/Western Star, Peterbilt, Kenworth, International/Navistar, Mack and Volvo. A common heavy-duty design is the DM Advantage On/Off.

Important logic: Horton DM Advantage On/Off is spring-engaged and air-disengaged. Loss of control air therefore tends toward fan engagement on this design—a fail-safe cooling strategy.

Horton lists 90 psi minimum air pressure for the DM Advantage On/Off specification. That number belongs to that fan-drive family, not every fan clutch ever installed on these truck makes.

KYSOR / BORGWARNER

Kysor Fan Drives

BorgWarner publishes current Kysor On/Off service and diagnostic literature covering front-air and rear-air fan-drive designs plus K30/K32 and related products.

Important: do not assume Kysor air logic from a Horton diagram—or vice versa. Identify the exact clutch and use its service guide.

Kysor diagnostic literature connects rear air leakage, piston-rod/O-ring damage and coupler damage with pulley-hub bearing wear. It also stresses correcting the cause of an overheated or damaged clutch rather than simply installing a kit.

Official BorgWarner/Kysor literature →
FREIGHTLINER · WESTERN STAR

DTNA Trucks

Freightliner maintenance literature covers Horton and Kysor fan-clutch inspection: electrical connections and solenoid wiring, solenoid filter where equipped, fan condition/clearance, belt condition/alignment, friction material and actual engagement/disengagement.

Western Star maintenance literature likewise treats the fan clutch as part of the engine cooling and belt-drive system. Exact installed fan-drive parts and wiring should be identified by VIN/build through DTNA resources.

Simple idea: the truck can command the fan correctly while an air leak or worn clutch prevents the fan from doing what it was told.

Freightliner manuals →
PETERBILT · KENWORTH

PACCAR Trucks

Peterbilt and Kenworth applications can use supplier fan drives such as Horton systems, but exact clutch, hub, solenoid and control strategy depend on engine, model, build and installed option. Horton maintains separate Peterbilt and Kenworth application guides.

Simple idea: use the VIN and fan-clutch identification together. “It came off a Peterbilt” is not enough information to select a repair kit or replacement clutch.

When a fan complaint accompanies overheating or A/C trouble, separate the electronic fan request from solenoid/air operation and physical clutch lockup.

INTERNATIONAL

International / Navistar

International applications likewise span multiple fan-drive configurations. Horton publishes a Navistar/International application guide, and the truck’s engine/control configuration determines when fan operation is requested.

Simple idea: identify the installed fan drive before deciding whether air should make that particular clutch engage or disengage.

Do not use another truck make’s fan-solenoid plumbing as a substitute for the exact International wiring/air schematic.

MACK · VOLVO

Mack & Volvo

Horton publishes separate Mack and Volvo application guides. Exact fan control can be integrated with engine electronics, cooling demand and vehicle systems, while the physical fan drive may still be a supplier-specific pneumatic or electronically controlled design.

Simple idea: prove whether the engine/controller is requesting fan operation, then prove whether the fan drive responds. Do not condemn the clutch because the fan is off—or condemn electronics because the fan stays on.

🪪

Block 1 — Identify What Is Actually on the Truck

  • Truck VIN, model and year.
  • Engine make/model.
  • Fan-drive manufacturer: Horton, Kysor/BorgWarner or other.
  • Fan-clutch model/part number.
  • Front-air, rear-air, on/off, two-speed, viscous/electronic design.
  • Solenoid and air-line arrangement.

Never order from “Freightliner fan clutch” or “Volvo fan clutch” alone.

🧠

Block 2 — Who Is Asking for the Fan?

Modern trucks may request fan operation for engine coolant temperature, A/C pressure/heat rejection, intake/charge-air conditions, engine protection or other calibrated strategies.

Use scan data/fault information and the exact OEM control logic to determine whether the fan is being commanded ON or OFF.

Command and physical fan operation are two different pieces of evidence.

🌬️

Block 3 — Air Supply & Solenoid

  1. Confirm adequate vehicle air supply.
  2. Inspect fan-clutch air line for leaks, kinks and damage.
  3. Check fan solenoid electrical connector and wiring.
  4. Verify the solenoid actually changes air state when commanded.
  5. Inspect/clean a solenoid filter where the manufacturer specifies one.

A perfect clutch cannot respond correctly if the control air never reaches it.

↔️

Block 4 — Air-to-Engage or Air-to-Disengage?

This is one of the most important fan-clutch questions.

Horton DM Advantage On/Off: spring-engaged, air-disengaged.

Other designs can use different logic. Some older/conversion applications change solenoid requirements when fan-drive type changes.

Never diagnose the air circuit until you know what air is supposed to do.

🔥

Block 5 — Fan Will Not Engage

  1. Confirm the truck is requesting fan ON.
  2. Confirm correct air state for that clutch design.
  3. Inspect solenoid and plumbing.
  4. Inspect clutch lining/friction surfaces where applicable.
  5. Check hub/bearings and mechanical damage.
  6. Verify fan and belt drive can turn correctly.

Do not jumper/bypass controls as a permanent repair.

🧊

Block 6 — Fan Stays Engaged

First ask whether the truck is intentionally commanding the fan. If command is OFF but fan remains locked, inspect the fan-drive control air, solenoid, internal seals/mechanism and mechanical condition according to the identified design.

On a spring-engaged/air-disengaged Horton design, loss of air can make the fan stay engaged.

💨

Block 7 — Air Leak at Fan Clutch

Find the exact leak point before ordering a seal kit. Inspect lines/fittings, solenoid circuit and clutch/hub interface.

Kysor diagnostic literature notes that rear O-ring/piston-rod damage and air leakage can be related to worn pulley-hub bearings. Fixing only the seal without fixing hub movement can repeat the failure.

🌀

Block 8 — Fan Slips When Engaged

Check lining/friction condition, contamination, overheating evidence, clutch engagement and fan/hub condition. Freightliner maintenance literature calls for inspecting friction facing for wear, oil contamination and burn marks on applicable Horton/Kysor units.

A commanded fan that slips cannot move the designed airflow.

🔊

Block 9 — Vibration / Wobble / Noise

  1. Inspect fan blades for cracks, bends and damage.
  2. Check fan-to-shroud/component clearance.
  3. Inspect fan mounting.
  4. Check clutch/hub bearings for play.
  5. Inspect pulley/belt alignment and tension.
  6. Look for damaged coupler or clutch components.

Never stand in the fan plane during a live test.

🪢

Block 10 — Belt & Pulley Matter

The clutch cannot deliver airflow if the belt drive is slipping, misaligned or damaged. Freightliner and Western Star maintenance information includes belt condition/alignment as part of fan-clutch inspection.

A seized or loose hub bearing can also damage clutch components and seals.

🌡️

Block 11 — Overheating Does Not Automatically Mean Fan Clutch

Before replacing the clutch, separate:

  • Coolant quantity/condition.
  • Radiator/condenser/CAC airflow restriction.
  • Fan command.
  • Fan-clutch response.
  • Fan blade/rotation/clearance.
  • Thermostat/circulation problems.
  • Engine load and other cooling-system causes.
❄️

Block 12 — A/C Warm at Idle

A fan that fails to move enough air across the condenser can contribute to poor A/C performance at idle. But prove condenser airflow and fan request before blaming the clutch.

If the fan is being correctly commanded and physically locked, continue A/C diagnosis rather than replacing a working fan clutch.

🔧

Block 13 — Repair Kit or Complete Clutch?

That decision depends on exact model and damage. Inspect the hub/bearings, piston rod/shaft, friction surfaces, housing and coupler. BorgWarner Kysor literature specifically notes that some damage conditions call for clutch replacement rather than a repair kit, and aluminum clutch assemblies identified in its service guide are replace-not-repair.

Correct the cause of overheating or abnormal wear before installing the replacement.

📦

Block 14 — Before Ordering Parts

  • VIN and engine.
  • Fan-drive manufacturer and exact part number.
  • Truck application and build.
  • Fan pilot and mounting configuration.
  • Pulley/groove configuration.
  • Air-control logic/design.
  • Solenoid configuration.
  • Fan diameter/blade/application information when required.
  • Repair kit vs hub vs complete fan-drive need.

Cross by exact identification—not appearance.

Quick Problem Finder

Fan stays on all the time

Check commanded state first. Then identify air logic and verify solenoid, air supply and clutch release operation.

Fan never comes on

Prove the ECM/controller requests it, then prove control air/electrical state and physical clutch engagement.

Fan cycles constantly

Check whether the command itself is cycling because of temperature/A/C inputs before replacing the clutch.

Air leaks from clutch

Locate the leak and inspect hub/bearing movement and seals. A new O-ring may not cure the root cause.

Fan engages but engine still heats

Verify fan speed/lockup, blade condition, shroud/clearance and radiator/CAC/condenser airflow before moving deeper into cooling diagnosis.

New clutch works backwards

Stop and verify the fan-drive type and solenoid air logic. Different designs/conversions can require different control-valve arrangements.

Easy memory rule

IDENTIFY → COMMAND → AIR → SOLENOID → CLUTCH → FAN/BELT → VERIFY.

That order separates a truck-control problem from an air-control problem and a mechanical fan-clutch failure.

⚠️ Fan Clutch Safety

A heavy-duty engine fan can engage without warning. Before hands-on inspection, follow the manufacturer procedure to disable starting, disconnect batteries where required and drain/isolate air where required. Never work in the fan plane with the engine running. Inspect a running fan only from a safe distance using the approved procedure. Replace cracked or damaged fan blades; do not attempt to straighten or repair them unless the fan manufacturer explicitly provides such a procedure.
