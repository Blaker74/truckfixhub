If the leak appears only during a brake application, focus on the parts and plumbing that become pressurized during that application.

🔀 Sound near a valve or frame area

First determine whether air is escaping from a line or fitting, a valve exhaust, or another nearby source. Air coming from a valve area does not automatically prove the valve itself failed.

🚛 Leak changes with the trailer

Compare tractor-only behavior with the trailer connected. If the symptom appears only with one trailer, that comparison is powerful evidence. Follow tractor-to-trailer plumbing, couplings and trailer brake components before replacing tractor parts at random.

❓ Cannot tell where it is

Record four things: whether the leak exists with brakes released, whether it starts when the pedal is pressed, which gauge changes, and whether connecting the trailer changes the symptom. Those observations choose the next branch.

A sound points toward an area. It does not prove the failed component.

🛑 SAFETY: Brake-application air loss is safety-critical. Do not operate a truck that cannot maintain required air pressure or has an unresolved brake defect. Never crawl beneath an unsecured vehicle to locate a leak. Never bypass low-air warnings or spring-brake protection. Spring brake chambers can contain dangerous stored mechanical energy. Verify exact procedures and limits for the exact vehicle.
