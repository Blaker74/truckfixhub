🔎 TEST THAT PATH

One wheel points local. Several brakes point toward something shared.

↔️ One axle or several brakes drag

When more than one brake stays applied together, shared air delivery or control becomes more important. Record whether the pattern is one axle, tractor-only or trailer-only before replacing individual chambers or foundation-brake parts.

🚚 Several tractor brakes stay applied

Identify what happened immediately before the brakes stuck: service-brake application, parking-brake release, pressure loss, or another control change. Shared timing is evidence that can lead toward the common control path.

🐌 Truck feels like it is being held back

Do not assume engine or transmission trouble. Safely compare wheel-end heat without touching hot components and note whether the resistance appears after brake use. A dragging brake can feel like a powertrain problem from the driver's seat.

⚠️ If a spring brake is involved

Spring brake chambers can contain dangerous stored mechanical energy. Do not open, cut or disassemble them and do not improvise a release procedure. Use the correct manufacturer procedure and qualified brake service practices.

A dragging brake is a symptom. Prove whether the cause is air delivery/control, chamber movement, foundation brake, or wheel-end related.

🛑 SAFETY: Do not drive against a dragging or locked brake. A brake that remains applied can rapidly overheat and damage friction, tire and wheel-end components. Do not touch a suspected hot wheel end. Never crawl beneath an unsecured vehicle or bypass brake-protection systems. Verify exact inspection and repair procedures for the vehicle.
