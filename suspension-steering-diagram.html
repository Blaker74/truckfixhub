👇 PICK THE COMPLAINT

↔️ It wanders or feels loose

  1. Verify tire pressure, tire condition and obvious wheel problems first.
  2. Check steering-wheel free play and follow the movement through the column, steering gear and linkage.
  3. Inspect tie-rod ends, drag link, kingpins, wheel ends and suspension joints for looseness using the correct procedure.
  4. Check axle location, ride height and suspension components that can let the axle move.
  5. Perform alignment checks only after loose or worn components are corrected.

💪 Steering is suddenly hard

  1. Do not force the truck back into service if steering effort is unsafe.
  2. Check steering-assist fluid level / condition and obvious leaks where applicable.
  3. Inspect pump drive, hoses and assist-system operation using the manufacturer procedure.
  4. Check linkage, kingpins and axle components for mechanical binding.
  5. Condemn the steering gear only after assist supply and external binding are ruled out.

🔨 It clunks over bumps or while steering

  1. Identify exactly when the noise occurs: bumps, steering, braking, acceleration or axle movement.
  2. Inspect spring and suspension mounting hardware, bushings, torque rods and shocks.
  3. Check steering linkage and kingpins if steering changes the noise.
  4. Check wheel-end looseness if the noise or movement is near a wheel.
  5. Repair the proven loose or damaged component instead of replacing parts based only on the sound.

Steering complaints can start outside the steering gear

A truck that wanders, pulls, feels loose or wears tires unevenly can have causes in the tires, wheel ends, steering linkage, kingpins, axle location, suspension or alignment. Inspect the whole path and measure free play or alignment to the manufacturer’s specifications instead of adjusting or replacing parts by feel alone.
