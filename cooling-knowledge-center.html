Cooling System Toolbox

The engine makes heat. The cooling system has one big job: pick up that heat and get rid of it.

🌡️ SIMPLE COOLING RULE
COOLANT PICKS UP HEAT → COOLANT MOVES → RADIATOR RELEASES HEAT → AIR CARRIES IT AWAY

🧰 Five Cooling Drawers

1 · ENOUGH COOLANTLow coolant can introduce air and reduce heat transfer. First find why the coolant is low.
2 · COOLANT MOVESThe water pump and coolant passages must move coolant through the engine and radiator.
3 · RADIATOR RELEASES HEATInternal restriction can reduce flow. External debris or damaged fins can reduce heat transfer.
4 · AIR MOVES THROUGH ITThe fan, fan clutch, shroud, radiator and charge-air-cooler face all affect airflow.
5 · PRESSURE STAYS CONTROLLEDLeaks, cap trouble, trapped gas or abnormal combustion pressure can change cooling-system behavior.

🔍 Let The Symptom Pick The Drawer

🔥 HOT PULLING HARDThink heat load, airflow, restriction, fan performance and circulation.
🚦 HOT AT IDLEAirflow becomes especially important. Check whether the fan system is doing what it is commanded to do.
💧 LOSING COOLANTLook for external leaks, overflow, pressure behavior and coolant entering another system.
⬆️ PUSHES COOLANT OUTDo not automatically call a head gasket. Check temperature, fill level, cap, pressure and combustion-gas evidence.
🌡️ TEMP CHANGES FASTLook at coolant level, air pockets, sensor plausibility, thermostat behavior and circulation.
❄️ RUNS TOO COOLConsider thermostat behavior, fan operation, sensor accuracy and operating conditions.

🔧 Overheating Path

SEE ITConfirm the engine is actually overheating. Note load, road speed, ambient conditions, coolant level and warnings.
NAME ITDoes the evidence point toward coolant loss, airflow, circulation, pressure, sensor information or excessive heat load?
TEST ITInspect safely and compare temperatures, fan operation, coolant condition, pressure behavior and approved test results.
PROVE ITRepair the supported cause, reproduce the original operating condition and confirm temperature stays controlled.

⚠️ HOT COOLANT CAN BURN YOU

Never remove a pressure cap or open a hot cooling system until it is safe according to the manufacturer procedure. Exact pressure limits, coolant specifications, fill procedures, fan tests and temperature thresholds depend on the engine and vehicle application.
