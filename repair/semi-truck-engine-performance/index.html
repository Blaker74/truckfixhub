Start with exactly how the engine is behaving.

Low power, smoke, rough running, low boost and derates can overlap across fuel, air, electrical, mechanical and aftertreatment systems. Record the operating condition and fault information first, then test the system before replacing expensive components.

Diagnose before replacing

Record whether the symptom occurs cold or hot, at idle or under load, and whether it changes with engine RPM. Note smoke color, boost behavior, fluid consumption, warning lights and SPN/FMI information before clearing faults.

A symptom does not prove a component. Verify fuel supply, air and charge-air integrity, electrical power and grounds, sensor data and applicable aftertreatment conditions before condemning injectors, turbochargers, control modules or internal engine parts.

Safety boundary

If the complaint includes a red stop-engine warning, severe knocking, rapid oil or coolant loss, uncontrolled engine speed, severe overheating or another manufacturer-directed shutdown condition, stop safely and follow OEM procedures. Do not continue operating a truck that cannot meet applicable safety requirements.
