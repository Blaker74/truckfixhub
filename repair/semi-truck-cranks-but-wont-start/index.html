Full troubleshooting explanation

If the starter physically turns the engine but it will not start, organize the evidence before replacing parts. Useful evidence includes ECM communication, active and stored faults, engine RPM while cranking, fuel supply observations and recent repair work.

  1. Scan active and stored fault codes and verify ECM communication.
  2. Check ECM power and grounds during cranking using correct service information.
  3. Observe engine RPM while cranking.
  4. Verify fuel level and inspect the applicable low-pressure supply path, filters and restrictions.
  5. For systems that require it, compare verified engine-specific fuel-pressure requirements with measured values.
  6. Verify crank/cam synchronization and injector-control information where supported by the engine diagnostic system.

If the engine physically cranks but scan data shows no RPM, investigate the crankshaft-position signal path, wiring, connector and related trigger system using correct engine information. That observation alone does not prove the sensor itself failed.

After recent repairs, inspect disturbed connectors, grounds and fuel-system connections before widening the search.
