Engine Cranks — But Won't Start

Show first. Explain second. If the starter is physically turning the engine, use observable evidence to choose the next diagnostic path instead of guessing at a failed component.

🛑 Secure the vehicle first

Follow manufacturer procedures and normal shop safety practices. Do not bypass starting, neutral-safety or other protective systems. High-pressure diesel fuel systems require appropriate procedures and equipment.

👁️ Next checks to organize

  1. Scan active and stored fault codes and verify ECM communication.
  2. Verify ECM power and grounds during cranking using the correct service information.
  3. Observe engine RPM while cranking.
  4. Verify fuel level and inspect the applicable low-pressure supply path, filters and restrictions.
  5. For systems that require it, compare verified engine-specific fuel-pressure requirements with measured values.
  6. Verify crank/cam synchronization and injector-control information where supported by the engine diagnostic system.

TruckFixHub rule: evidence first → engine-specific testing → component conclusion last.

Full troubleshooting explanation

If cranking speed is normal, shift diagnosis toward engine management, fuel delivery and position signals.

  1. Scan active/stored codes and verify ECM communication.
  2. Check ECM power and grounds during cranking.
  3. Watch engine RPM while cranking.
  4. Verify fuel level, filters, restrictions and low-pressure supply.
  5. Check required high-pressure fuel values where applicable.
  6. Verify crank/cam synchronization and injector command.

If the engine physically cranks but scan data shows no RPM, investigate crankshaft-position signal, wiring, connector and trigger system. After recent repairs, inspect disturbed connectors, grounds and fuel-system connections first.
