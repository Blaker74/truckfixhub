💨 AIR ESCAPES?

Do not ask “What part leaks?” first. Ask “What changed when the leak started?”

🅿️ Loses air parked

Watch both gauges. Note which circuit falls and whether the loss is fast or slow. Safely listen around reservoirs, drains, lines, fittings, dryer, valves, chambers and air-operated accessories.

If only one circuit behaves differently, keep that observation—it can sharply narrow the search.

🔓 Leak changes when parking brakes release

A leak that appears or changes when spring/parking brakes are released tells you the system state matters. Follow the affected circuit and use the parking-brake and chamber paths rather than replacing a valve from sound alone.

⚙️ Pressure loss with engine running

The compressor can hide a leak by replacing escaping air. Watch whether pressure cycles unusually, struggles to recover, or the dryer behavior changes. Separate air being lost from air being produced too slowly.

🌙 Air drops after shutdown

Record which gauges fall, how far they fall, elapsed time, and whether a trailer was connected. A slow leak may be hard to hear but the timing and affected circuit are still useful evidence.

A hiss helps locate the area. It does not prove which component failed.

🛑 SAFETY: Do not operate a truck that cannot maintain required air pressure or has an unresolved brake defect. Never bypass low-air warnings or spring-brake protection. Never crawl beneath an unsecured vehicle to locate a leak. Spring brake chambers can contain dangerous stored mechanical energy. Verify exact leak limits, pressure requirements and procedures for the exact vehicle and applicable requirements.
