TRUCKFIXHUB AIR SYSTEM GUIDE

Semi Truck Losing Air Pressure?

How to organize the symptoms when a Class 8 truck leaks air, pressure drops while parked, or the primary or secondary system loses pressure.

Air loss can become a brake-safety issue

Do not operate a truck that cannot maintain safe air pressure or has an unresolved air-brake defect. If a low-air warning or buzzer is active, treat it as real until the system is properly evaluated and use the low-air warning troubleshooting guide for the symptom path.

When does the pressure drop?

The timing of the air loss is useful diagnostic information.

  • Engine off, brakes released: note which gauge falls and how quickly.
  • Service brake applied: a leak that appears or becomes worse during brake application points the diagnostic process toward components active during application.
  • Parking brakes released: compare the symptom with the parked condition.
  • Engine running: the compressor may mask a leak until demand exceeds supply.
  • After shutdown: record whether pressure falls immediately or over a longer period.

Common areas technicians inspect

  • Air lines, hoses, fittings and connections
  • Brake chambers and related plumbing
  • Relay, protection and control valves
  • Air dryer and purge-valve area
  • Reservoirs, drains and tank connections
  • Cab, suspension and other air-operated accessories
  • Trailer supply and tractor/trailer connections when a trailer is attached

Hearing an air leak helps locate a problem, but the failed part should still be positively identified before replacement.

Air dryer keeps purging or leaking

If the dryer purges repeatedly or leaks continuously, record whether the truck is also losing system pressure. Frequent compressor cycling caused by another air leak can affect purge behavior, so repeated purging does not automatically identify the dryer as the failed component.

Primary vs. secondary pressure

Watch both gauges. If one circuit drops while the other remains relatively stable, tell the technician exactly which one is affected. That information can be far more useful than simply reporting that “the truck leaks air.”

Truck loses air only when brakes are applied

Record whether the loss occurs with a light application, a full application or only when the parking brakes are released. A leak that is loudest at one wheel position may warrant inspection of the chamber, nearby hoses, fittings and related components.

Brake chamber leak troubleshooting →

Do not crawl under an unsecured vehicle or attempt to locate a leak around moving brake components without proper training, support and safety procedures.

Air pressure drops overnight

A truck that is fully charged when parked but low the next morning may have a slower leak. Note the starting pressure, ending pressure, elapsed time and whether a trailer was connected. Those details give the repair shop a better starting point.

Never defeat low-air protection

Do not bypass low-air warnings, spring brakes or other safety devices to keep the truck moving. Air-brake diagnosis and repair should follow the vehicle and component manufacturer's procedures and applicable inspection requirements.
