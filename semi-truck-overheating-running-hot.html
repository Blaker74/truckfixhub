TRUCKFIXHUB COOLING SYSTEM GUIDE

Semi Truck Overheating or Running Hot?

A practical starting point when a Class 8 truck has high coolant temperature, overheats under load, runs hot climbing hills or repeatedly loses coolant.

Do not keep driving an overheating engine

Continued operation with excessive coolant temperature or major coolant loss can cause severe engine damage and can create a roadside safety hazard. Follow the vehicle and engine manufacturer's warnings and shutdown procedures.

When does the truck run hot?

  • Only pulling a grade or heavy load: note ambient temperature, load and whether the fan engages.
  • At highway speed: airflow, coolant circulation and cooling-system condition deserve investigation.
  • At idle or low speed: fan operation and low-speed airflow become useful clues.
  • Immediately after service: check what changed, including coolant level, trapped air, connections and disturbed components.
  • Constantly: do not assume a thermostat is the cause; diagnose the entire cooling system.

Common areas technicians investigate

  • Coolant level and external leaks
  • Radiator and charge-air-cooler airflow restriction
  • Cooling fan and fan-control operation
  • Thermostat operation
  • Water-pump and coolant-circulation concerns
  • Hoses, surge tank, pressure cap and related plumbing
  • Coolant contamination or improper coolant condition
  • Internal engine or EGR-cooler related concerns when symptoms support further testing

Overheating has many possible causes. Replacing parts from the symptom alone can get expensive quickly.

Useful clues to record

  • Maximum coolant temperature shown
  • Outside temperature and whether the truck was loaded
  • Road speed, grade and engine load when temperature rises
  • Whether the engine fan comes on
  • Any coolant loss, odor, steam or visible leakage
  • Active fault codes or warning messages
  • Recent cooling-system or engine repairs

Overheats only on hills

A complaint that appears mainly under heavy engine load is valuable information. Cooling-system capacity, airflow, fan operation and coolant circulation may need to be evaluated under conditions that reproduce the problem.

Losing coolant with no obvious external leak

Document how much coolant is being added and how often. A pressure test and proper engine-specific diagnostic procedure may be needed to determine where the coolant is going. Do not remove a pressure cap from a hot cooling system.

Hot cooling systems can cause severe burns

Never open a hot pressurized cooling system. Allow the system to cool and follow OEM service and safety procedures. TruckFixHub provides educational troubleshooting information and does not replace qualified diagnosis.
