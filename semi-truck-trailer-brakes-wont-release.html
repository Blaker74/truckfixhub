TRUCKFIXHUB TRAILER BRAKE GUIDE

Trailer Brakes Won't Release or Wheels Stay Locked?

If the tractor releases normally but the trailer brakes stay applied, separate the complaint into air-supply, trailer-control and individual wheel-end symptoms before replacing parts.

Do not drag a locked trailer

Do not force the combination to move against brakes that remain applied. A dragging or locked brake can rapidly create dangerous heat, damage friction components and wheel-end parts, and create a fire risk. If a wheel is hot, smoking or smells burned, use the hot brake / smoking wheel guide.

First clues to record

  • Do all trailer brakes stay applied, or only one wheel or axle?
  • Do the tractor brakes release normally?
  • Are tractor primary and secondary air pressures normal?
  • Does the trailer air-supply control remain in the normal position?
  • Can you hear an air leak at the trailer, gladhand area, valve or chamber?
  • Did the problem begin after coupling to this trailer?
  • Did it begin in freezing or wet weather?
  • Was recent work performed on chambers, valves, hoses, brakes or wheel ends?

All trailer brakes stay applied

When multiple trailer brakes remain applied, technicians may need to verify that the trailer is receiving the correct air supply and that the trailer's supply, relay/emergency and spring-brake control circuits are operating as designed. The exact arrangement varies by trailer and brake system.

If the truck itself cannot build or maintain adequate air pressure, start with the won't build air pressure guide →

Only one wheel or axle stays locked

A complaint isolated to one wheel or axle follows a different path from all trailer brakes staying applied. Brake chamber operation, air delivery, foundation-brake components and the wheel end may require inspection.

Air leak while trying to release the trailer brakes

Record where the air is escaping and whether leakage changes when the service brakes are applied or the trailer supply is changed. Do not assume the nearest component is automatically the failed part.

Parking or spring brakes will not release

If the problem also affects the tractor parking brakes, or the complaint is primarily that spring brakes remain applied despite normal release commands, compare the symptoms with the dedicated parking-brake guide.

Problem started in freezing weather

Moisture and freezing conditions can change air-system behavior, but do not assume ice is the only cause. Record ambient conditions, air-dryer behavior and whether the problem changes after the system is properly serviced or warmed according to approved procedures.

What to tell the repair shop

  • Trailer type and which axle or wheel positions remain applied
  • Whether tractor brakes release normally
  • Primary and secondary air-pressure readings
  • Whether all trailer brakes or only one brake is affected
  • Any audible leak and its approximate location
  • Whether a wheel is hot, smoking or smells burned
  • Weather conditions when the problem began
  • Recent trailer brake, chamber, valve, hose or wheel-end repairs

Spring-brake safety

Spring-brake chambers contain powerful stored mechanical energy. Do not disassemble a spring-brake chamber or improvise a brake-release procedure. Heavy-duty brake systems are safety-critical; follow the applicable manufacturer procedures and use qualified service personnel. Never work beneath an unsecured vehicle.
