TRAILER AIR BRAKES · SPRING BRAKES · LOCKED WHEELS

Trailer brakes won't release or trailer wheels stay locked?

If the tractor releases normally but the trailer remains applied, first determine whether all trailer brakes, one axle or one wheel is affected. That distinction can prevent an air-supply problem from being confused with a localized brake or wheel-end problem.

Do not drag a locked trailer

Do not force the combination to move against brakes that remain applied. A dragging or locked brake can rapidly create dangerous heat, damage friction and wheel-end components, and create a fire risk.

Quick answer: what should you check first?

  1. Confirm the tractor brakes release normally.
  2. Check tractor primary and secondary air-pressure indications.
  3. Identify whether all trailer brakes, one axle or one wheel stays applied.
  4. Record whether the trailer air-supply control behaves normally.
  5. Listen for an obvious leak at the trailer, gladhand area, valve or chamber from a safe position.
  6. Record whether the problem began after coupling, service or freezing weather.

All trailer brakes stay applied

When multiple trailer brakes remain applied while the tractor releases, the trailer's air supply and control system become important diagnostic paths. Qualified diagnosis may include supply delivery through the coupling, trailer supply/emergency circuit, relay/emergency or spring-brake control components and trailer plumbing. Exact configurations vary.

Truck also has low air pressure

If the tractor cannot build or maintain adequate pressure, solve that system-wide air problem before treating the trailer as an isolated brake failure.

Air leaks while trying to release the trailer brakes

Record where the air is escaping and whether leakage changes when the service brakes are applied or trailer supply is changed. Do not assume the component nearest the sound is automatically the failed part.

Parking or spring brakes also won't release

If the problem is not isolated to the trailer, compare the symptom with the broader parking/spring-brake diagnostic path. Adequate air pressure and the location of the applied brakes are the first useful clues.

Problem began after hooking to this trailer

If the tractor operates normally with other trailers, record that fact. Check the coupling and trailer-specific supply/control path using the applicable service procedure rather than immediately replacing tractor components.

Problem started in freezing weather

Moisture and freezing conditions can change air-system behavior, but do not assume ice is the only cause. Record ambient conditions and air-dryer history and use approved cold-weather procedures. Do not use unsafe heat sources around brake components.

What not to assume

  • All trailer brakes locked does not automatically mean every brake chamber failed.
  • One locked wheel is different evidence from every trailer brake staying applied.
  • An air leak near a chamber does not automatically prove the chamber itself failed.
  • A problem isolated to one trailer can be valuable diagnostic evidence.
  • Do not replace tractor or trailer valves until the air-delivery problem is positively located.

Spring-brake safety

Spring-brake chambers contain powerful stored mechanical energy. Do not disassemble a spring-brake chamber or improvise a release procedure. Never work beneath an unsecured vehicle. Follow applicable vehicle, trailer and brake-component procedures and use qualified heavy-duty service personnel.
