Trailer brakes won't release

Separate the tractor from the trailer first. Then find out how much of the trailer is affected.

1️⃣ FIRST: Does the tractor release normally?

If the tractor has the same problem, move back to the main parking-brake or air-pressure path. If the tractor releases and the trailer does not, you have powerful evidence that the complaint is trailer-side or in the tractor-to-trailer path.

🚛 All trailer brakes stay applied

When every trailer brake is affected together, shared supply and control paths become more important than individual wheel components. Follow the air from tractor supply, through the coupling, into the trailer supply/control system.

👂 Air leaks while trying to release

Record where the sound is strongest—coupling area, trailer plumbing, valve area or wheel/chamber area—and whether it changes when trailer supply or service brakes are operated. Sound identifies an area, not automatically the failed component.

🔗 Only this trailer has the problem

If the tractor works normally with other trailers, save that comparison. It strongly shifts attention toward this trailer or its connection rather than random tractor component replacement.

❄️ Started in freezing weather

Cold can expose moisture or icing problems. Preserve weather timing and air-system history as evidence. Do not use improvised heat sources or unapproved chemicals around brake components.

⚠️ Spring brake stored energy

Never open, cut or disassemble a spring brake chamber. Never improvise a release or caging procedure. Follow the correct manufacturer procedure and qualified heavy-duty brake practices.

SEE IT → NAME IT → TEST IT → PROVE IT.

All trailer brakes locked points toward something shared. One wheel locked points local. That simple distinction prevents a lot of unnecessary parts replacement.

🛑 SAFETY: Do not drag a locked trailer. Applied brakes can rapidly overheat, damage tires and wheel-end components, and create a fire risk. Never crawl beneath an unsecured combination or bypass brake-protection systems. Verify exact trailer, brake and air-system procedures for the equipment being serviced.
