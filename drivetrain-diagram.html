👇 Diagnose by behavior

〰️ VIBRATION: Record road speed, engine RPM, gear and load. Rule out wheels/tires and wheel ends, then inspect shafts, U-joints, support bearings, slip components, phasing, runout and geometry.
🔊 CLUNK: Record whether it occurs taking off, changing from pull to coast, shifting or during suspension movement. Follow lash through joints, splines, yokes, output and axle rather than assuming the rear differential.
🔁 REPEAT FAILURE: Preserve the failed component as evidence. Check application, installation, yokes, adjoining components, shaft condition, support/slip parts, ride height and driveline geometry.
⚙️ NO TORQUE: Follow torque from clutch/transmission output through each shaft and axle connection until the first point where power stops transferring.

🧠 The driveline geometry firewall

1️⃣ Verify the truck is at the correct suspension ride height.
2️⃣ Verify shafts/components are in the correct position and phasing for the application.
3️⃣ Inspect U-joints, support bearings and slip sections before blaming angle alone.
4️⃣ Measure driveline angles/runout only by the exact component/vehicle manufacturer method.
5️⃣ Never substitute a generic internet angle specification for the exact driveline configuration.

More symptoms in this system

Use these focused checks alongside the system explanation above. Confirm the exact vehicle configuration and current manufacturer procedures before testing or repair.

⭕ CARRIER / SUPPORT BEARING

A damaged carrier bearing may be the victim of geometry, mounting or another driveline problem. Find out why it failed.

1️⃣ COMPLAINT: Record vibration/noise speed, load and whether the complaint changes on acceleration versus coast.
2️⃣ RUBBER SUPPORT: Inspect the support cushion for tearing, separation, distortion or evidence that the shaft is not centered normally.
3️⃣ BEARING: With the driveline safely secured, inspect for roughness, looseness and damage using the manufacturer's method.
4️⃣ BRACKET / MOUNT: Inspect mounting hardware, bracket condition and evidence of movement or misalignment.
5️⃣ SHAFT ALIGNMENT: Inspect adjoining shaft sections, U-joints, slip components and phasing. A support bearing cannot correct a driveline assembled or positioned incorrectly.
6️⃣ RIDE HEIGHT / ANGLES: Suspension ride height changes driveline geometry. Verify the vehicle sits at the correct specified height before final angle conclusions.
7️⃣ VERIFY: Correct the cause, not only the damaged bearing, then road-test through the original condition.
COMPLAINT → SUPPORT RUBBER → BEARING → MOUNT → SHAFT ALIGNMENT → RIDE HEIGHT / ANGLES → VERIFY.

🔁 U-JOINT KEEPS FAILING

A repeat failure means look beyond the replacement joint.

1️⃣ PRESERVE EVIDENCE: Note failed caps, lubrication, heat, rust dust, looseness and abnormal wear before cleaning.
2️⃣ APPLICATION / INSTALLATION: Confirm correct joint, retention hardware, yoke condition and exact installation procedure.
3️⃣ YOKES: Inspect bores/ears and retention surfaces for wear, distortion or cap movement.
4️⃣ SHAFT / PHASING: Inspect damage, runout evidence, balance weights and correct shaft phasing/configuration.
5️⃣ SUPPORT / SLIP: Carrier-bearing or slip-spline problems can transfer abnormal load into the joint.
6️⃣ RIDE HEIGHT / ANGLES: Verify specified ride height and driveline geometry using exact manufacturer guidance—not a generic universal angle.
7️⃣ ROOT CAUSE: Correct why the joint failed before returning the truck to the same operating condition.
FAILURE EVIDENCE → APPLICATION → YOKES → SHAFT / PHASING → SUPPORT / SLIP → RIDE HEIGHT / ANGLES → ROOT CAUSE.

〰️ DRIVESHAFT VIBRATION

Do not condemn the driveshaft just because the vibration is felt in the floor. First prove the complaint follows vehicle speed rather than engine RPM.

1️⃣ CLASSIFY IT: Record speed range, gear, acceleration, steady pull, coast and whether changing engine RPM at the same road speed changes the vibration.
2️⃣ RULE OUT WHEELS / TIRES / WHEEL ENDS: Road-speed vibration commonly begins outside the driveshaft. Inspect tire condition, wheel mounting, runout and wheel-end condition before deeper driveline work.
3️⃣ VISUAL DRIVELINE INSPECTION: With the vehicle safely secured, inspect shaft tubes for dents/damage, missing balance weights, obvious runout evidence and incorrect component position.
4️⃣ U-JOINTS / SUPPORT: Inspect U-joints and carrier/support bearings for play, binding, deterioration, loose mounting or other evidence using the component manufacturer's procedure.
5️⃣ PHASING / ANGLES / RIDE HEIGHT: Incorrect shaft phasing, driveline geometry or ride height can create vibration even when individual components appear usable. Verify exact configuration and manufacturer guidance.
6️⃣ MEASURE BEFORE REPLACING: Runout, angles and related measurements should be made with approved methods for the exact driveline.
7️⃣ VERIFY: Road-test through the original speed/load window after correction.
ROAD SPEED? → WHEELS / TIRES → SHAFT CONDITION → U-JOINTS / SUPPORT → PHASING / ANGLES → MEASURE → VERIFY.
⚠️ Never inspect or measure near a rotating driveshaft. Secure and support the vehicle and follow current driveline manufacturer procedures.

↔️ SLIP YOKE / SPLINE PROBLEM

The slip section must transmit torque while allowing the driveline to change length with suspension and chassis movement.

1️⃣ DEFINE THE SYMPTOM: Clunk, bind, stick-slip, vibration or visible spline wear? Record when it happens.
2️⃣ INSPECT ENGAGEMENT: Verify the shaft/slip assembly is the correct application and operates within the manufacturer’s engagement/travel requirements.
3️⃣ CHECK WEAR / PLAY: Inspect spline condition and rotational/radial looseness by the component manufacturer's procedure.
4️⃣ CHECK FOR BINDING: Corrosion, damage, contamination or incorrect service can prevent smooth slip movement.
5️⃣ LUBRICATION: If the design is serviceable/lubricated, use the exact lubricant and procedure specified for that component. Do not assume every slip design is serviced the same way.
6️⃣ SUSPENSION / RIDE HEIGHT: Incorrect ride height or suspension geometry can force the slip section into abnormal travel or load.
7️⃣ VERIFY: Correct the root cause and reproduce the original torque-change or suspension-travel condition.
SYMPTOM → ENGAGEMENT / TRAVEL → WEAR → BINDING → CORRECT SERVICE → RIDE HEIGHT → VERIFY.

🔩 U-JOINT PROBLEM

U-joints can create clunk, vibration, squeak, heat or driveline failure. Look for evidence instead of judging only by whether the joint visibly wiggles.

1️⃣ DEFINE THE COMPLAINT: Takeoff clunk, road-speed vibration, squeak, shudder or visible failure? Record load and speed.
2️⃣ SECURE THE VEHICLE: Prevent movement and remove driveline torque according to the correct service procedure before touching the shaft.
3️⃣ CHECK PLAY AND BINDING: Inspect the joint through its usable movement. A joint may fail by looseness or by stiffness/binding.
4️⃣ LOOK FOR EVIDENCE: Rust-colored dust, failed seals, dry caps, displaced retaining hardware, heat discoloration or a cap moving in the yoke are important clues.
5️⃣ INSPECT YOKES / FASTENERS: A damaged yoke or incorrect/loose retention can ruin a new joint. Do not treat the cross-and-bearing assembly as the only possible failure.
6️⃣ CHECK THE REST OF THE DRIVELINE: Support bearing, slip spline, shaft damage, angles and phasing can load a U-joint abnormally and cause repeat failure.
7️⃣ VERIFY: Use exact component procedures and fastener requirements, then reproduce the original condition.
COMPLAINT → SECURE → PLAY / BINDING → FAILURE EVIDENCE → YOKE / RETENTION → DRIVELINE GEOMETRY → VERIFY.
⚠️ A failed driveline can cause severe injury and vehicle damage. Never work around a rotating shaft or under an unsupported vehicle.
