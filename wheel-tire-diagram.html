👇 PICK THE COMPLAINT

〰️ The truck vibrates at road speed

  1. Record the speed where vibration begins, peaks and disappears.
  2. Inspect tire inflation, damage, unusual wear, matching and obvious wheel condition.
  3. Check wheel / tire runout or balance concerns using the proper equipment and procedure.
  4. If the wheel assemblies check out, inspect wheel ends, suspension and steering.
  5. If vibration follows driveline load or persists beyond the wheel area, continue into drivetrain diagnosis.

📉 One tire or axle is wearing strangely

  1. Identify the wear pattern and which tires are affected.
  2. Verify cold inflation pressure and tire/load suitability.
  3. Inspect for looseness or wear in wheel-end, steering and suspension components.
  4. Check ride height and alignment only after worn or loose mechanical parts are addressed.
  5. Correct the cause before installing a replacement tire or the wear may return.

🔥 One wheel / tire is much hotter than the others

  1. Stop in a safe location and do not continue driving until the cause is understood.
  2. Compare tire inflation and look for damage or overload signs without touching dangerously hot components.
  3. Check for brake drag and wheel-end / bearing trouble using safe inspection procedures.
  4. Inspect hub lubricant condition / level where applicable according to manufacturer instructions.
  5. If heat is severe, smoke is present, or the wheel end is loose, keep the vehicle out of service until repaired.

📚 Read what the tire is telling you

The tire is where several systems meet the road. Inflation, load, wheel condition, wheel-end movement, steering, suspension, alignment and operating conditions can all leave evidence there. The goal is to connect the visible pattern to a proven cause before replacing the tire.

〰️ Vibration: speed and load narrow the search

A vibration that appears in a repeatable road-speed range points differently than one that changes mainly with engine RPM or driveline load. Record where it begins, where it is strongest, and whether acceleration, coast or braking changes it. That pattern helps separate wheel/tire concerns from driveline, brake or engine-related sources.

📉 Wear pattern: describe it before interpreting it

Record which tire, which axle and which part of the tread is affected. Compare neighboring tires and note inflation history, load, rotation or position changes where known. Similar-looking wear can have different causes, so use the pattern as evidence alongside mechanical inspection and measurements.

💨 Repeated air loss needs a source

Repeatedly adding air treats the symptom, not the leak. Inspect the tire, valve hardware, wheel/rim and sealing areas using an approved procedure. A tire that has operated significantly underinflated may require further inspection even after the leak source is found.

🔥 Heat: tire, brake or bearing?

One hot wheel position can involve inflation/load problems, brake drag or wheel-end trouble. Compare similar wheel positions after comparable operation and look for the evidence that separates them. Smoke, severe heat, damage or wheel-end looseness are reasons to keep the vehicle out of service until the cause is corrected.

🔩 Mounting matters

Wheel fasteners and mounting surfaces are part of the assembly, not an afterthought. Dirt, damage, improper seating, damaged hardware or incorrect installation can create serious problems. Follow the applicable wheel and vehicle manufacturer procedures for inspection, tightening sequence, torque and recheck requirements.

📐 Alignment cannot fix looseness

Before aligning a truck for a tire-wear complaint, inspect wheel ends, steering linkage, kingpins, suspension joints and axle location. If the hardware can move when it should not, alignment values may not remain meaningful after the mechanical problem is repaired.

TruckFixHub diagnostic chain: identify tire/wheel position → record wear/heat/vibration/air-loss pattern → verify inflation and condition → inspect wheel and fasteners → inspect wheel end/steering/suspension → measure as required → repair proven cause → verify under comparable conditions.
