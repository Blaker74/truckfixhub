👇 Follow the complaint

〰️ HIGHWAY VIBRATION: Record the exact speed where it starts, peaks and changes. Inspect pressure, tire condition, matching and wheel condition. If wheel/tire checks pass, move inward to wheel ends, steering/suspension and driveline.
📉 UNEVEN / CUPPED WEAR: Describe which tire, axle and tread area is affected before interpreting the pattern. Verify inflation and load suitability, then inspect wheel condition, wheel ends, steering, suspension and axle location before alignment.
💨 REPEATED AIR LOSS: Find the actual leak at the tire, valve hardware, wheel/rim or sealing area using an approved procedure. Repeated inflation is not a repair. A tire operated significantly underinflated may require further inspection.
🔥 HOT WHEEL POSITION: Stop safely. Compare similar wheel positions after comparable operation. Separate inflation/load trouble from brake drag and wheel-end/bearing trouble. Severe heat, smoke, damage or looseness means the truck stays out of service.
🔩 REPEAT LOOSE WHEEL FASTENERS: Remove the truck from service. Identify the wheel system, then inspect studs, nuts, wheel condition and mounting surfaces and verify the exact installation procedure. Repeated retightening is not a root-cause repair.

🧠 Before buying tires or ordering an alignment

1️⃣ VERIFY PRESSURE CORRECTLY. Use the appropriate cold-inflation/load information for the tire and application rather than one universal pressure assumption.
2️⃣ COMPARE POSITIONS. One tire, one side, one axle or every tire tells a different story.
3️⃣ CHECK MECHANICAL CONDITION FIRST. Alignment cannot permanently correct loose wheel ends, worn steering parts, damaged suspension or incorrect axle location.
4️⃣ MEASURE WHEN NEEDED. Runout, balance, alignment and mechanical movement are measurement problems—not eyeball-only diagnoses.
5️⃣ VERIFY THE ORIGINAL CONDITION. Recheck vibration at the original road speed or compare wear/temperature/pressure under similar operation after repair.

📉 TIRE CUPPING / SCALLOPED WEAR

The wear pattern is evidence. Do not jump straight to alignment.

1️⃣ MAP THE PATTERN: Record tire position, tread area affected, repeating pattern and whether one tire, one axle or multiple positions are involved.
2️⃣ VERIFY INFLATION / LOAD: Confirm appropriate cold inflation for the tire and actual application/load.
3️⃣ CHECK TIRE / WHEEL CONDITION: Inspect matching, balance concerns, wheel condition and runout where appropriate.
4️⃣ CHECK WHEEL END: Bearing looseness or other wheel-end movement can contribute to abnormal tire behavior.
5️⃣ CHECK STEERING / SUSPENSION: Inspect mechanical looseness, worn components, damping and axle-location conditions before paying for alignment.
6️⃣ ALIGNMENT LAST, NOT FIRST: Once mechanical condition is proven sound, measure alignment to the exact vehicle/application procedure.
7️⃣ VERIFY OVER TIME: Correcting the root cause stops new abnormal wear; it does not restore tread already worn away.
MAP WEAR → INFLATION / LOAD → TIRE / WHEEL → WHEEL END → STEERING / SUSPENSION → ALIGNMENT MEASUREMENT → VERIFY.

💨 TIRE KEEPS LOSING AIR

Repeated inflation is not a repair. Find where the air is leaving and determine whether the assembly is safe to return to service.

1️⃣ IDENTIFY THE POSITION: Record axle, side, inner/outer dual position, cold pressure and how quickly pressure drops.
2️⃣ INSPECT THE TIRE: Look for penetrations, cuts, casing damage, irregular wear or other visible evidence. Use an approved leak-detection method.
3️⃣ CHECK VALVE HARDWARE: Inspect valve stem, core, extension and related sealing hardware. Do not assume the tread is leaking.
4️⃣ CHECK WHEEL / RIM SEALING AREAS: Inspect the wheel or rim condition and applicable bead/sealing surfaces using the correct wheel-system procedure.
5️⃣ CONSIDER PRIOR UNDERINFLATION: A commercial tire that has been operated significantly underinflated can have internal damage not proven safe by simply restoring pressure.
6️⃣ VERIFY: After an approved repair, establish the correct cold inflation pressure for the load/application and confirm the pressure remains stable.
POSITION → PRESSURE LOSS RATE → TIRE → VALVE → WHEEL / RIM → UNDERINFLATION HISTORY → APPROVED REPAIR → VERIFY.
⚠️ Commercial tire and wheel assemblies can store tremendous energy. Do not service a damaged or suspect assembly while pressurized. Follow current tire, wheel and vehicle manufacturer procedures and use trained personnel and required restraining equipment.

🔩 WHEEL NUTS KEEP COMING LOOSE

Repeat wheel-fastener looseness is a serious condition. Do not treat repeated retightening as the repair.

1️⃣ REMOVE FROM SERVICE: If wheel fasteners are loose, missing or show movement evidence, stop operation until the assembly is properly inspected.
2️⃣ IDENTIFY THE WHEEL SYSTEM: Confirm wheel type, mounting system and exact fastener/application before applying any torque or installation procedure.
3️⃣ INSPECT STUDS / NUTS: Look for damaged threads, stretching, corrosion, wrong hardware or other evidence that prevents proper clamping.
4️⃣ INSPECT WHEEL / MOUNTING SURFACES: Check for damage, wear, distortion, contamination or evidence of wheel movement at the mounting interface.
5️⃣ VERIFY INSTALLATION METHOD: Follow the wheel/vehicle manufacturer's sequence, lubrication condition where specified, torque method and any required recheck procedure. Do not use a universal torque assumption.
6️⃣ FIND WHY IT REPEATED: Incorrect parts, damaged mounting surfaces, improper installation or component damage must be corrected before the truck returns to service.
OUT OF SERVICE → IDENTIFY SYSTEM → STUDS / NUTS → WHEEL / MOUNTING → EXACT INSTALLATION PROCEDURE → ROOT CAUSE → VERIFY.
⚠️ Wheel separation can cause catastrophic injury or death. Use trained personnel and current wheel, hub and vehicle manufacturer procedures for inspection, replacement and torque requirements.
