👇 Follow the complaint

〰️ HIGHWAY VIBRATION: Record the exact speed where it starts, peaks and changes. Inspect pressure, tire condition, matching and wheel condition. If wheel/tire checks pass, move inward to wheel ends, steering/suspension and driveline.
📉 UNEVEN / CUPPED WEAR: Describe which tire, axle and tread area is affected before interpreting the pattern. Verify inflation and load suitability, then inspect wheel condition, wheel ends, steering, suspension and axle location before alignment.
💨 REPEATED AIR LOSS: Find the actual leak at the tire, valve hardware, wheel/rim or sealing area using an approved procedure. Repeated inflation is not a repair. A tire operated significantly underinflated may require further inspection.
🔥 HOT WHEEL POSITION: Stop safely. Compare similar wheel positions after comparable operation. Separate inflation/load trouble from brake drag and wheel-end/bearing trouble. Severe heat, smoke, damage or looseness means the truck stays out of service.
🔩 REPEAT LOOSE WHEEL FASTENERS: Remove the truck from service. Identify the wheel system, then inspect studs, nuts, wheel condition and mounting surfaces and verify the exact installation procedure. Repeated retightening is not a root-cause repair.

🧠 Before buying tires or ordering an alignment

1️⃣ VERIFY PRESSURE CORRECTLY. Use the appropriate cold-inflation/load information for the tire and application rather than one universal pressure assumption.
2️⃣ COMPARE POSITIONS. One tire, one side, one axle or every tire tells a different story.
3️⃣ CHECK MECHANICAL CONDITION FIRST. Alignment cannot permanently correct loose wheel ends, worn steering parts, damaged suspension or incorrect axle location.
4️⃣ MEASURE WHEN NEEDED. Runout, balance, alignment and mechanical movement are measurement problems—not eyeball-only diagnoses.
5️⃣ VERIFY THE ORIGINAL CONDITION. Recheck vibration at the original road speed or compare wear/temperature/pressure under similar operation after repair.
