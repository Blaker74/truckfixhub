Delco Remy Starters & Alternators Made Simple

Do not replace the starter because it will not crank or the alternator because the batteries are low. Prove the batteries, cables, control circuit and actual component performance first.

STARTERS

39MT · 38MT & Heavy-Duty Starting

The Delco Remy 39MT family is widely used in heavy-duty applications. Current all-makes 39MT literature highlights Integral Magnetic Switch (IMS), Over Crank Protection (OCP) on applicable units and a rotatable-flange design on applicable replacement starters.

Simple idea: the starter needs high current at the motor and a strong control signal at the solenoid. A weak cable, bad ground or control-voltage loss can imitate a bad starter.

38MT and other Delco Remy models exist in multiple voltage, mounting, pinion, rotation and protection configurations. Match the exact application/part specification.

ALTERNATORS

40SI · 55SI & Heavy-Duty Charging

Delco Remy 40SI and 55SI families include high-output heavy-duty alternators. Individual applications vary in voltage, amperage, mount and terminals; current catalog examples include 12V and 24V 40SI units and higher-output 55SI upgrade options.

Simple idea: the alternator can be working correctly at its output terminal while cable resistance keeps the batteries from receiving the same voltage. Measure both ends.

Remote Sense is standard/available on several premium Delco Remy families and is used to compensate for voltage loss between alternator and battery/distribution point.

Always Start at the Batteries

A starter and alternator both depend on the battery system. Before condemning either component:

  1. Inspect battery condition and connections.
  2. Verify batteries are adequately charged.
  3. Load/conductance-test as appropriate.
  4. Check battery interconnects on multi-battery systems.
  5. Inspect positive and ground paths.

Delco Remy charging diagnostics specifically begin at the batteries.

📉

Voltage Drop — The Test That Finds Hidden Resistance

A cable can look perfect and still lose voltage under load. Measure voltage drop while the circuit is actually working.

Starting side: test positive path and ground path while cranking.

Charging side: compare alternator output with voltage delivered to the batteries. Delco Remy Remote Sense troubleshooting uses a maximum combined charging-cable loss guideline of about 0.5 V at full rated alternator output.

Rule: resistance under load matters more than appearance.

🧲

IMS — Integral Magnetic Switch

On applicable Delco Remy starters, IMS is a relay mounted at the starter. Its job is to reduce voltage drop in the starter control circuit so the solenoid receives stronger available voltage.

Important: Delco Remy specifically warns not to remove the IMS from starters designed with it. Diagnose the vehicle start signal, IMS connections/ground and solenoid circuit as a system.

🌡️

OCP — Over Crank Protection

OCP protects applicable starters from thermal damage caused by excessive cranking. Standard OCP versions use a built-in circuit breaker that resets after safe temperature returns; Delco Remy notes that standard OCP requires the proper customer wiring connection.

Some applications use integrated overcrank protection (iOCP), which differs from standard OCP wiring.

Do not bypass protection to make a starter crank longer.

🔑

No Crank

  1. Verify batteries and system voltage.
  2. Check high-current positive cable and grounds under load.
  3. Confirm start command reaches the starter/IMS circuit.
  4. Check solenoid operation.
  5. Determine whether starter pinion/motor is engaging and turning.
  6. Rule out engine/flywheel mechanical problems.

No click and clicks but will not crank are different diagnostic paths.

🐢

Slow Crank

Slow crank can be weak batteries, excessive cable/ground resistance, starter condition or excessive mechanical load.

Measure battery voltage and cable drop during the event. Do not install a starter simply because cranking sounds slow.

⚙️

Starter Spins but Engine Does Not

Separate motor rotation from pinion engagement. Inspect the starter drive/pinion and flywheel/ring-gear engagement using the exact service procedure. Confirm the correct starter application, pinion and mounting configuration.

A replacement that physically bolts on is not automatically the correct starter.

📡

Remote Sense — What It Does

Remote Sense monitors voltage at the batteries or common distribution point instead of judging system voltage only at the alternator. The regulator can increase alternator output voltage enough to compensate for cable loss.

Delco Remy identifies the Remote Sense connection as the Remote Sense/S terminal. The R and I terminals are not the Remote Sense terminal.

Simple idea: the alternator asks, “What voltage actually made it to the battery?”

🔌

Remote Sense Wiring

Delco Remy instructions state that when adding a Remote Sense alternator to a vehicle without an existing sense lead, the sense circuit can be connected through a fused lead to battery positive/common distribution according to its installation instructions.

Do not guess terminal identity. Verify the alternator label/instruction sheet before connecting auxiliary wires.

⬇️

Low Charging Voltage

  1. Start with charged/tested batteries.
  2. Inspect belt/drive condition where applicable.
  3. Measure alternator voltage directly at B+ and ground.
  4. Measure battery voltage at the same operating condition.
  5. Voltage-drop test positive and negative charging paths.
  6. Inspect Remote Sense wiring if equipped.
  7. Only then judge alternator performance.
⬆️

Alternator Voltage Higher Than Battery Voltage

On a Remote Sense system, higher voltage at the alternator than at the battery can be the regulator compensating for cable resistance. Delco Remy notes that alternator-terminal voltage can be higher while the battery remains in the normal charging range.

The important question is why the voltage is being lost between the alternator and batteries.

💡

Charging Light / Intermittent Charge

Check battery/cable foundation, alternator connections, grounds, belt/drive, control/indicator wiring and Remote Sense circuit where equipped. Wiggle/routing evidence, heat damage and rubbed wiring can matter.

An intermittent wiring problem can disappear on a bench test, so capture voltage at the truck during the complaint when possible.

📦

Before Ordering a Starter

  • Original Delco Remy part number whenever available.
  • 12V or 24V system.
  • Starter model/family.
  • Mount type and mounting-hole configuration.
  • Pinion teeth/pitch.
  • Rotation/polarity.
  • Solenoid/IMS position and clearance.
  • OCP/iOCP requirement and vehicle wiring.
  • Engine/application and VIN information.

Cross by exact application—not “it looks like a 39MT.”

📦

Before Ordering an Alternator

  • Original part number.
  • 12V or 24V.
  • Rated amperage/output requirement.
  • Mount style and dimensions.
  • Pulley/drive configuration.
  • B+ and ground configuration.
  • R/I/Remote Sense terminal requirements.
  • Vehicle electrical-load/application requirements.

A higher-amperage unit may be an approved upgrade in some applications, but verify Delco Remy application/upgrade information before substitution.

Quick Problem Finder

One click, no crank

Load-test batteries and voltage-drop the high-current positive/ground paths before condemning the starter.

No click at starter

Trace the start command through vehicle controls and IMS/solenoid circuit. High-current starter cables alone cannot create the command.

Starter quits after long crank

If equipped with OCP, thermal protection may have opened. Find the reason the engine requires excessive cranking instead of bypassing protection.

New starter still cranks slow

Return to batteries, cable size/connections, grounds and voltage drop. A new motor cannot overcome a poor current path.

Alternator tests good, batteries stay low

Measure charging voltage at alternator and battery simultaneously and voltage-drop the cable/ground path. Check Remote Sense if equipped.

Remote Sense wire damaged

Repair/route it according to the exact Delco Remy/vehicle instructions. Do not move it to the R or I terminal.

Easy memory rules

NO CRANK: BATTERY → CABLES/GROUNDS → COMMAND/IMS → SOLENOID → STARTER → ENGINE.

NO CHARGE: BATTERY → DRIVE → ALTERNATOR OUTPUT → CABLE DROP → REMOTE SENSE/CONTROL → VERIFY.

⚠️ Starting & Charging Safety

Heavy-duty battery systems can deliver extremely high current. Disconnect/isolate power according to the vehicle and Delco Remy procedure before component removal. Prevent tools from bridging B+ to ground. Keep clear of belts and rotating components during running charging tests. Never bypass starter thermal protection or defeat required circuit protection.
