Truck Air Leak: What to Record Before Replacing Parts

A practical first-check guide to identifying when and where a heavy-duty truck air system loses pressure, without guessing at valves.

Purpose: Gather useful observations and component information before ordering parts. This is not a diagnosis of any particular truck.

1. Start with the conditions

Record whether the loss occurs with the engine off or running, parking brakes applied or released, and service brakes applied or released. Note whether the leak changes when the suspension height changes. These differences can help a qualified technician isolate a circuit.

2. Record the gauges and warning behavior

Write down the indicated primary and secondary air pressures, the approximate time and circumstances of pressure loss, and any audible warning. Do not assume both gauges represent the same circuit on every vehicle.

3. Locate the sound without dismantling

From a safe position, note whether the sound appears near the cab, tanks, suspension, trailer connections, or a wheel end. An audible leak may travel along a line; the loudest point is not necessarily the failed component.

4. Identify the assembly before ordering

Capture the vehicle VIN, the component maker's tag, port orientation, tubing size and photos of connected lines. Similar valves can have different functions and specifications. Verify application in current manufacturer documentation.

5. Separate tractor and trailer observations

Record whether the symptom occurs with a trailer connected and what changes when the configuration changes. Do not disconnect pressurized fittings or improvise tests; use approved procedures performed by qualified personnel.

6. Verify the repair

After the root cause is confirmed and repaired, a qualified technician should complete the manufacturer's pressure-loss, warning, brake-function and roadworthiness checks before returning the vehicle to service.

Safety first: Park securely and follow the vehicle manufacturer's safety procedures. Never work under an unsupported vehicle or release pressurized systems. Brake and electrical work may require trained personnel and specialized equipment. Do not operate a vehicle with an unresolved safety-critical defect.
