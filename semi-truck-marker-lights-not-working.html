TRUCKFIXHUB LIGHTING GUIDE

Semi Truck Marker or Clearance Lights Not Working?

Start by identifying whether one lamp, one side, the tractor, the trailer or every marker and clearance lamp is affected.

Required lighting matters

Marker and clearance lighting helps other road users identify the vehicle at night and in low visibility. Required lighting faults should be repaired according to applicable inspection requirements and manufacturer procedures before continued operation where the lighting is required.

First clues

  • One lamp, one side or all lamps?
  • Tractor, trailer or both?
  • Do tail, brake and turn lamps work?
  • Does the fault change with another trailer?
  • Is a fuse repeatedly opening?
  • Any wet, loose, corroded or damaged connectors?

One marker light is out

Begin near the affected lamp. Depending on the system, inspect the bulb or LED assembly, connector, local wiring, ground path, corrosion, moisture and physical damage.

Several lights are out together

A group failure can point toward a shared feed, connector, ground, circuit protection or harness section. Use the correct wiring information rather than assuming every lamp failed at once.

Trailer marker lights are out

Compare other trailer lighting and inspect the tractor-to-trailer connection and trailer-side circuits. Whether the symptom follows a particular trailer is useful diagnostic information but should be confirmed with proper testing.

Fuse keeps blowing

Do not install a higher-rated fuse or bypass protection. Repeated fuse failure requires diagnosis for excessive current, damaged wiring or a short circuit.

Electrical note

TruckFixHub provides educational troubleshooting information. Heavy-duty lighting circuits vary by manufacturer and configuration. Use correct diagrams, protection and qualified repair procedures.
