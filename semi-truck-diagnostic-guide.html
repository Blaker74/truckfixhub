Semi Truck Diagnostic Guide

Start with what the truck is actually doing. Heavy-duty problems that sound alike can come from completely different systems. Separate the symptom, record when it happens, inspect the basics, measure the system and verify the repair before buying parts.

Use current manufacturer service information for exact specifications, torque procedures, electrical values, safety procedures and application-specific repair decisions.

Starting, charging & electrical problems

When a truck will not start, first separate no crank from cranks but will not start. That single observation changes the diagnostic path.

Diesel engine performance, air & fuel

Low power can involve air restriction, charge-air leaks, turbo control, fuel supply, engine condition, emissions derate or drivetrain behavior. Avoid treating “low power” as a parts diagnosis.

DPF, DEF, SCR & engine derate

Modern diesel aftertreatment complaints should be diagnosed as a system. A warning light or sensor code can be the result of another condition upstream or downstream.

Cooling system & overheating

Overheating diagnosis depends on operating condition. Note whether temperature rises at idle, highway speed, under load or during a pull and whether coolant is being lost or pushed from the reservoir.

Air brakes & compressed air

Air complaints can involve supply, control or delivery. Record reservoir pressure, build time, whether leakage occurs parked or applied, and which circuit loses pressure.

Driveline, transmission & vibration

For vibration and driveline complaints, determine whether frequency follows engine RPM or road speed and whether it changes under acceleration, coast or braking.
