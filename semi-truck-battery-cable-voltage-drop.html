🔌 BATTERY CABLE VOLTAGE DROP

A cable can pass an ohmmeter check and still fail under starter current. Voltage drop tests the path while it is working.

1️⃣ LOAD THE CIRCUIT: Perform the test under the manufacturer-specified load or crank condition. Without current flow, a voltage-drop test tells little.
2️⃣ POSITIVE PATH: Measure across the relevant positive path and then divide the circuit into smaller sections to locate excessive loss.
3️⃣ GROUND PATH: Test the return side under the same loaded condition. Battery-to-frame, frame-to-engine and direct grounds vary by application.
4️⃣ FIND THE DROP: Move meter leads across individual connections, disconnects, terminals and cable sections. The excessive loss identifies where resistance is consuming available voltage.
5️⃣ INSPECT THE CAUSE: Look for corrosion, loose hardware, damaged strands, internal cable corrosion, heat discoloration or poor contact surfaces.
6️⃣ VERIFY: Repair the connection/cable correctly and repeat the loaded test plus the original start/charge complaint.
APPLY LOAD → TEST WHOLE PATH → SPLIT INTO SECTIONS → FIND EXCESSIVE DROP → REPAIR → RETEST.
⚠️ Use the exact vehicle manufacturer’s allowable voltage-drop specifications. Avoid shorting meter probes across high-current circuits.
