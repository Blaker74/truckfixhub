🔌 BATTERY CABLE VOLTAGE DROP

Visual workshop guide • Updated October 24, 2026

Use this guide for a slow-crank or no-crank complaint where a technician needs to locate resistance in the starter supply and return paths. It explains probe placement; the vehicle and starter service procedure determines the test load and acceptance limit.

Set up safely: Park in neutral, apply the parking brake, chock the wheels and prevent unintended starting according to the chassis procedure. Wear eye protection, remove jewelry, and keep test leads away from belts and exhaust. Stop for leaking, swollen or damaged batteries. High-current load testing requires trained personnel and a suitably rated tester.

Test → isolate → repair

1. Identify the system before loading it

Record nominal system voltage, starter model and battery layout. Series/parallel switching and separate battery banks need their own wiring procedure. Test battery condition first; resting voltage alone does not establish cranking capability.

2. Measure both paths

With the meter on DC volts, test battery positive to starter B+; then starter ground to battery negative. Keep the prescribed load and test conditions consistent. Remove the load before repositioning leads.

Document: positive loss, negative loss, applied load and total loss. Delco Remy’s referenced heavy-duty guide specifies a 500 A carbon-pile test and a 0.5 V total limit for its procedure. Confirm applicability before using those values; they are not a universal truck specification.

3. Locate the connection consuming voltage

On the failing path, measure across each accessible connection under the same approved load: battery post to clamp, clamp to cable lug, disconnect switch input to output, and cable end to cable end. A reading across a connection is the loss at that connection. Avoid piercing insulation; use approved access points.

FindingNext action
High positive-side lossInspect the positive path, including battery connections and disconnects. Subdivide it before replacing a cable.
High ground-side lossTrace the actual return route on the chassis schematic. Inspect engine, frame and battery bonds in that route.
Near-zero loss but no starter currentThis does not prove the cable is good. Verify test load and starter command; an unloaded circuit can hide resistance.
Both paths pass but complaint remainsContinue battery capability, starter command and mechanical-load diagnosis. A passed cable test does not identify the failed component.

4. Repair the proven fault

De-energize the system using the chassis isolation procedure before loosening hardware. Clean serviceable contact surfaces, replace damaged terminals or internally corroded cables, and use the correct cable size, routing and fastener torque. Do not tighten by feel or substitute a smaller cable.

5. Repeat the test and original complaint

Repeat both loaded measurements after repair. Test under the temperature conditions that caused the failure. Record before/after readings and confirm normal starting before closing the job.
