🛢️ OIL IN CHARGE-AIR PIPING

Oil residue alone does not prove a failed turbo. Judge the amount, source and related symptoms.

1️⃣ QUANTIFY: Light film, wet pooling, repeated accumulation? Compare with manufacturer inspection criteria when available.
2️⃣ CHECK OIL CONSUMPTION: Is engine oil level actually dropping? Record consumption over a meaningful interval instead of relying on one visual observation.
3️⃣ TRACE THE SOURCE: Inspect the inlet, crankcase ventilation/breather system where applicable, compressor outlet and downstream charge-air path.
4️⃣ TURBO CONDITIONS: Turbo oil leakage can be influenced by lubrication, drain, crankcase pressure, operating conditions and turbo hardware. Diagnose the system, not just the wet pipe.
5️⃣ ENGINE CONDITION: Excessive crankcase pressure or other engine problems can change oil movement through the air system. Follow OEM tests if evidence points there.
6️⃣ VERIFY: Clean/service as directed, correct the proven source and monitor recurrence plus actual oil consumption.
AMOUNT → OIL CONSUMPTION → SOURCE → BREATHER / ENGINE CONDITIONS → TURBO CONDITIONS → PROVE → MONITOR.
⚠️ Significant oil ingestion can create serious engine risk. If oil accumulation is heavy or the engine shows abnormal runaway-like behavior, stop operation and follow manufacturer procedures.
