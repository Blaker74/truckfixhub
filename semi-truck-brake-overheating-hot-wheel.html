Heat tells you where to look. It does not prove which part failed.

🦶 Gets hot after brake use

Compare the affected position with the others and note whether the brake seems to release after the pedal is released. Brake application/release problems, foundation-brake conditions and wheel-end concerns can produce overlapping symptoms.

💨 Smoke, severe odor or extreme heat

Stop safely. Do not continue driving just to see whether it cools down. Overheated brake and wheel-end components can damage friction material, tires, seals, hubs and bearings and can create a fire risk.

🔥🔥 Several wheels are hot

Several positions heating together changes the diagnostic direction. Record whether they share an axle, tractor/trailer circuit or recent control event. Multiple brakes affected together can point toward something shared rather than several unrelated wheel failures.

✋ Never diagnose heat by touch

Use safe inspection methods. Brake drums, rotors, hubs and nearby components can cause severe burns even when they no longer look hot.

A hot wheel can come from a dragging brake or another wheel-end condition. Prove the source before replacing parts.

🛑 SAFETY: If a wheel end is smoking, extremely hot, braking has changed, or the vehicle is difficult to control, stop in a safe location and do not continue operating it. Do not touch overheated components or crawl beneath an unsecured vehicle. Brake and wheel-end inspection must follow the correct manufacturer procedures and applicable requirements.
