🧰 Five Trailer Drawers

1 · TRACTOR OUTPUTBefore condemning the trailer, prove the tractor is supplying the required electrical or pneumatic input at the connection.
2 · CONNECTIONInspect the J560/electrical connection, air lines, glad hands and other interfaces for damage, contamination, looseness or poor contact.
3 · TRAILER PATHFollow the circuit, air path or mechanical path toward the affected area instead of jumping directly to the end component.
4 · COMPONENTTest the lamp, valve, chamber, sensor, wheel end, suspension part or accessory only after its inputs have been verified.
5 · RESPONSEVerify the trailer actually responds correctly under the same condition that produced the original complaint.

🔧 SEE IT → NAME IT → TEST IT → PROVE IT

SEE ITRecord exactly what fails, which side/axle/lamp is affected, whether the complaint changes when connected to another tractor, and any codes or warning lamps.
NAME ITRoute the complaint to electrical, air/brake, ABS, wheel end, suspension or accessory systems.
TEST ITCheck the supply at the tractor-trailer interface, then work downstream until the evidence changes.
PROVE ITRepair the supported cause and verify operation at the complete tractor-trailer level—not just at the replaced component.

🧠 Trailer Clues That Save Time

Everything electrical is dead: begin at tractor supply and the main connection before chasing individual trailer circuits.

Only one lamp or function is dead: compare working and failed circuits and work locally after confirming the shared supply.

Brake problem follows the trailer to another tractor: that is useful evidence pointing the diagnosis toward the trailer side.

ABS warning: capture the actual code and configuration before assuming a wheel-speed sensor is bad.

One wheel is hot: treat it as a wheel-end/brake safety complaint and separate brake drag from bearing/lubrication causes.

⚠️ TRAILER SAFETY & EXACT INFORMATION

Trailer brakes, ABS, wheel ends, suspension, electrical systems and liftgates can create serious hazards. Chock and support equipment correctly and isolate stored energy before service. Exact wiring, pin functions, brake adjustment, torque, bearing setup, air-system limits and configuration vary by equipment. Verify current manufacturer information for the exact trailer and components.
