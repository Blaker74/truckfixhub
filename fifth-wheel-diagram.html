🚛 FIFTH WHEEL

A safe coupling must be lined up, locked and verified.

1️⃣ TRAILER APPROACH
Correct trailer height and alignment allow the fifth wheel to engage the upper coupler correctly
↓
2️⃣ FIFTH WHEEL PLATE
Supports the trailer upper coupler and guides the kingpin toward the throat
↓
3️⃣ KINGPIN ENTERS THROAT
The kingpin must enter the locking mechanism in the proper position
↓
4️⃣ JAWS / LOCK ENGAGE
The locking mechanism captures the kingpin according to the fifth-wheel design
↓
5️⃣ VISUAL CHECK + COUPLING TEST
Verify the lock by the manufacturer’s required inspection and coupling-check procedure before travel
🔒 Won’t lock?
Stop and inspect trailer height/alignment, lock position, contamination, damage, lubrication and release components. Never force or assume a partial lock is safe.
🔓 Won’t release?
Secure the tractor and trailer and relieve coupling tension as specified. Then inspect the release handle/linkage and locking mechanism by the manufacturer procedure.
↔️ Too much movement?
Inspect kingpin wear, locking components, bushings, brackets, slider/mounting components and adjustment against the manufacturer’s wear limits.
🧴 Binding / poor articulation?
Inspect required plate lubrication, contact surfaces, pivot points and mounting condition. Binding can also come from damaged or worn components.

The handle is not proof of a safe coupling

Fifth-wheel designs differ, but the principle is the same: the kingpin must be correctly captured by the locking mechanism and the connection must be physically verified. A handle that appears to be in position does not replace the required visual inspection and coupling check. Use the exact fifth-wheel manufacturer procedure for the model installed on the tractor.

🧠 Simple rule: ALIGN → LOCK → LOOK → VERIFY. Never trust the handle position alone.
⚠️ An improperly coupled trailer can separate from the tractor and cause severe injury or death. Secure the vehicle, stay clear of pinch/crush areas and complete the manufacturer’s coupling inspection before moving.
