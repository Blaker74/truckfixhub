A failed regen may be the messenger

Active faults → enable conditions → plausible inputs → temperature response → soot/load behavior → DEF/SCR operation → verification.

Do not treat “regen will not start” as proof the DPF is bad. The controller may intentionally inhibit regeneration because another engine, sensor, temperature, pressure, dosing or system condition has not been satisfied.

Regen will not begin

Preserve active faults and determine whether the request is being denied or whether the process starts and then aborts. Those are different failures. Verify prerequisites and input plausibility before forcing repeated regeneration attempts.

Regen completes but returns

Repeated regeneration can point upstream. Investigate why soot is accumulating, whether the engine is producing excessive particulate matter, whether temperature response is appropriate and whether the system is accurately measuring load.

SCR / NOx diagnosis needs the whole system

A NOx-related fault does not automatically prove a NOx sensor has failed. Conversion performance depends on exhaust conditions, sensor plausibility, DEF quality/delivery, dosing, temperature and catalyst operation. Follow the complete fault definition and diagnostic procedure for the exact engine before replacing a sensor or SCR component.

Evidence worth capturing

  • Complete SPN/FMI information, source controller and active/inactive status before clearing faults.
  • Dash warnings, derate level and the sequence in which warnings appeared.
  • Whether regen is requested, denied, starts, aborts or completes.
  • Soot/load information and relevant temperature/pressure sensor behavior using the approved diagnostic method.
  • DEF level/condition and any recent refill, contamination or repair history.
  • Engine-performance complaints such as low boost, smoke or excessive fuel/oil consumption that may affect aftertreatment loading.

Exact temperatures, pressure limits, dosing tests, soot thresholds and inducement logic vary by engine family, calibration and emissions configuration. Verify them with current manufacturer service information.

Aftertreatment safety

Exhaust and aftertreatment components can reach extremely high temperatures during regeneration. Keep combustible material clear, use an appropriate safe location and follow current manufacturer procedures for parked regeneration, testing and service. Do not bypass emissions or safety protections.
