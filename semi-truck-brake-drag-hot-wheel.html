🔥 BRAKE DRAG / HOT WHEEL

A hot wheel position is evidence. First prove whether the heat comes from the BRAKE, WHEEL END, or TIRE.

🛑 Stop first when heat is severe

Smoke, burning odor, extreme heat, wheel looseness, lubricant loss, tire damage or abnormal braking can indicate an unsafe condition. Stop safely and keep the vehicle out of service until the cause is identified and corrected.

👇 Diagnostic path

1️⃣ LOCATE THE HEAT. Identify the exact tractor/trailer axle and wheel position. Compare similar positions only after comparable operation.
2️⃣ RECORD WHEN IT HAPPENS. After braking? After highway cruise? One wheel? Both wheels on an axle? Does the truck pull? Does heat return after the brakes should be released?
3️⃣ SEPARATE BRAKE FROM WHEEL END. Inspect for evidence of brake drag and incomplete release, but also inspect hub lubricant, bearing condition, seal leakage and wheel-end movement. Do not assume every hot hub is a brake problem.
4️⃣ FOLLOW THE BRAKE RELEASE PATH. On air brakes, determine whether air/control is releasing correctly and whether the chamber, slack adjuster, foundation brake and related hardware return as designed. Use the exact brake system procedure.
5️⃣ INSPECT FRICTION COMPONENTS. Look for abnormal lining/pad wear, heat damage, drum/rotor evidence, contamination, seized or binding hardware and incorrect mechanical condition.
6️⃣ CHECK THE TIRE TOO. Underinflation, damage or overload can create heat at the same corner. Do not stop diagnosis at the first hot component without locating the source.
7️⃣ VERIFY THE REPAIR. Recreate the original operating condition safely and compare wheel temperatures/behavior again. Confirm normal brake release and no recurring heat.

🧠 Pattern clues

ONE WHEEL HOT: Think local first—foundation brake, chamber/release hardware, wheel end/bearing, lubricant or tire.
BOTH ENDS OF ONE AXLE HOT: Expand toward shared control, adjustment, loading or axle-level conditions while still inspecting each end.
MULTIPLE BRAKES HOT: Look upstream for a shared pneumatic/control or operating condition rather than assuming several unrelated wheel-end failures occurred together.
HOT AFTER LITTLE BRAKING: Incomplete release, mechanical drag, wheel-end friction or tire trouble becomes especially important evidence.
