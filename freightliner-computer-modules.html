Freightliner Computer Module & ECU Identification

A technician-first repository for identifying the electronic controllers found on Freightliner trucks. This first catalog is anchored to Cascadia/Detroit documentation and will expand by model, generation, engine and option package.

Important: Freightliner does not have one universal module list. Installed ECUs vary by model year, engine, transmission, safety package, HVAC, chassis options and vehicle configuration. Always verify by VIN and OEM service information. TruckFixHub will not label a module as present on every Freightliner unless the source supports that claim.
Cascadia / Detroit core module catalog

CPC — Common Powertrain Controller

PowertrainDetroit
📷 Official Detroit documentation includes a CPC identification illustration. TruckFixHub links to the source rather than copying copyrighted OEM artwork into the repository.

What it does: Vehicle/powertrain coordination controller. It receives driver and vehicle inputs, exchanges information with the engine controller and broadcasts powertrain information on the datalinks.

Diagnostic importance: A CPC problem can affect throttle/accelerator input, engine brake requests, vehicle communication and powertrain operation. Diagnose power, ground and datalink integrity before condemning the controller.

Official Detroit source / CPC illustration

MCM — Motor Control Module

EngineDetroit
📷 Official Detroit DD Platform documentation contains an MCM photograph/illustration and controller description.

What it does: Engine controller. It evaluates information from the CPC and engine sensors and controls functions such as fuel injection quantity and timing.

Diagnostic importance: Engine sensor, actuator, injector and engine-control faults may involve the MCM, but module replacement should follow power, ground, harness, sensor/actuator and datalink testing.

Official Detroit source / MCM visual

ACM — Aftertreatment Control Module

AftertreatmentDetroit
📷 Identification source: Detroit DD Platform operator/service literature.

What it does: Controls and monitors major aftertreatment functions and communicates aftertreatment information with the rest of the powertrain system.

Common location: Detroit documentation identifies the ACM at the aftertreatment system; exact mounting varies by configuration.

Diagnostic importance: DEF, NOx, temperature, dosing and aftertreatment faults do not automatically prove an ACM failure. Verify circuits, sensors, actuators, supply conditions and datalink communication first.

Official Detroit source

TCM — Transmission Control Module

Transmission
📷 Photo/connector identification varies with installed transmission. Verify transmission make and model by VIN before using an image.

What it does: Controls automated/automatic transmission functions and exchanges engine, vehicle-speed and shift information with other controllers.

Diagnostic importance: Transmission faults can originate in power/ground, datalink, sensors, actuators, clutch systems or transmission hardware rather than the TCM itself.

Official Freightliner ECU acronym source

sSAM / SSAM — Single Signal Detect and Actuation Module

CabChassis
📷 Freightliner New Cascadia manual identifies the sSAM and its location in the cab electrical system.

What it does: Primary electrical-system controller combining chassis power-distribution, cab power-distribution and modular-switch functions. It reads sensor, switch and databus inputs and drives outputs/CAN messages.

Common location: New Cascadia documentation places it near the center of the cab behind the lower dash panel.

Official Freightliner source

CGW — Central Gateway

NetworkCab
📷 ECU appearance/location depends on Cascadia generation and configuration; use VIN-specific OEM information for physical identification.

What it does: Gateway controller used to exchange information between vehicle networks/controllers.

Diagnostic importance: A gateway-related complaint can affect communication across multiple ECUs. Shared power, grounds and network integrity should be checked before module replacement.

Official Freightliner ECU acronym source

ICU — Instrumentation Control Unit

Dash
📷 Instrument-cluster/controller appearance changes by model year and dash generation.

What it does: Instrumentation controller associated with driver information, gauges, warnings and diagnostic display functions.

Diagnostic importance: Loss of display information can be a cluster/ICU problem, but may also be caused by power, ground or vehicle-network faults.

Official Freightliner source

ABS ECU — Antilock Brake System Controller

BrakesSafety
📷 Physical controller varies with brake-system supplier and vehicle specification.

What it does: Monitors wheel-speed/brake-system information and controls ABS functions; equipped systems may integrate additional stability/braking functions.

Diagnostic importance: Identify the installed brake system before applying connector, pinout or module-location information.

Official Freightliner ECU acronym source

DCMD — Driver Door Control Module

CabDoor

What it does: Controls/monitors applicable driver-door electrical functions such as powered mirror/door features depending on specification.

SAS — Steering Angle Sensor

SteeringSafety
📷 Sensor appearance/location varies by steering-column and safety-system configuration.

What it does: Provides steering-angle information used by applicable stability and driver-assistance systems. Although often called a sensor rather than a standalone ECU, Freightliner lists SAS among the electronic units visible to the vehicle diagnostic system.

Official Freightliner source

Legacy & configuration-dependent controllers
SAM CabEarlier Cascadia electrical architecture uses a SAM Cab. It contains electrical distribution/control functions and is located in the cab.
SAM ChassisEarlier Cascadia architecture uses a SAM Chassis in the engine compartment/frontwall area, with fuses, relays and solid-state circuit protection.
XMC and other option controllersFreightliner/DTNA documentation recognizes additional vehicle/chassis controllers such as XMC. Presence and function are configuration-specific and will be added only when a reliable OEM source is attached.
Repository standard: Every module entry must identify the controller, explain its job, state configuration/location limitations, and point to an authoritative source. OEM photos and technical illustrations are linked to their original source rather than copied into TruckFixHub unless reuse rights are established.
