What are you seeing?

💧 Oil or grease leakingStart with seal, hub and vent evidence.🌡️ One wheel end is hotSeparate bearing, brake and lubrication causes.↔️ Wheel has loosenessCheck bearing adjustment and worn components.🔊 Growl, rumble or roughnessInspect bearings, races and related rotating parts.

Know the assembly

HubCarries the bearing cups/races and wheel mounting system.
Inner & outer bearingsSupport the rotating hub on the spindle.
Wheel sealKeeps lubricant in and contamination out.
SpindleSupports the hub and provides bearing and seal running surfaces.
Adjustment hardwareControls bearing setting/end play according to the exact system.
LubricantOil or approved grease depending on the wheel-end design.

💧 Leak path

1. Identify the fluid and exact source.Do not call every wet wheel a failed wheel seal. Look at where the wetness begins.
2. Check lubricant level and condition.Low level, contamination or mixed lubricant is evidence that changes the path.
3. Inspect the hub, seal area and venting.Look for damage, looseness, blocked venting where applicable, and evidence of installation problems.
4. If disassembled, inspect the seal running surface.A new seal cannot repair a damaged spindle or wear surface.
5. Repair the proven cause, then verify.Set up and lubricate the wheel end using the exact manufacturer procedure for that axle/hub system.

🌡️ Hot wheel-end path

1. Compare sides safely.A temperature difference is a clue, not a diagnosis.
2. Separate brake heat from bearing heat.Check for dragging brake evidence before condemning bearings.
3. Check lubricant level/condition and wheel-end play.Loss of lubrication or incorrect adjustment can create heat.
4. Inspect bearings and races when evidence requires teardown.Look for discoloration, scoring, pitting, spalling or roughness.

↔️ Looseness path

1. Confirm where the movement is.Do not confuse king-pin, suspension or steering looseness with wheel-bearing movement.
2. Measure wheel-end movement correctly.Use the approved procedure and specification for the exact wheel-end system.
3. Inspect adjustment hardware and components.If adjustment will not remain correct, find out why rather than repeatedly tightening it.

🔊 Noise or roughness path

1. Reproduce and locate the symptom.Road speed, turning load and braking can help separate possible sources.
2. Inspect related tire, brake and driveline causes.Do not replace wheel-end parts because a noise merely sounds like a bearing.
3. Inspect bearing surfaces when teardown is justified.Damage patterns are evidence; document what you find.

⚠️ Safety-critical assembly

Wheel ends carry vehicle load and retain the wheel/hub assembly. Incorrect bearing adjustment, fastener procedure, seal installation or lubrication can cause serious failure. TruckFixHub intentionally does not publish one universal torque or end-play value.

Verify the exact equipment

Before assembly, identify the axle, hub, bearing-adjustment system, seal and lubrication method. Use the current manufacturer procedure for torque sequences, end play, lubricant quantity and component reuse limits.
