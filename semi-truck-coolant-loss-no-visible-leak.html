💧 LOSING COOLANT — NO VISIBLE LEAK?

Quick answer

Coolant can escape only when hot/pressurized, leak into another system, be expelled from the surge tank, or enter the engine/exhaust path. Start with a cold visual inspection and manufacturer-approved cooling-system pressure test. Inspect the cap/surge tank and every external circuit first. If no external loss is found, check engine oil and other fluids for contamination and follow the engine manufacturer's procedures for internal coolant-loss diagnosis, including EGR-cooler or combustion-related testing where applicable.

Diagnostic order

  1. Document coolant level cold at the same reference point.
  2. Inspect hoses, clamps, radiator, surge tank, cap area, water pump, heater lines and fittings for dried residue.
  3. Pressure-test the cooling system to the correct specification.
  4. Inspect for leaks that appear only under pressure or after heat soak.
  5. Check engine oil and other relevant fluids for coolant contamination.
  6. On equipped engines, follow OEM procedures for EGR-cooler/internal circuit diagnosis.
  7. If combustion intrusion is suspected, use the engine manufacturer's approved diagnostic tests before disassembly.

Pattern clues

Dried white/colored residue: often marks a small external leak that evaporates before dripping.
Coolant pushed from surge tank: inspect cap/system pressure and follow OEM tests; do not jump directly to a head gasket.
Coolant contamination in oil: stop normal operation and diagnose the internal leak source before engine damage increases.
White exhaust/steam or unexplained coolant loss: follow engine-specific internal/EGR-cooler diagnostic procedures where applicable.
🧠 Parts-cannon rule: unexplained coolant loss does not automatically equal a head gasket. Pressure-test and isolate the path before teardown.
⚠️ Never remove a cooling-system cap while hot or pressurized. Coolant loss can lead to severe engine damage; investigate recurring loss promptly.
