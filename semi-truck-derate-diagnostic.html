ENGINE DERATE

Find out why the truck is limiting power before firing the parts cannon.

What derate means

A derate is a control strategy that limits engine power, speed or vehicle operation because the electronic system detected a condition requiring protection or correction. Derate is the result—not the root cause. The fastest useful starting point is the complete active fault-code picture.

🔎 First three moves

1️⃣ SAVE EVERY ACTIVE CODE — Record SPN/FMI or OEM codes before clearing them. One code may be a consequence of another.
2️⃣ IDENTIFY THE SYSTEM — Separate emissions/aftertreatment faults from boost/air, fuel, cooling, electrical or other engine-control faults.
3️⃣ VERIFY THE CONDITION — Note DEF level and quality concerns, regen history, temperatures, pressures, boost behavior, engine temperature, battery voltage and anything recently repaired.

♻️ If aftertreatment codes are active

Do not assume the DPF is automatically the failed component. The aftertreatment system depends on sensors, wiring, exhaust temperature, DEF delivery and quality, NOx feedback, dosing, pressure readings and the engine producing conditions that allow the system to work correctly. Diagnose the active faults in the manufacturer's prescribed order for the exact engine and emissions configuration.

⚙️ If the problem is power-related

A truck can feel derated because commanded power is being limited, but similar complaints can also come from low boost, charge-air leaks, fuel-delivery problems, excessive restriction, overheating or electrical faults. Compare commanded and actual values when appropriate instead of relying on the driver's description alone.

🚫 Don't erase your evidence

Clearing codes before recording them can remove valuable clues. Capture the complete fault picture, occurrence information and operating conditions first. After the root cause is repaired, follow the manufacturer's procedure for clearing faults, completing required regens or verification routines, and confirming the derate has released.
