🧰 Six Engine Drawers

1 · AIR INThe engine needs clean air with a clear path through the intake, turbocharger and charge-air system.
2 · FUEL INFuel must be clean, available, correctly supplied and delivered at the right time and amount.
3 · COMPRESSIONThe cylinder must trap and compress air well enough for diesel combustion. Mechanical condition matters.
4 · TIMING & CONTROLThe ECM needs believable sensor information and must command fuel and air-management components correctly.
5 · EXHAUST OUTExhaust must leave the engine. Restriction or aftertreatment problems can reduce power and create derates.
6 · OIL & COOLINGOil protects moving parts. Coolant and airflow control heat. Problems here can become engine failures quickly.

🔍 Let The Symptom Pick The Drawer

⬛ BLACK SMOKEThink too much fuel for available air, poor air delivery or combustion trouble. Test before blaming the turbo.
⬜ WHITE SMOKEThink unburned fuel, low combustion temperature, coolant intrusion or other combustion evidence.
🔵 BLUE SMOKEThink oil entering combustion or exhaust flow. Find the source before replacing a component.
〰️ ROUGH RUNNINGCompare cylinders and inspect fuel, air, sensor and mechanical evidence instead of guessing at injectors.
🐢 LOW POWERFirst ask whether the ECM is intentionally limiting torque. Then check air, fuel, exhaust and mechanical condition.
🛢️ USES OILLook for leakage, crankcase ventilation, turbo evidence and internal consumption before choosing a cause.

🔧 The Diagnostic Drawer Never Changes

SEE ITDescribe the complaint and the exact conditions when it happens.
NAME ITRoute the evidence toward air, fuel, mechanical, control, exhaust, lubrication or cooling.
TEST ITUse fault information, live data, pressure, temperature, restriction, visual inspection and approved tests.
PROVE ITRepair the supported cause, reproduce the original condition and verify the complaint is gone.

⚠️ DON'T DIAGNOSE BY REPUTATION

A common failure is still only a possibility until evidence supports it. Exact pressure limits, sensor values, torque, timing, programming and test procedures depend on the engine and application. Verify those values with current manufacturer information.
