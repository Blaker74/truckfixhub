Low power is not a turbo diagnosis

Air supply → charge-air integrity → fuel delivery → combustion → exhaust flow → electronic command.

A restricted intake or filter, charge-air leak, fuel restriction, inaccurate input, exhaust restriction or commanded derate can all feel like a weak turbo.

Weak only under load

Record boost, smoke, engine speed, road load, gear and related faults. High demand can expose restrictions, leaks and supply limitations that are not obvious at idle.

Weak all the time

Check commanded operation, active faults, basic air/fuel supply and whether the engine is electronically limited.

Smoke is evidence

  • Black: investigate available air, commanded fuel, boost/charge-air integrity and combustion.
  • White: document temperature and operating condition; investigate incomplete combustion or fluid entry with proper testing.
  • Blue: investigate oil entering the combustion/exhaust path.

Smoke color narrows the investigation; it does not identify a failed part by itself.

Evidence worth capturing

Record when the complaint occurs, complete fault information, boost behavior, smoke, recent repairs, fluid consumption, filter/service history and whether a derate is active. Exact pressure, temperature, flow and performance specifications must be verified for the exact engine and vehicle.

Engine diagnostic safety

Use appropriate precautions around hot exhaust components, rotating machinery, high-current starting systems and pressurized intake/fuel systems. Do not defeat engine or aftertreatment protections.
