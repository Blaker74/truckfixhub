AIR BRAKES · LOW PRESSURE · WARNING SYSTEM

Semi truck low air warning light or buzzer stays on?

Treat a low-air warning as a real pressure problem until the system is properly verified. The cause may be actual air loss, slow pressure build or a sensing/warning-system fault, but braking safety comes first.

Do not ignore or silence a low-air warning

Do not continue operating a truck that cannot maintain the required air pressure or brake-release conditions. Never bypass, silence or defeat the warning device to keep the vehicle in service. Follow the vehicle manufacturer's procedures and applicable inspection requirements.

Quick answer: what is the air system actually doing?

  1. Look at both primary and secondary pressure indications.
  2. Record whether one or both circuits appear low.
  3. Observe whether pressure builds normally, slowly, stalls or drops.
  4. Record whether pressure loss changes when the service brakes are applied.
  5. Note whether parking/spring brakes release normally.
  6. Listen for obvious leakage only from a safe position.
  7. Record recent compressor, dryer, governor, brake, dash or electrical work.

Air pressure really is low

If gauge indications and system behavior show low pressure, diagnose the air-production or air-loss problem rather than treating the warning lamp itself as the failure. Slow build, no build and pressure that builds then leaks down are different complaints.

Low air keeps the parking brakes from releasing

Spring brakes require adequate system pressure to release. If the parking brakes remain applied while a low-air condition exists, diagnose the pressure problem first. Do not force the truck to move against applied brakes.

Pressure drops when the brake pedal is applied

If pressure loss becomes significant specifically during a service-brake application, record which gauge changes and whether an air leak can be heard. That pattern deserves a brake-application leak diagnostic path.

Air leak near a wheel or brake chamber

A wheel-area leak during brake application may involve a chamber, hose, fitting or nearby plumbing. Positively locate the leak instead of replacing a brake chamber solely because the sound comes from that area.

Air dryer keeps purging or leaking

Repeated dryer activity can occur when the compressor cycles frequently, including when another leak causes the system to repeatedly reload. Continuous leakage at the dryer also deserves diagnosis of the dryer, purge mechanism and control system rather than automatic dryer replacement.

Gauges show pressure but warning stays on

Do not immediately assume the warning is false. After actual air pressure and brake safety have been verified with the proper procedure, technicians may need to investigate pressure-sensing devices, wiring, connectors, dash/module information and warning-system components applicable to the truck.

What not to assume

  • A low-air warning does not automatically mean the compressor is bad.
  • A warning with normal-looking gauges does not automatically mean the sensor is bad.
  • Frequent air-dryer purging does not automatically mean the dryer is the root cause.
  • An air leak heard near a wheel does not automatically prove the brake chamber failed.
  • Never clear, bypass or ignore the warning before the actual air system is verified.

Air-brake diagnosis is safety-critical

TruckFixHub provides educational troubleshooting information. Air-brake configurations, specifications and required tests vary. Use current OEM and brake-component procedures and applicable inspection requirements before returning the vehicle to service.
