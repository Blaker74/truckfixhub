TRUCKFIXHUB AIR-BRAKE WARNING GUIDE

Low Air Warning Light or Buzzer On?

Treat a low-air warning as a real air-pressure problem until the system is properly verified. A warning light, buzzer or gauge concern can involve actual pressure loss, slow air build or an electrical/sensing issue, but braking safety comes first.

Do not ignore a low-air warning

Air pressure is critical to heavy-duty braking systems. Follow the vehicle and brake-system manufacturer's warning procedures. Do not continue operating a truck that cannot maintain safe air pressure, and never bypass or silence a warning device to keep the vehicle in service.

First: compare the warning with what the air system is doing

  • Primary and secondary gauges both show low pressure
  • Only one circuit appears low
  • Gauges show pressure but the warning remains on
  • Pressure builds very slowly after startup
  • Pressure drops rapidly with the engine off
  • Pressure drops when the brakes are applied
  • Parking brake or spring brakes will not release normally
  • Air dryer purges or leaks repeatedly
  • Warning started after air-system, dash or electrical work

If low air is keeping the parking brakes applied

Spring brakes require adequate air pressure to release. If the parking brake will not release while the low-air warning is active, diagnose the air-pressure problem first and do not force the truck to move against applied brakes.

If the truck actually will not build air

Slow or inadequate pressure buildup can involve major air leaks, compressor performance, governor/control concerns, air-dryer problems, lines, valves or other application-specific components. Diagnose the air-production problem rather than treating the warning lamp as the cause.

If pressure builds and then drops

Listen for and properly test air leakage throughout the truck and trailer system. Note whether the loss occurs continuously, only with a brake application, from one circuit, or after the engine is shut down.

Losing air pressure / air leak guide →

If the pressure loss happens specifically when the service brakes are applied, use the air pressure drops when braking guide. If you can actually hear air escaping during the brake application, see air leak when brakes are applied.

If the leak is near a wheel or brake chamber

A wheel-area leak during brake application may involve the brake chamber, hose, fittings or nearby plumbing. Positively locate the leak instead of assuming the chamber is the failed component.

Brake chamber air leak symptoms →

If the air dryer keeps purging or leaking

Repeated purging can occur with frequent compressor cycling, and an air leak elsewhere in the system can make the compressor reload repeatedly. Continuous leakage at the dryer also deserves proper diagnosis of the dryer, purge mechanism and control system.

Air dryer purging / leaking guide →

Gauges show pressure but warning stays on

Do not assume the warning is false. After actual system pressure and brake safety are verified using the correct procedure, technicians may investigate pressure-sensing devices, wiring, connectors, dash/module information and other warning-system components applicable to the truck.

What to record for the shop

  • Primary and secondary gauge readings
  • Whether the warning is a light, buzzer or both
  • How quickly pressure builds after startup
  • Whether pressure falls with brakes released or applied
  • Whether the parking or spring brakes release normally
  • Any audible leak location
  • Air-dryer purge behavior
  • Recent compressor, governor, dryer, brake or electrical repairs
  • Any related fault codes or dash messages

Do not bypass the warning system

TruckFixHub provides educational troubleshooting information. Air-brake systems are safety-critical and configurations vary. Use the applicable OEM and brake-component procedures and qualified heavy-duty service practices before returning the vehicle to service.
