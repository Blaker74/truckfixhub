Documented Cummins ISX Problems: Fuel Pumps, EGR Coolers and Coolant Loss

An ISX badge alone does not identify the failure—or the repair. These Cummins documents cover specific engine platforms and different component concerns.

TruckFixHub editorial overview · Sources reviewed October 11, 2026

Identify the engine before choosing a repair. Supply the engine serial number (ESN), control-system designation and complete fault codes. Follow the vehicle’s warning and shutdown instructions.

Are these the most common ISX problems?

The documents below establish that these concerns were documented. They do not provide a fleet-wide failure-rate comparison, so this page does not rank them as the most common. A bulletin’s existence also does not mean every engine carrying the ISX name is affected.

Fuel-pump plunger and roller-tappet concerns

Manufacturer inspection and repair guidance

A pump malfunction can require more than replacing the pump

Cummins bulletin TSB140019 addresses pumping-plunger and roller-tappet malfunctions. Its affected-product list includes ISX15 CM2250, CM2250 SN and CM2350 X101, along with specified ISX12 and QSX applications.

The May 2016 document illustrates a fractured ceramic plunger, but its revised repair guidance applies regardless of plunger or tappet type. The scope does not mean all listed engines contain ceramic plungers.

Its decision process considers tappet condition, debris in the lubricating-oil filter, logged faults and engine condition. Depending on findings, repair may extend to oil-system cleaning and inspection of other engine components. The bulletin says progressive damage varies by incident.

What to ask your shop: Which pump design is installed, what inspection found, and whether the proposed repair addresses contamination and any additional damage.

Historical campaign · Specific eligibility

CM2350 tappet campaign: a separate component concern

Cummins campaign C1794, revision C dated March 21, 2017, covered certain ISX15 CM2350 high-pressure fuel-pump tappets. It specified replacing brass roller-pin tappets with diamond-like-coated roller-pin tappets.

This is distinct from ceramic-plunger material. The historical campaign required an eligible engine to show an open campaign status in QuickServe and listed a June 2, 2017 expiration date.

Do not interpret this archived document as a current offer of free repairs. Ask a Cummins-authorized location to check the ESN, repair history and any applicable current coverage.

Coolant loss and the EGR cooler

Manufacturer design update

ISX15 CM2250: complete the diagnosis first

Cummins TSB120257 describes an improved EGR cooler for ISX15 CM2250 engines. Changes included coolant distribution at the gas inlet and a revised gas diffuser to reduce constraints during expansion and contraction.

The bulletin identifies production and service introduction dates in 2012. Crucially, it instructs technicians investigating internal coolant loss to complete the published troubleshooting before replacing the cooler.

This documents a design update and a diagnostic requirement. It does not prove that an EGR cooler caused a particular truck’s coolant loss or establish the frequency of cooler failure.

What to ask your shop: Which tests established the source of the coolant loss, and how the replacement was matched to the ESN and current service information.

Before authorizing parts

Use this TruckFixHub checklist to organize the conversation with your repair shop:

  • Identify: Truck year and model, ESN, engine platform and component identification.
  • Describe: Exact warning, full fault codes, when the symptom occurs and what changed recently.
  • Document: Test results that support the diagnosis, rather than a list of possible causes.
  • Check history: Previous pump, cooler or engine repairs; ask the shop to check relevant campaigns by ESN.
  • Review the quote: Parts, labor, any additional inspections and the planned post-repair verification.
