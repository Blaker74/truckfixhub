💡 LIGHTING

Every light needs a command, a complete power path and a complete return path.

1️⃣ SWITCH / COMMAND
A driver input or control module requests the light as equipped
↓
2️⃣ FUSE / CIRCUIT PROTECTION
Protects the circuit from excessive current
↓
3️⃣ WIRING + CONNECTORS
Carry electrical power through the circuit
↓
4️⃣ LAMP / LED ASSEMBLY
Converts electrical energy into light
↓
5️⃣ GROUND / RETURN PATH
Completes the electrical circuit back to its source
⬛ One light out?
Start locally. Verify the lamp or LED assembly, connector, power feed and ground/return at that light before chasing the whole truck.
🚫 Several lights out?
Look for what the failed lights share: circuit protection, power feed, ground, switch/module command, harness or common connector.
✨ Dim / flickering?
Inspect for corrosion, loose terminals and poor connections. Use loaded voltage-drop testing where appropriate instead of relying only on unloaded voltage readings.
💥 Fuse keeps opening?
Stop replacing fuses. Find the short-to-ground, damaged wiring, failed component or overload that is causing excessive current.

Seeing voltage is not always the same as having a good circuit

A corroded connection can sometimes show system voltage on a meter while carrying almost no useful current. That is why a lighting circuit may test “hot” and still fail when the lamp is connected. Verify the command, power and return path under the proper load and use the vehicle manufacturer’s wiring information for electronically controlled lighting.
