Semi Truck Parking Brake Won't Release?

If the parking brake or spring brakes remain applied after the control is released, do not force the truck to move. First determine whether the air system has enough pressure and whether the problem affects the tractor, trailer, one axle or one wheel.

Stop before dragging an applied brake

Do not drive against brakes that remain applied. A dragging spring brake can rapidly generate dangerous heat and damage brake, tire and wheel-end components. If a wheel is hot, smoking or smells burned, see the hot brake / smoking wheel guide.

First clues to record

  • Primary and secondary air pressure.
  • Whether the parking-brake control moves and behaves normally.
  • Whether the tractor, trailer, one axle or one wheel stays applied.
  • Whether you hear an air leak near a valve, hose, gladhand or brake chamber.
  • Whether a low-air warning is active.
  • Whether the problem began during freezing or wet weather.
  • Any recent brake chamber, valve, hose, brake or air-system work.

Truck has low air pressure

Spring brakes require adequate air pressure to release. If system pressure is low or will not build normally, diagnose the air-pressure problem before assuming the foundation brake itself is locked.

Air pressure is normal but brakes stay applied

With normal system pressure, qualified technicians may need to verify the parking/spring-brake control circuit, air delivery, applicable relay or quick-release valves, brake chamber operation, pushrod movement and foundation-brake components. Exact plumbing and component layout varies by vehicle and brake system.

Tractor releases but trailer brakes stay locked

If the tractor releases normally but the trailer brakes remain applied, treat that as a more specific trailer air-supply or trailer brake-control symptom. Record whether all trailer brakes are affected or only one axle or wheel.

One brake or wheel stays locked

If most brakes release but one wheel or axle remains applied, the symptom points more toward a localized brake, chamber, air-delivery or foundation-brake problem than a truck-wide lack of air.

Hear an air leak while trying to release?

An audible leak can help narrow the system area. Note exactly where the sound is strongest and whether it changes when the parking control or service brake is operated.

Problem started in freezing weather

Freezing conditions can expose moisture-related air-system problems. Do not use unsafe heat sources or improvised methods around brake components. Check the air system and dryer history as part of the diagnosis.

What to tell the repair shop

  • Tractor, trailer or both affected.
  • Exact wheel or axle if localized.
  • Primary and secondary air pressure.
  • Whether the low-air warning is active.
  • Whether an air leak can be heard and where.
  • Whether any brake is hot, smoking or smells burned.
  • Weather conditions when the problem started.
  • Recent brake, chamber, valve, hose or air-system repairs.

Spring-brake safety

Spring-brake chambers contain powerful stored mechanical energy. Do not disassemble a spring-brake chamber or improvise a brake-release procedure. Heavy-duty brake systems are safety-critical. Follow the vehicle and component manufacturers' service procedures and use qualified heavy-duty service personnel. Never work beneath an unsecured vehicle.
