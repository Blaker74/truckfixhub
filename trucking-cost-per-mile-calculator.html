How to calculate trucking cost per mile

  1. Add loaded and empty miles. Both contribute to the operating-mile total.
  2. Calculate fuel cost. Total miles ÷ MPG × diesel price.
  3. Add the rest of your costs. Multiply each per-mile budget by total miles, then add monthly fixed costs.
  4. Divide by total miles for operating cost per mile.
  5. Divide by loaded miles for the revenue per loaded mile needed to cover those costs.

For a target margin, required revenue = total costs ÷ (1 − margin as a decimal). Divide that revenue by loaded miles to get the corresponding loaded-mile rate.

Worked example: why empty miles matter

With 8,000 loaded miles, 2,000 empty miles, 6.5 MPG and $4 diesel, estimated fuel cost is $6,153.85. Add a $0.20 maintenance reserve, $0.65 driver compensation and $0.10 other variable costs per total mile, plus $5,000 fixed costs.

Total estimated monthly cost is $20,653.85: approximately $2.07 per total mile, or $2.58 per loaded mile to break even. At a 10% target margin, the corresponding loaded-mile revenue is approximately $2.87. These are example figures, not operating benchmarks.

Questions about trucking costs and freight rates

What is the difference between cost per mile and rate per mile?

Cost per mile describes your expenses divided by the miles you use for the calculation. Rate per mile describes revenue. Always check whether a number uses loaded miles or all miles; comparing different denominators can hide the cost of empty travel.

Why include empty or deadhead miles?

They increase the total-mile costs in this model without adding loaded miles to the revenue calculation. Keeping loaded miles fixed and increasing empty miles therefore raises the estimated break-even loaded-mile rate when variable costs are greater than zero.

Does break-even include paying myself?

Only if you include your compensation in the driver budget or another cost field. The operating result is after the costs you entered, so a positive result is not a substitute for a driver-pay budget you left out.

Is a 10% margin the same as adding 10% to costs?

No. Margin is the operating result divided by revenue. A $100 cost with a 10% revenue margin requires about $111.11 in revenue. Adding 10% to $100 produces $110, which is a markup calculation.

Can this tell me what a load should pay?

It estimates your cost-based threshold using monthly figures. It does not know lane conditions, negotiating terms, waiting time, accessorial revenue, or your return load. Check those separately before evaluating a specific offer.
