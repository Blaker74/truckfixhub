🚚 SEMI-TRAILER TROUBLESHOOTING

Put the trailer problem into one of four boxes before chasing parts.

💨 AIR
Supply and service lines, reservoirs, valves, chambers and brake controls
💡 ELECTRICAL
Tractor/trailer connector, power, wiring, grounds, ABS and lighting circuits
🛞 RUNNING GEAR
Tires, wheels, wheel ends, brakes, suspension and axles
🔒 COUPLING / BODY
Kingpin/upper coupler, landing gear, doors, frame and structure
🛑 Brakes wrong?
First prove tractor side or trailer side.
💡 Lights dead / strange?
Start at the tractor-trailer connection.
↔️ Pulls / dog-tracks?
Inspect running gear before alignment guessing.
⬆️ Landing gear binds?
Secure it. Do not force it.

👇 PICK THE COMPLAINT

🛑 Trailer brakes are not working right

  1. Secure the combination and confirm the complaint safely.
  2. Verify the tractor is supplying the expected air / control to the trailer connection.
  3. If the tractor side is correct, follow the trailer supply and service circuits into reservoirs, valves and chambers.
  4. Inspect foundation brakes and ABS information as applicable.
  5. Repair the proven air, control or mechanical cause and verify normal brake operation before service.

💡 Trailer lights are dead, dim or acting strange

  1. Identify exactly which trailer light functions fail.
  2. Start at the tractor-to-trailer electrical connector and verify the required circuits there.
  3. If several lights fail, look for a shared feed, ground, connector or harness problem.
  4. If only one light fails, continue locally to its wiring, connector, ground and lamp / LED assembly.
  5. Repair the circuit and verify every required trailer light function afterward.

↔️ Trailer pulls, dog-tracks or wears tires

  1. Record which axle / tire positions show the complaint and the wear pattern.
  2. Verify tire inflation and inspect wheels and wheel ends.
  3. Inspect suspension components, bushings, torque rods, air springs and ride height as equipped.
  4. Inspect axle position / alignment and structural mounting points after mechanical looseness or damage is ruled out.
  5. Correct the cause before replacing tires or treating alignment as the only problem.

⬆️ Landing gear is hard to crank or will not move correctly

  1. Secure the trailer and make sure the landing gear is not being dangerously overloaded.
  2. Inspect leg alignment, mounting and obvious structural damage.
  3. Check crank handle, cross shaft, gearbox and lubrication points as applicable.
  4. Do not use excessive force to overcome a bind; internal or structural damage may worsen.
  5. Use the landing-gear manufacturer procedure for disassembly, repair and load limits.

📚 Diagnose the trailer from the connection outward

A semi-trailer is not one system. Air, electrical, running gear and structure meet at different points, and the tractor can create symptoms that appear to belong to the trailer. First put the complaint into the correct system, then find the boundary where the fault begins.

🔌 The tractor-trailer interface is a powerful test point

For air and electrical complaints, verify what the tractor actually delivers at the connection before moving deeper into the trailer. If the correct supply or signal never reaches the trailer, trailer parts cannot fix the cause. If it arrives correctly, continue downstream and locate where it is lost.

🛑 ABS warning and poor braking are not the same complaint

A trailer ABS warning can involve electrical, sensor or control information while a pneumatic or foundation-brake problem can affect actual braking force. They can occur together, but one does not automatically prove the other. Separate warning-lamp diagnosis from air and mechanical brake performance.

💡 Multiple strange lights often share something

When several trailer lamps are dim, flashing incorrectly or interacting with another circuit, shared connector, feed, ground or harness problems become more important than several lamps failing at once. Verify the circuit under load so a weak connection is not mistaken for a good one.

🛞 Tire wear is evidence of what the trailer has been doing

Inflation, wheel-end condition, brake drag, suspension wear, ride height, axle position and alignment can all influence trailer tire wear. Record the exact tire position and wear pattern. Replacing the tire removes the evidence but does not correct the condition that created it.

↔️ Dog-tracking deserves a mechanical inspection before adjustment

Before treating a tracking complaint as an alignment number, inspect axle-locating components, bushings, suspension mounts, ride height and structural attachment points. Alignment cannot permanently correct a loose, shifted, bent or damaged component.

🔥 One hot wheel points local before global

If one trailer wheel end is much hotter than the others, compare brake drag, bearing/wheel-end condition and nearby tire concerns at that position. A single hot location is different evidence from an entire axle or trailer showing abnormal heat.

⬆️ Landing gear resistance is a warning—not a request for more force

A hard crank can result from load, misalignment, damaged mounting, cross-shaft/gearbox problems or internal leg trouble. Forcing the handle can increase damage or expose the operator to stored load and crush hazards. Secure and unload the mechanism according to the manufacturer procedure before deeper service.

🔒 Coupling complaints include trailer components too

Repeated fifth-wheel locking or movement trouble can involve the trailer kingpin, upper coupler or approach geometry as well as the tractor fifth wheel. Inspect both mating sides instead of repeatedly repairing only the tractor component.

TruckFixHub diagnostic chain: define AIR/ELECTRICAL/RUNNING GEAR/COUPLING-BODY → prove tractor-to-trailer interface → locate affected position or circuit → inspect mechanical condition → measure/test → repair proven cause → verify the original complaint.
