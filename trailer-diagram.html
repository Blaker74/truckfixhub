👇 Follow the complaint

🛑 BRAKES WRONG: Secure the combination. Verify what the tractor delivers at the gladhand/control connection. If tractor supply and control are correct, follow the trailer circuit through reservoirs, valves, chambers and foundation brakes. Treat an ABS warning as a related but separate diagnostic clue.
💡 LIGHTS DEAD / DIM / STRANGE: Verify the required circuit at the tractor-trailer connector first. Several affected lamps suggest shared feed, ground, connector or harness trouble. One lamp usually sends the search local. Test circuits under load when appropriate.
↔️ DOG-TRACKING / TIRE WEAR: Record exact tire positions and wear. Check inflation, wheels, wheel ends, suspension, bushings, torque rods, air springs and ride height before treating alignment as the whole repair.
🔥 ONE HOT WHEEL: A single hot position points local. Compare brake drag, wheel-end/bearing condition, lubricant and tire condition. Severe heat, smoke or looseness keeps the trailer out of service.
⬆️ LANDING GEAR BINDS: Do not solve resistance with more force. Verify load, leg alignment, mounting, crank/cross-shaft/gearbox and lubrication. Structural or internal damage can worsen when forced.
🔒 COUPLING PROBLEM: Inspect both sides of the connection. Fifth-wheel trouble can involve the tractor fifth wheel, trailer kingpin, upper coupler, mounting or approach geometry.

🧠 The tractor-trailer boundary test

1️⃣ DEFINE THE SYSTEM: Air/brakes, electrical, running gear, or coupling/structure.
2️⃣ TEST THE INTERFACE: For air and electrical complaints, verify what the tractor actually delivers at the trailer connection.
3️⃣ FOLLOW DOWNSTREAM: If the correct supply or signal arrives, move into the trailer until you find where it disappears or changes.
4️⃣ LOCATE THE POSITION: One lamp, one wheel, one axle or the whole trailer produces different evidence.
5️⃣ VERIFY THE REPAIR: Repeat the original brake, lighting, tracking, heat or coupling condition before returning the trailer to service.
