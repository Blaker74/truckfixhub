🚚 TRAILER

Put the trailer problem into one of four boxes before chasing parts.

💨 AIR
Supply and service lines, reservoirs, valves, chambers and brake controls
💡 ELECTRICAL
Tractor/trailer connector, power, wiring, grounds, ABS and lighting circuits
🛞 RUNNING GEAR
Tires, wheels, wheel ends, brakes, suspension and axles
🔒 COUPLING / BODY
Kingpin/upper coupler, landing gear, doors, frame and structure
🛑 Brakes wrong?
First prove tractor side or trailer side.
💡 Lights dead / strange?
Start at the tractor-trailer connection.
↔️ Pulls / dog-tracks?
Inspect running gear before alignment guessing.
⬆️ Landing gear binds?
Secure it. Do not force it.

👇 PICK THE COMPLAINT

🛑 Trailer brakes are not working right

  1. Secure the combination and confirm the complaint safely.
  2. Verify the tractor is supplying the expected air / control to the trailer connection.
  3. If the tractor side is correct, follow the trailer supply and service circuits into reservoirs, valves and chambers.
  4. Inspect foundation brakes and ABS information as applicable.
  5. Repair the proven air, control or mechanical cause and verify normal brake operation before service.

💡 Trailer lights are dead, dim or acting strange

  1. Identify exactly which trailer light functions fail.
  2. Start at the tractor-to-trailer electrical connector and verify the required circuits there.
  3. If several lights fail, look for a shared feed, ground, connector or harness problem.
  4. If only one light fails, continue locally to its wiring, connector, ground and lamp / LED assembly.
  5. Repair the circuit and verify every required trailer light function afterward.

↔️ Trailer pulls, dog-tracks or wears tires

  1. Record which axle / tire positions show the complaint and the wear pattern.
  2. Verify tire inflation and inspect wheels and wheel ends.
  3. Inspect suspension components, bushings, torque rods, air springs and ride height as equipped.
  4. Inspect axle position / alignment and structural mounting points after mechanical looseness or damage is ruled out.
  5. Correct the cause before replacing tires or treating alignment as the only problem.

⬆️ Landing gear is hard to crank or will not move correctly

  1. Secure the trailer and make sure the landing gear is not being dangerously overloaded.
  2. Inspect leg alignment, mounting and obvious structural damage.
  3. Check crank handle, cross shaft, gearbox and lubrication points as applicable.
  4. Do not use excessive force to overcome a bind; internal or structural damage may worsen.
  5. Use the landing-gear manufacturer procedure for disassembly, repair and load limits.

The tractor and trailer are two systems joined together

A trailer complaint can originate on either side of the connection. Air and electrical problems should begin at the tractor-to-trailer interface so you can prove which side owns the fault. Running-gear complaints should be narrowed by location and symptom. Coupling and structural concerns require the correct component manufacturer inspection limits and procedures.

👇 PICK THE TRAILER SYSTEM

💨 Air / Brakes💡 Electrical / Lights🛞 Tires / Running Gear🔒 Fifth Wheel / Coupling
🧠 Simple rule: for AIR and ELECTRICAL problems, start where the TRACTOR and TRAILER connect.
⚠️ Chock and secure the trailer before inspection. Properly support equipment before working underneath it, never rely on air suspension alone, and stay clear of pinch/crush areas.
