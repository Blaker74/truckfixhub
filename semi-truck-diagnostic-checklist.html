Field workflow / six stages

Diagnose before you buy parts.

A universal starting checklist for heavy-duty truck problems. Work from evidence until the failed circuit, component, connection or mechanical condition is proven.

The goal is simple: stop guessing. A fault code, symptom or somebody else’s repair is a starting point—not proof.

01 / Verify the complaint

□ What exactly is the truck doing?
□ When does it happen—cold, hot, loaded, idle, highway, regen?
□ Is the problem constant or intermittent?
□ Can you safely reproduce it?

02 / Collect the evidence

□ Record active and inactive fault codes.
□ Save SPN/FMI or OEM code information.
□ Note pressures, voltages, temperatures and fluid levels.
□ Ask what changed immediately before the problem started.
□ Photograph damaged wiring, leaks or unusual wear when useful.

03 / Check the simple stuff first

□ Battery condition and system voltage.
□ Fuses, grounds, connectors and obvious harness damage.
□ Fluid level, contamination and visible leaks.
□ Loose clamps, broken hoses, disconnected lines or damaged plumbing.
□ Recent repairs or disturbed components near the failure.

04 / Follow the system

□ Identify what the system needs to work: power, ground, signal, pressure, flow, air, fuel or mechanical movement.
□ Test from a known-good point toward the failure.
□ Compare commanded values with actual values when scan data is available.
□ Do not condemn a component just because its name appears in a fault description.

05 / Prove the root cause

□ Does the failed test directly explain the complaint?
□ Can the suspected failure be isolated or confirmed?
□ Could wiring, plumbing, contamination or another upstream problem create the same symptom?
□ Verify exact application before ordering parts.

06 / Verify the repair

□ Clear faults only when appropriate.
□ Reproduce the original operating condition.
□ Confirm the symptom is gone—not merely hidden.
□ Recheck for leaks, faults and abnormal readings.
□ Record what actually fixed it for the next technician.
