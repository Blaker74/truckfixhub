🌪️ ENGINE FAN STAYS ON?

First determine whether the truck is commanding the fan on or the fan clutch/control hardware is staying engaged.

Quick answer

If a semi truck engine fan runs constantly, do not replace the fan clutch first. Check actual coolant temperature and other fan-request inputs, then compare commanded fan status with what the fan physically does. The engine or vehicle controller may intentionally request the fan because of coolant temperature, A/C pressure/demand, intake/charge-air temperature, transmission conditions, regeneration or another protection strategy. If command is OFF but the fan remains engaged, move toward the fan clutch, solenoid, air/electrical control and related hardware.

Start here — in this order

1️⃣ Verify whether the fan is truly engaged

Distinguish normal freewheeling/drag from a fully engaged fan. Use the fan manufacturer's and chassis manufacturer's procedures; never reach into a fan area to test it by hand with the engine capable of starting.

2️⃣ Read actual temperatures and pressures

Use diagnostic data to compare coolant temperature and applicable A/C or other fan-request inputs with expected conditions. A sensor reading that is implausible can create an unnecessary fan request.

3️⃣ Check commanded fan status

Determine whether the controller is commanding the fan ON or OFF. This is the key split between a legitimate/control-side request and a clutch/solenoid/hardware problem.

4️⃣ If commanded ON, find the reason

Review fan-request parameters, active fault codes and operating conditions. Do not bypass the fan simply because the engine temperature looks normal on the dash.

5️⃣ If commanded OFF but physically ON, inspect the control path

Follow the correct schematic for the fan-clutch type. Check the solenoid, wiring, air supply/control and clutch mechanism according to manufacturer specifications.

6️⃣ Verify operation through a complete cycle

After repair, confirm the fan can engage when commanded and release when the request is removed under safe operating conditions.

What the pattern can tell you

❄️ Fan runs whenever A/C is on

Check A/C pressure/demand and condenser airflow strategy before blaming the clutch. Some fan operation may be commanded as part of normal A/C control.

🌡️ Fan stays on with a cold engine

Compare diagnostic sensor values with actual conditions and inspect for fault/default strategies or another fan request.

⚠️ Fan came on with a fault code

Read the code and live data first. Some control systems use fan-on operation as a protective response when a critical input is missing or implausible.

🔌 Command says OFF but fan remains locked

Move toward the fan clutch, solenoid and its pneumatic/electrical control path. Prove the control signal and supply before replacing the clutch.

🔥 Fan stays on because engine is actually hot

The fan may be doing its job. Diagnose the overheating/cooling-system problem rather than disabling the fan.

🔧 Problem started after cooling or A/C repair

Recheck disturbed sensors, connectors, wiring, pressure inputs, air lines and control components first.

Information to collect before buying parts

  • Truck year, make, model and engine
  • Fan clutch manufacturer/type when known
  • Actual coolant temperature
  • A/C on versus off behavior
  • Fan commanded ON/OFF status
  • Active and inactive fault codes
  • Relevant sensor readings and plausibility
  • Solenoid/control signal and air supply where applicable
  • Recent cooling, fan or A/C repairs
🧠 Parts-cannon rule: fan running constantly does not automatically equal a bad fan clutch. First prove whether the controller is requesting the fan.
⚠️ Engine fans can engage automatically without warning. Keep hands, tools, clothing and test leads clear of the fan and belts whenever the engine can start or run.
