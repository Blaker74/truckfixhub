SPN 3364 — DEF quality fault

A DEF quality-related fault is a starting point for diagnosis. It does not automatically mean the DEF is bad, the tank must be drained or a DEF component needs replacement.

Quick answer: what should you check first?

  1. Save the complete SPN/FMI and all companion faults before clearing codes.
  2. Verify what is actually in the DEF tank. Check level and look for obvious contamination or incorrect fluid using approved procedures.
  3. Inspect accessible tank/header wiring and connectors for damage, moisture, corrosion or poor connections.
  4. Look for related DEF pressure, dosing, temperature, NOx and SCR-efficiency faults.
  5. Confirm the exact OEM definition and diagnostic tree for the engine, model year and calibration.

Does SPN 3364 automatically mean bad DEF?

No. A DEF-quality-related code or dash message must be interpreted with its FMI, companion codes and system data. Depending on the application, the diagnostic path can involve fluid condition, sensing, wiring, dosing and SCR-system performance. Test the condition instead of assuming the fluid is the root cause.

Should you drain the DEF tank immediately?

Not simply because SPN 3364 is present. If contamination or incorrect fluid is confirmed, follow the manufacturer's service procedure. If fluid condition has not been established, draining the tank can add expense without identifying an electrical, sensing, dosing or SCR problem that may still remain.

Why the FMI and related codes matter

The SPN identifies a monitored parameter while the FMI helps describe the reported failure condition. Aftertreatment controllers also evaluate multiple inputs together. A DEF-quality complaint may appear with dosing, NOx-sensor, temperature, pressure or SCR-efficiency faults. Treat the complete fault set as evidence.

Useful inspection and diagnostic areas

  • DEF level, fluid condition and possible contamination
  • DEF tank/header sensor connections and harness condition
  • DEF supply, pressure and dosing system faults
  • Temperature-related faults where applicable
  • Inlet and outlet NOx sensor faults
  • SCR efficiency and aftertreatment performance faults
  • Recent DEF-system or electrical repairs

Can SPN 3364 lead to a derate?

Unresolved aftertreatment faults can contribute to warning and inducement strategies depending on the engine and calibration. If a derate is already present, record the warning sequence and entire code set rather than assuming SPN 3364 alone identifies the failed component.

Important

SPN/FMI definitions, expected values and diagnostic procedures can vary by manufacturer, model year and calibration. Follow the engine manufacturer's service information. Never defeat emissions or safety systems.

Save the evidence and the repair

Keep the FMI, companion faults, DEF observations, test results and final repair together. That turns one repair into useful history for the next complaint.
