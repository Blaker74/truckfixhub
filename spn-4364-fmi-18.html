SPN 4364 FMI 18

What should you check first?

  1. Save the entire fault set before clearing anything, including active and inactive aftertreatment faults.
  2. Confirm DEF condition and related DEF faults rather than assuming fluid quality is good or bad.
  3. Inspect for accessible exhaust leaks and recent exhaust-system work that could affect readings.
  4. Review inlet and outlet NOx information with compatible diagnostic software under the manufacturer's test conditions.
  5. Evaluate dosing and SCR performance using the exact OEM diagnostic procedure for the engine and calibration.

What does SPN 4364 FMI 18 mean?

This fault generally indicates the engine or aftertreatment controller has determined that SCR NOx conversion performance is below the expected range under the conditions used for system evaluation. It identifies a performance problem; it does not by itself prove which component caused that performance problem.

The complete diagnosis depends on the engine family, aftertreatment configuration, software calibration, related faults and the conditions under which the monitor ran.

What is the SCR system trying to do?

The selective catalytic reduction system uses DEF as part of reducing NOx in the exhaust stream. The control system evaluates multiple inputs and operating conditions to determine whether conversion performance is acceptable. That means the diagnostic picture can involve DEF delivery, sensing, exhaust integrity, catalyst performance and other aftertreatment conditions.

Areas commonly involved in diagnosis

  • DEF quality, contamination or incorrect fluid
  • DEF supply, pressure, dosing quantity and doser operation
  • Upstream and downstream NOx sensor information
  • NOx sensor wiring, connectors or communication faults
  • Exhaust leaks that can affect system evaluation
  • SCR catalyst performance
  • Related active aftertreatment faults
  • Temperature and operating conditions required for SCR evaluation
  • Recent aftertreatment, sensor, exhaust or wiring repairs

Before replacing a NOx sensor

A NOx-related companion fault can be important evidence, but a low-conversion fault is not an instruction to replace a NOx sensor. Save the FMI for each related code, inspect wiring and connectors where appropriate, and use OEM tests to determine whether sensor information is plausible.

Before condemning the SCR catalyst

Confirm that the rest of the system is capable of giving the catalyst a fair test. DEF condition, dosing, NOx information, exhaust leaks, temperature conditions and unresolved companion faults can all affect the diagnostic path. Catalyst replacement can be expensive, so evidence should support the decision.

Derate and emissions warning

Emissions faults can progress to inducement or derate conditions depending on the application. Dashboard warnings and operating restrictions should not be ignored. Follow the vehicle and engine manufacturer's instructions. Do not defeat emissions controls or repeatedly clear faults to conceal an unresolved condition.

Save enough evidence to prove the repair

  • SPN 4364 FMI 18 active/inactive status
  • All companion faults
  • DEF observations and recent refill/service history
  • NOx sensor and exhaust-system observations
  • Tests performed under OEM procedures
  • Derate or warning status before and after repair
  • The final verified cause and repair

That record is more useful than simply writing “SCR code.”
