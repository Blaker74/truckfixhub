👇 Diagnose in the right order

🔇 NO CRANK: Verify battery capability, connections, start permission/command, and the positive and ground paths under load before condemning the starter.
🐌 SLOW CRANK: Check battery capability and voltage drop first. If electrical delivery is proven, evaluate starter performance and abnormal mechanical load as applicable.
🔑 NORMAL CRANK / NO START: The starter is already rotating the engine. Move toward engine-control, engine-speed/synchronization, fuel, electrical and OEM no-start diagnosis.
⚡ NOT CHARGING: Verify actual charging-system performance, drive condition, connections and applicable control circuits before replacing an alternator.
🪫 DEAD AGAIN: Separate a weak/undercharged battery, charging problem and unwanted key-off current draw. Replacing batteries without finding why they discharged can bring the complaint right back.

🧠 Why load testing matters

A circuit can show voltage when almost no current is flowing and still fail when the starter demands high current. That is why loaded testing and manufacturer-approved voltage-drop procedures are valuable for starting circuits.
Bright dash lights do not prove a battery bank can supply starter current. Likewise, a click does not prove the high-current path or starter can carry the required load.
Intermittent failures are best tested while the complaint is present. Capture voltage, command status and operating behavior before disturbing connections whenever practical.
