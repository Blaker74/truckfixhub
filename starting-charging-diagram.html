🔋 STARTING & CHARGING

Start with the batteries. Follow the power.

1️⃣ BATTERIES
Store the electrical energy used to crank the engine
↓
2️⃣ CABLES + CONNECTIONS
Carry high current through positive and ground paths
↓
3️⃣ START CONTROL
Key/start request, switches, relays and control logic command cranking
↓
4️⃣ STARTER
Turns electrical energy into cranking torque
↓
5️⃣ ALTERNATOR
Supplies electrical power while running and restores charge to the batteries
🔇 No crank?
Separate a control problem from a high-current problem. Verify battery condition, start command, relay/control operation and power/ground at the starter.
🐌 Cranks slowly?
Load-test the battery system as required and check voltage drop through both positive and ground cables before condemning the starter.
🪫 Batteries keep dying?
Verify charging performance, belt/drive condition and connections. If charging is correct, investigate unwanted key-off current draw using the proper procedure.
⚡ Charging warning?
Verify actual system voltage and charging performance at the batteries. A warning light or message alone does not prove the alternator failed.

No crank, slow crank, or dead batteries?

These complaints can look similar but come from different parts of the system. A no-crank can be a control or high-current problem. Slow cranking often points toward battery capacity, resistance in cables/connections, mechanical load, or starter performance. Repeated dead batteries require separating a charging problem from a key-off electrical draw.

🧠 Simple rule: test the system while it is doing the work. A cable can look clean and show voltage with no load yet still drop too much voltage during cranking.
