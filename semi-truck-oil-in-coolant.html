🛢️ OIL IN COOLANT

Oil contamination in the cooling system needs source testing—not assumptions.

1️⃣ CONFIRM THE FLUID: Rule out old residue, incorrect additives or previous contamination. Record engine-oil and coolant level trends.
2️⃣ PRESSURE DIFFERENCE MATTERS: Engine oil pressure can exceed cooling-system pressure during operation, making certain oil-to-coolant interfaces important suspects—but still not proof.
3️⃣ TEST OIL COOLER / HOUSING: If the engine has an oil-to-coolant cooler or housing, follow the OEM isolation/pressure test before condemning the cylinder head or gasket.
4️⃣ INTERNAL PATHS: If external/interface components test good, use the engine-specific procedure for head, gasket, casting or other internal oil-gallery-to-coolant passages.
5️⃣ CLEANUP: Oil can remain throughout hoses, reservoir and cooling passages. Follow the manufacturer-approved cleaning/flushing procedure after the source is repaired.
CONFIRM → LEVEL HISTORY → TEST OIL/COOLANT INTERFACES → INTERNAL PATH IF REQUIRED → PROVE → REPAIR → CLEAN SYSTEM → VERIFY.
⚠️ Never open a hot pressurized cooling system. Use the correct engine and coolant manufacturer cleaning procedure; contamination can damage hoses and cooling-system components.
