🧰 Five Electrical Drawers To Understand

1 · VOLTAGE = PRESSUREA voltage reading shows electrical potential. It does not by itself prove the connection can carry the load.
2 · CURRENT = WORK MOVINGHigh-current loads such as starters expose weak cables and connections that can look fine with no load.
3 · RESISTANCE = SOMETHING FIGHTING FLOWCorrosion, loose connections, damaged wire and poor contacts can restrict current and create heat.
4 · GROUND = THE WAY BACKA component can have good power and still fail because the return path is poor.
5 · VOLTAGE DROP = FIND THE BOTTLENECKTest across a connection or cable while current is flowing to expose unwanted resistance.

🔧 When Something Is Dead

SEE ITWhat exactly does not work—one component, several components or the whole truck?
NAME ITDoes it look like supply power, command, load, ground or network communication?
TEST ITCheck the circuit while it is failing. Do not stop at “I have 12 volts” when the circuit must carry a load.
PROVE ITAfter repair, operate the circuit and confirm normal voltage, load operation and communication.

🧰 Common Failure Drawers

OPEN CIRCUITThe intended electrical path is broken.
SHORT TO GROUNDPower finds an unintended path to ground.
SHORT TO POWERA circuit receives voltage from an unintended source.
HIGH RESISTANCEA poor connection restricts current and creates voltage drop.
BAD GROUNDThe return side cannot carry the load correctly.
COMMUNICATION FAULTModules cannot exchange valid network information.

⚠️ SAFETY DRAWER

Do not bypass circuit protection, probe connectors carelessly, short terminals together or assume every circuit is a simple 12-volt circuit. Confirm the exact truck, circuit and manufacturer procedure before pin testing, load testing, programming or repair.
