Cooling fan not coming on?

First question: Is the truck ASKING for the fan?

🛑 KEEP CLEAR OF THE FAN.

A heavy-duty engine fan can engage unexpectedly. Never put hands, clothing or tools near the fan, belts or pulleys while the engine is running or capable of starting. Follow manufacturer lockout and service procedures.

🧒 Fan diagnosis made simple

🌡️ ENGINE GETS HOT
🧠 FAN COMMAND?
🌬️ FAN RESPONDS?
🧊 AIR MOVES?

Do not start by buying a fan clutch. Separate the command from the physical fan response.

🧠 The most important split

✅ COMMAND = ON

The controller is requesting fan operation, but the fan does not physically respond.

Look toward: fan drive/clutch, electrical power or ground, wiring/connectors, air supply or control components on applicable systems, and application-specific hardware.

❌ COMMAND = OFF

The fan is not being requested even though you believe conditions should call for it.

Look toward: sensor/input data, control strategy, fault information, wiring, calibration and whether the engine is actually meeting the OEM conditions for fan engagement.

Different question = different diagnostic path. Engagement strategy varies by truck, engine and fan-drive design. There is no safe universal engagement temperature to assume.

🅿️ Gets hot mainly at idle or low speed

Low road speed means less natural air moves through the cooling package. Fan operation and airflow through the charge-air cooler/radiator stack become especially useful clues. Also inspect for airflow restriction rather than assuming the fan itself is bad.

⛰️ Gets hot pulling hard

Record coolant temperature behavior, load, grade, ambient conditions, road speed and whether the fan is commanded and physically engages. Heavy-load overheating can involve more than the fan: coolant level, radiator airflow, thermostat behavior, coolant circulation and engine-specific problems can all matter.

🌡️ Open overheating path →

🚫 Fan never seems to engage

“I never hear it” is a clue—not proof of a failed fan clutch. When the application provides diagnostic data, compare commanded fan state with what the fan actually does. Record relevant SPN/FMI or manufacturer fault information before clearing it.

🔧 Problem started after service

Start with what changed. Inspect connections, wiring, air lines or plumbing that were disconnected, moved or repaired. A symptom appearing after a repair does not automatically mean the new component is defective.

🌬️ Fan spins—but is air getting through?

A working fan cannot cool efficiently if the cooling package cannot pass enough air. Dirt, debris or restriction through the charge-air cooler/radiator stack can matter. Inspect safely using the correct service procedure.
