TRUCKFIXHUB COOLING SYSTEM GUIDE

Semi Truck Cooling Fan Not Working?

If a heavy-duty truck is running hot and the cooling fan does not appear to engage, the fan itself is only one part of the diagnostic path. Fan design and control strategy vary by truck and engine.

Keep clear of the fan

A heavy-duty cooling fan can engage unexpectedly. Never place hands, clothing or tools near the fan, belts or other rotating components with the engine running or capable of starting. Follow the manufacturer's service and lockout procedures.

First: describe what the fan is doing

  • Never seems to engage
  • Engages only sometimes
  • Truck overheats at idle or low road speed
  • Truck overheats mainly under heavy load or on grades
  • Fan stays engaged more than expected
  • Problem began after cooling-system, electrical or air-system service
  • Check-engine or STOP ENGINE warning appears with the temperature rise

If the problem is the opposite and the fan runs constantly, use the dedicated engine fan stays on troubleshooting guide.

Common areas technicians investigate

The exact system varies by application, but diagnosis may include:

  • Engine coolant temperature and other commanded-fan inputs
  • Active fault codes and engine-controller data
  • Fan clutch or fan-drive condition
  • Electrical power, grounds, relays, wiring or connectors where applicable
  • Air supply, valves, lines or control components on air-operated systems where applicable
  • Temperature, pressure or other sensor data used by the control strategy
  • Recent repairs or disturbed connections
  • Radiator, charge-air cooler and airflow restrictions that can contribute to overheating

Fan not coming on plus overheating

Do not assume the fan is the only cause. Low coolant, external or internal coolant loss, restricted heat exchangers, thermostat or coolant-flow concerns, water-pump issues and other engine-specific conditions may also contribute to high temperature.

Fan complaint plus STOP ENGINE warning

If high temperature is accompanied by a critical warning or manufacturer-directed shutdown condition, respond to the warning first. Do not bypass an engine-protection strategy simply to keep operating.

STOP ENGINE warning guide →

Check fault codes before replacing the fan clutch

Record the SPN/FMI or manufacturer fault information and relevant engine data before clearing faults. A fan-related symptom can involve control inputs, wiring, air supply or other components rather than the fan clutch itself.

Check engine / fault-code guide →

Problem started after service

If the complaint began after radiator, charge-air cooler, thermostat, water pump, fan clutch, belt, electrical, air-system or engine work, tell the technician exactly what was changed. Inspecting disturbed connections and components is a useful starting point before unrelated parts are condemned.

Do not force or bypass fan operation

TruckFixHub provides educational troubleshooting information. Fan-control systems differ across makes, engines and applications. Do not jumper circuits, bypass controls or work around a rotating fan without the correct OEM procedure and qualified heavy-duty service practices.
