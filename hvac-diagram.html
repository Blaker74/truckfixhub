👇 Follow the complaint

❄️ A/C NOT COLD: Confirm airflow first. Then verify whether A/C is being commanded, condenser airflow is available, compressor/control operation is correct, and refrigerant pressures/temperatures support the suspected failure before replacing a compressor or adding refrigerant.
💨 WEAK AIRFLOW: Compare blower speeds. Inspect the cab filter if equipped, evaporator for restriction/icing, ducts and mode doors. A loud blower with weak vent flow points differently than a blower that is actually turning slowly.
🔌 BLOWER DEAD / SOME SPEEDS ONLY: Follow command → protection/control → loaded power → blower → return. Depending on the truck, speed control may use resistors, electronic modules or networked HVAC control.
🔥 NO HEAT: Confirm the engine reaches proper temperature and coolant level is safe/correct. Compare heater-hose temperatures and check coolant flow/control. If the heater core is hot but vent air stays cold, move toward blend-door/control diagnosis.
🧊 COLD THEN WARM: Record whether vent airflow also fades. Evaporator icing, compressor/control cycling, refrigerant conditions, sensor inputs and condenser airflow can produce different versions of the same complaint.
🌡️ DIFFERENT TEMPERATURE LEFT/RIGHT: On multi-zone systems, compare commanded and actual blend-door/actuator operation before assuming the refrigerant circuit has two different temperatures.

🧠 Four questions before replacing HVAC parts

1️⃣ IS AIR MOVING? Separate airflow from temperature before touching the refrigerant system.
2️⃣ IS THE SYSTEM BEING COMMANDED? A compressor, valve or actuator cannot respond correctly if the control side is withholding the command for a valid reason.
3️⃣ WHAT DO PRESSURE AND TEMPERATURE SHOW? Refrigerant diagnosis requires proper equipment and interpretation of system conditions—not pressure alone and not blind refrigerant charging.
4️⃣ DOES THE COMPLAINT CHANGE WITH ENGINE SPEED, ROAD SPEED OR TIME? Those clues can separate condenser airflow, icing, control, blower and refrigerant-side problems.
