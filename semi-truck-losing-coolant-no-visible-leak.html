🛑 DO NOT JUST KEEP TOPPING IT OFF.

Repeated coolant loss can lead to overheating and engine damage. Stop if coolant loss becomes rapid, steam appears, the engine overheats, or the truck's warning/shutdown system requires it. Never open a hot pressurized cooling system.

🧪 TEST INSIDE

Start outside the engine before jumping to expensive internal failures. Tiny leaks can happen only when hot, coolant can evaporate on hot parts, and coolant can be expelled while driving without leaving a puddle where the truck parks.

1️⃣ First prove it is really losing coolant

Check level only by the manufacturer's safe procedure and compare it under similar conditions. Record how much coolant is added and over how many miles, hours or days. A repeatable loss rate is much more useful than “I add some sometimes.”

2️⃣ Search for the leak that hides

Look for dried residue, staining, coolant odor and wetness around hoses, fittings, radiator connections, surge tank, cap area, heater circuit and engine plumbing. Airflow can move coolant far from its source, and coolant touching hot components may evaporate.

🔥 Only loses coolant when hot

Temperature and pressure can expose a leak that does not show while parked cold. Do not open or inspect a dangerous hot system. A qualified technician can reproduce the condition and use the exact OEM-approved pressure and inspection procedures.

⛰️ Mostly loses coolant under load

Record whether it happens pulling grades, hauling heavy, during sustained highway work, or around regeneration events. Higher heat and pressure can reveal marginal external leaks or coolant being expelled. The operating pattern narrows the test—it does not name the failed part.

💨 Exhaust or vapor clue

Unusual exhaust vapor, coolant odor or other exhaust-side clues can justify engine-specific testing. On some applications, EGR-system components are part of the diagnostic path. But vapor alone does not prove an EGR cooler, head gasket or cylinder head has failed.

🛢️ Another fluid changed?

If engine oil or another fluid suddenly looks abnormal while coolant is disappearing, stop treating this as a simple external leak. Cross-contamination and internal-loss possibilities require qualified diagnosis and engine-specific procedures. Do not condemn a component from appearance alone.

🌡️ Losing coolant AND overheating?

Treat both symptoms together. Coolant loss can cause overheating, while excessive temperature or pressure can expose another cooling-system problem. Topping it off may temporarily hide the symptom without fixing the cause.
