💨 AIR & BRAKES

Follow the air from where it is made to where it does the work.

1️⃣ COMPRESSOR
Makes compressed air
↓
2️⃣ GOVERNOR / AIR DRYER
Governor controls compressor loading; dryer removes moisture and contamination
↓
3️⃣ AIR RESERVOIRS
Store compressed air for the brake system
↓
4️⃣ VALVES & LINES
Control and route air through the system
↓
5️⃣ BRAKE CHAMBERS
Convert air pressure into mechanical brake force
🪫 Builds air slowly?
Check for excessive leakage and follow the supply side: compressor, governor, dryer and related plumbing.
💨 Loses air?
With the vehicle safely secured, locate leakage at lines, fittings, valves, chambers and other air components using approved procedures.
🛑 One brake acts wrong?
Compare that wheel end and its air/mechanical components with the others.
⚠️ Whole system acts wrong?
Think about shared supply pressure, controls and common valves before focusing on one wheel.

Start by deciding: supply problem or brake problem?

If the truck cannot build or hold air correctly, begin with the air-supply side. If system pressure is normal but braking is wrong at one axle or wheel, move downstream toward the valves, chambers and mechanical brake components. Use the vehicle manufacturer’s procedures and required pressure, leakage and brake-adjustment checks for the exact vehicle.

⚠️ Air brakes are safety-critical. Secure and properly support the vehicle, follow approved procedures, and never rely on air pressure or air suspension alone to hold a vehicle.
