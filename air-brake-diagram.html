👇 First split: supply or local brake?

🐌 BUILDS SLOW: Listen for significant leakage first. Then follow the supply path through compressor, governor/dryer, plumbing and reservoirs using the correct pressure-build procedure.
🪫 WON’T BUILD: A compressor is only one possible cause. Separate major leakage, compressor loading/control, dryer/supply problems, damaged plumbing and compressor output before replacing components.
📉 PRESSURE DROPS FAST: Determine whether loss occurs with brakes released, applied or both. The operating condition helps identify which pneumatic circuit is active when the leak appears.
🔊 DRYER PURGES REPEATEDLY: Determine whether the compressor/governor system is cycling because pressure is actually falling, whether control plumbing is involved, or whether the dryer itself has a confirmed fault.
🛑 ONE WHEEL ACTS WRONG: If system pressure behaves normally but one wheel drags, overheats or brakes differently, move downstream toward that wheel’s air delivery, chamber and foundation brake—and consider the wheel end.

🧠 Use the operating condition as evidence

Leak with brakes released: investigate circuits pressurized in the released condition.
Leak appears during service application: focus on components and circuits that receive air when the brake pedal is applied.
Truck and trailer behave differently: separate tractor supply/control from trailer circuits instead of treating the combination as one undivided system.
ABS warning: record the complete electronic fault. An ABS warning and a pneumatic leak require different evidence even though both belong to the braking system.
