TRUCKFIXHUB COOLING SYSTEM GUIDE

Semi Truck Coolant Leak?

Where and when coolant appears can narrow the search before parts are replaced.

Hot coolant can cause severe burns

Do not remove a pressurized cooling-system cap while hot. If the engine is overheating or coolant loss is severe, stop and follow the manufacturer's shutdown and service procedures.

Useful clues

  • Leak only cold, only hot, or after shutdown
  • Front of engine versus radiator, surge tank, cab or underbody
  • Visible wetness, dried coolant residue or coolant odor
  • Leak begins under load or when system pressure rises
  • Recent hose, radiator, water-pump or cooling-system work

Areas commonly inspected

Depending on the truck and engine, technicians may inspect hoses and clamps, radiator seams and connections, surge tank and cap area, water-pump region, thermostat housing, heater circuits, coolant pipes and other fittings. Pressure testing must follow the manufacturer's specifications.

No puddle?

If coolant level drops without an obvious external leak, use the separate losing-coolant guide. Internal leakage, evaporation from a small hot leak or leakage that occurs only while driving may require a different diagnostic path.
