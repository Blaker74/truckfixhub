Coolant can run downward and airflow can blow it backward. The wettest spot is not always the source. Start where you see residue, then trace the path toward the highest or earliest wet point you can safely inspect.

❄️ Leaks when cold

Look for wet hose connections, clamps, seams, fittings and dried residue before warm-up. Some connections can seep cold and change as components expand. The cold pattern is a clue—not proof of a failed part.

🔥 Leaks only when hot

Heat and system pressure can expose a leak that is invisible cold. Do not open the system hot. A qualified technician can use the exact manufacturer-approved inspection or pressure-test procedure after the system is safe to service.

🅿️ Appears after shutdown

Heat remains after the engine stops and temperatures can redistribute through the cooling system. Record exactly where coolant first appears and how long after shutdown it begins. That timing can help reproduce the leak safely.

⚙️ Front of engine

Inspect the water-pump region, nearby hoses, fittings and connections applicable to the exact engine. Coolant above a pump can run onto it, so visible coolant on the pump does not automatically prove the pump is leaking.

🧊 Radiator area

Trace wetness or dried residue around radiator seams, hose connections, clamps and surrounding plumbing. Airflow can carry coolant away from the original leak while driving.

🔧 Radiator / hose guide →

🫙 Surge tank area

Look for tank cracks, connection leaks and evidence that coolant is escaping around the cap area. Coolant near the cap may be a leak, or it may have been expelled because of another cooling-system condition. Prove which happened.

🫙 Surge tank / cap guide →

🚛 Cab / heater area

Follow the heater circuit applicable to the truck: hoses, valves, fittings and heater components. Coolant odor, dampness or residue can help locate the path, but use the exact vehicle service information before disassembly.

No visible leak?

If the coolant level keeps dropping but the truck is dry when inspected, stop forcing the visible-leak path. Small hot leaks, coolant expelled while driving and internal-loss possibilities need a different diagnostic branch.

A puddle tells you where coolant ended up—not necessarily where it started. Follow the evidence before replacing parts.

🛑 SAFETY: Never remove a hot pressure cap. Keep clear of belts and fans; electronically controlled fans can engage unexpectedly. Use the exact OEM test pressure, procedures and specifications for the truck and engine.
